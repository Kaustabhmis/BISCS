How does fancy text actually work?

Instagram, WhatsApp and most apps do not let you choose fonts. Fancy text gets around that by swapping each letter for a different Unicode character that looks like a styled version.

Unicode, the standard that defines every character your phone can display, includes several complete alphabets originally intended for mathematical notation: bold, italic, script, fraktur, double-struck, monospace and sans-serif letters. There are also circled letters, squared letters, small capitals and full-width characters from East Asian typography.

So 𝐀 in bold is not the letter A with bold formatting. It is a separate character called "Mathematical Bold Capital A". That is why it survives copy and paste, and also why it causes the problems described later.

The accessibility catch: screen readers

This is the part most fancy text sites leave out. Screen readers, the software that reads the screen aloud for blind and low-vision users, do not see "bold text". They see unusual mathematical characters. Depending on the app and device, they might:

  • read each letter by its full name, such as "mathematical bold capital B, mathematical bold capital I…";
  • spell the word out one letter at a time;
  • skip the characters entirely, leaving silence.

A bio that says "𝐅𝐫𝐞𝐬𝐡 𝐇𝐨𝐦𝐞𝐦𝐚𝐝𝐞 𝐒𝐰𝐞𝐞𝐭𝐬 · 𝐎𝐫𝐝𝐞𝐫 𝐨𝐧 𝐖𝐡𝐚𝐭𝐬𝐀𝐩𝐩" may be unreadable for a visually impaired customer. For a business, that is a lost order; for anyone, it shuts people out.

A good rule: use fancy letters for a small decorative touch, and keep your name, contact details, prices and key information in normal text.

Other limits to know

  • Search. Platforms may not match fancy letters to normal ones, so a profile name written entirely in script may not appear when people search for it.
  • Hashtags and mentions. A hashtag in fancy letters will not become a clickable tag. Keep hashtags and @usernames plain.
  • Only A–Z and 0–9. The styled alphabets exist only for English letters and digits. Hindi, Bengali and other scripts stay unchanged, though effects like strikethrough, underline and spaced out work with any text.
  • Character limits. Many fancy characters count as two characters internally, so a styled bio can hit Instagram's limit sooner than you expect.
  • Professional settings. Avoid fancy text in CVs, LinkedIn headlines, email subject lines and official forms. It can look unprofessional, and automated systems may not read it.

Tips for using fancy text well

  • Style one line, not everything. A bold name or headline stands out more when the rest is plain.
  • Preview on another phone. Check how it looks on an older Android device if your audience uses them.
  • Mix with emoji sparingly. Too much decoration makes text hard to scan.
  • Use upside-down text for fun only. It is a good joke, but nobody can read it quickly. The Reverse Text tool has more flipping options.
  • Prefer real formatting where it exists. WhatsApp supports genuine bold with asterisks around words, which screen readers handle far better than fancy characters.

Example: an accessible Instagram bio

Imagine a home bakery in Lucknow writing its bio. A version written entirely in script looks pretty but is hard to read, may not show up in search, and is silent or garbled for screen reader users.

A better approach uses fancy text for just one element:

  1. First line: the brand name in a single fancy style, such as bold sans, for visual impact.
  2. Second line, in plain text: "Eggless cakes and cookies, Gomti Nagar".
  3. Third line, in plain text: "Order on WhatsApp: 98XXX XXXXX".
  4. Hashtag in plain text, such as #LucknowBakery, so it stays clickable.

Better still, keep the brand name plain in the profile's name field, which is the field search relies on most, and use the styled version only in the bio itself. Everyone can find the shop, and every customer can read how to order.

Frequently asked questions

Why do fancy fonts work in Instagram when it has no font options?

They are not really fonts. Each styled letter is a separate Unicode character, originally created for maths and other symbols, that happens to look like a bold, italic or script letter. Because they are characters rather than formatting, they paste into any app that supports Unicode text.

Can screen readers read fancy text?

Often not well. Screen readers used by blind and low-vision people may read each letter as its full technical name, such as mathematical bold capital A, spell it letter by letter, or skip it entirely. Keep your name, key information and calls to action in normal text, and use fancy letters only for decoration.

Why do some fancy letters show as boxes?

The device or app viewing the text does not have a font that includes those particular Unicode characters. Older phones and some desktop apps are most affected. Bold, italic and small caps styles are widely supported; rarer styles such as squared or fraktur letters are more likely to appear as empty boxes.

Do fancy letters work in hashtags and usernames?

Generally no. Hashtags and usernames on most platforms accept only standard letters, numbers and underscores, so a hashtag written in fancy letters will not link or be searchable. Keep hashtags and @mentions in plain text even if the rest of your caption is styled.

Does the generator support Hindi or other Indian scripts?

The styled alphabets exist only for the English letters A to Z and digits 0 to 9, so Hindi, Bengali and other scripts pass through unchanged. Effects such as strikethrough, underline and spaced out add marks or spaces around any character, so those also work with Indian scripts.
