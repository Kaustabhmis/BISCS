Simple Interest Calculator: SI = P × R × T ÷ 100 Explained

Calculate simple interest for any principal, rate and period in years, months or days, compare it with compound interest and spot costly flat-rate loan quotes.

Simple interest is interest calculated only on the original amount, called the principal. It does not earn interest on itself. The formula is SI = P × R × T ÷ 100, where P is the principal in rupees, R is the annual interest rate and T is the time in years. The total amount at the end is P + SI.

For example, ₹1,00,000 at 8% a year for 5 years earns 1,00,000 × 8 × 5 ÷ 100 = ₹40,000 in simple interest, so the total is ₹1,40,000. The free Simple Interest calculator does this instantly and shows how much more compound interest would have produced.

Simple interest formulas you can rearrange

Because SI = P × R × T ÷ 100 links four values, you can find any one if you know the other three:

  • Principal: P = SI × 100 ÷ (R × T)
  • Rate: R = SI × 100 ÷ (P × T)
  • Time: T = SI × 100 ÷ (P × R)

So if a friend lends you ₹30,000 and wants ₹3,600 back as interest after 2 years, the rate is 3,600 × 100 ÷ (30,000 × 2) = 6% a year.

2 ₹16,000 ₹1,16,000 ₹1,16,640

4 ₹32,000 ₹1,32,000 ₹1,36,049
5 ₹40,000 ₹1,40,000 ₹1,46,933

In year one they are identical. After that, compound interest pulls ahead because it earns interest on interest. Over long periods the gap becomes large, which is why savings products prefer compounding and why the Compound Interest calculator is better for long-term planning.

Short-period examples

  • Months: ₹50,000 at 12% for 9 months → 50,000 × 12 × 0.75 ÷ 100 = ₹4,500
  • Days: ₹20,000 at 10% for 146 days → 20,000 × 10 × (146 ÷ 365) ÷ 100 = ₹800

These are typical of short informal loans, interest on delayed invoice payments, or interest a seller may charge for paying late.

Watch out for flat-rate loans

Some personal, consumer and vehicle loans are quoted at a "flat" rate. That means simple interest on the full amount for the full term, even though you repay part of the principal each month.

Example: ₹1,00,000 at 10% flat for 3 years.

  • Interest = 1,00,000 × 10 × 3 ÷ 100 = ₹30,000
  • Monthly instalment = ₹1,30,000 ÷ 36 = about ₹3,611

A normal reducing-balance loan at 10% for 3 years has an EMI of about ₹3,227 and total interest of about ₹16,162. To match the flat loan's ₹3,611 instalment, a reducing-balance loan would need a rate of roughly 18%. You can test this yourself in the EMI Calculator.

Common mistakes

  1. Forgetting to convert months or days into years. T must be in years when R is a yearly rate.
  2. Using a monthly rate as an annual one. "1.5% a month" is 18% a year in simple terms.
  3. Comparing a flat rate with a reducing rate directly. They are not the same thing.
  4. Applying simple interest to products that compound. Bank FDs, PPF and most savings products compound; the simple formula will understate them.
  5. Rounding too early. Keep full figures until the final step to avoid small errors.

Practical tips

  • When lending to family or friends, write down the principal, rate and period and calculate the interest together, so there is no confusion later.
  • For a business, interest on late payments should follow what your invoice terms say. The Invoice Generator lets you add payment terms to your bills.
  • If a loan quote seems cheap, ask whether the rate is flat or reducing before signing.

A note on reliability

The calculator applies the textbook formula correctly, with days divided by 365. Real agreements may specify a 360-day year, different rounding or penalty interest. Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate simple interest for months?

Convert months to years by dividing by 12, then use the usual formula. For ₹50,000 at 12% for 9 months, time is 0.75 years, so SI = 50,000 × 12 × 0.75 ÷ 100 = ₹4,500. In the calculator, enter 9 and choose Months as the unit.

How do I calculate simple interest for days?

Divide the number of days by 365 to get time in years. ₹20,000 at 10% for 146 days gives SI = 20,000 × 10 × 146 ÷ 365 ÷ 100 = ₹800. Some lenders use a 360-day year or count days differently, so check their method if the exact rupee matters.

Where is simple interest used in India?

It appears in short-term loans between people, some gold and personal loans quoted at flat rates, interest on delayed payments, certain short fixed deposits paid at maturity and school maths problems. Most bank loans and long deposits use reducing balance or compound interest instead.

What is a flat interest rate?

A flat rate charges simple interest on the full loan amount for the whole tenure, even though you repay part of it every month. This makes the real cost much higher than the quoted figure. A 10% flat rate over three years costs about as much as an 18% reducing-balance loan.
