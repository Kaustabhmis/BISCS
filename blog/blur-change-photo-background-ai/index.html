A blurred background gives an ordinary phone photo the look of a portrait camera, while a replacement background turns a cluttered room into a clean colour, a smooth gradient or a completely different scene.

Getting a natural portrait blur

Choose a moderate strength

Real camera blur increases gradually with distance. Because this tool applies the same blur to everything behind the person, a very strong setting can look artificial, especially where the floor or a table runs from the person into the background. Moderate strength tends to look most believable.

Watch the edges near hair and hands

Where hair or fingers meet the background, a slightly soft edge looks more natural than a hard cut. Increase edge softness by a step or two if you see a crisp outline.

Start from a sharp subject

Blurring the background makes the subject look sharper by contrast, but it cannot fix a subject that is already blurry from camera shake.

Choosing a replacement background that looks real

When you replace the background with an image, the eye checks a few things instinctively:

  • Lighting direction. If sunlight falls on your face from the left, a background lit from the right looks wrong. Choose backgrounds with soft, even light.
  • Perspective. A photo taken at eye level looks odd on a background shot from high above.
  • Colour temperature. A warm indoor photo on a cool blue mountain scene looks pasted on. Softening the background with the slider reduces the mismatch.
  • Scale. Keep the background believable for the size of the person.

For professional uses such as LinkedIn, a company website or a conference badge, a plain colour or subtle gradient usually looks more polished than a scenic image.

Which background colour suits which purpose?

Colour sets the tone of a portrait before anyone reads a word, so it is worth choosing deliberately:

  • White or off-white: formal and neutral. Typical for ID-style photos, company directories and official profiles. Avoid it if you are wearing white, as you will blend into it.
  • Light grey: professional without being stark, and flattering for most skin tones. A safe choice for LinkedIn and résumés.
  • Light blue: calm and trustworthy. Popular for doctors, consultants, teachers and corporate headshots.
  • Charcoal or deep blue: confident and modern. Works well for founders, speakers and event posters, especially with light clothing.
  • Green or amber: lively and friendly. Good for creative profiles, coaching classes and social posts, less so for formal documents.
  • Gradients: a radial spotlight gradient, lighter behind the head, gives a studio feel and helps the face stand out.

Whatever you pick, use it consistently across a team page or a set of thumbnails so everything looks like it belongs together.

Ideas for everyday use

  • Profile photos: a light grey or soft blue gradient behind a well-lit face makes a clean professional headshot from a photo taken at home.
  • Wedding and festival pictures: blur a crowded pandal or reception hall so the people stand out.
  • Online classes and coaching: create consistent teacher photos for course pages with the same colour background.
  • Social posts and thumbnails: a bold gradient behind a cut-out face is a popular thumbnail style. Add text in the Social Post Maker.

Honest limits

  • The tool is designed for people. For products and objects, the AI Background Remover has an Object mode.
  • Fine hair, see-through fabric and fast motion blur can leave small halos.
  • Very large photos above about 16 megapixels are reduced before processing.
  • Processing happens on your device, so older phones can take longer, and the first run includes the model download.

Common mistakes

  • Using maximum blur on a full-length photo. The ground near the feet becomes blurred too, which looks unnatural.
  • Pasting a bright outdoor scene behind a dim indoor photo. Use the soften slider or pick a more neutral background.
  • Choosing JPG when you want to edit further. For more editing later, PNG avoids an extra round of compression.
  • Using a white background for a passport photo here. It will look right, but sizing and head position matter too. The AI Passport Photo tool handles all of that together.

Private by design

The AI model is downloaded to your browser and runs there. Your photo is processed on your own phone or computer and is not sent to any server, which makes it safe for family pictures and photos of children.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from portrait mode on my phone?

Phone portrait mode usually uses depth sensors or multiple cameras at the moment you take the picture. This tool works afterwards on any existing photo, using AI to find the person and blur everything else. It does not measure true depth, so the blur strength is the same across the whole background.

Does it work on photos without people?

This tool is built around a person-segmentation model, so it works best when there is at least one clearly visible person. For products, pets or other objects, use the AI Background Remover in Object mode, then place the cut-out on a background in another tool.

How big is the AI download?

The first time you use it, your browser downloads the AI engine and model, about 26 MB in total. It is stored by your browser, so later visits start much faster. The download is the same one the background remover uses, so if you have used that tool already, it may load instantly.

Can I use my own background image?

Yes. Choose Image, then pick any JPG, PNG or WebP picture. It fills the background behind the person, and the Soften background image slider adds blur to it, which helps the person stand out and hides differences in lighting between the two photos.
