How to Find the HSN or SAC Code for Your Product or Service

Search HSN codes for goods and SAC codes for services, understand 4-, 6- and 8-digit codes, and learn how many digits your GST invoices need to show.

By BISCS India Editorial Team Updated 2 November 2026 5 min read
Quick answer

HSN codes classify goods and SAC codes classify services for GST. Search by product or service name, narrow to the right chapter and heading, then confirm the full code and current rate in the official CBIC schedule. To find the HSN code for a product, search by what the product is (for example, "t-shirt" or "laptop"), identify the right chapter and 4-digit heading, and then take the full 6- or 8-digit code from the official tariff. For services, do the same with SAC codes, which all start with 99. Always confirm the code and its GST rate on the CBIC website before you use it on invoices.

What do HSN and SAC codes look like?

HSN codes get more specific as digits are added:

Digits Level Example
2 Chapter 61: Articles of apparel and clothing accessories, knitted or crocheted
4 Heading 6109: T-shirts, singlets and other vests, knitted
6 Sub-heading (international HS) Specific type within 6109
8 Tariff item (India) Used in customs and for exports or imports

SAC codes for services are usually 6 digits, starting with 99. For example, 9982 is the group for legal and accounting services, and 998211 covers legal advisory and representation in criminal law.

How many digits do you need on a GST invoice?

The rule under current CBIC notifications depends on your aggregate turnover in the previous financial year:

  • Above ₹5 crore: 6-digit HSN or SAC on all tax invoices.
  • Up to ₹5 crore: 4-digit HSN or SAC on B2B tax invoices; optional on B2C invoices.
  • Exporters and importers generally use the full 8-digit ITC-HS code in shipping documents.

HSN-wise summaries are also reported in GSTR-1, so using consistent codes on every invoice makes return filing easier.

Goods or services? Tricky cases

Choosing between an HSN and a SAC code is usually obvious, but a few situations catch people out:

  • Restaurants and takeaways supply a service, so they use SAC 996331, even though the customer receives food.
  • Repairs and maintenance are services. A shop that repairs laptops uses a SAC code, such as 998713, for the labour, and an HSN code for any spare parts it sells separately on the invoice.
  • Software can be goods or a service depending on how it is supplied, for example packaged media versus a subscription or development work.
  • Construction is generally a service under SAC group 9954, while selling building materials alone is a supply of goods.
  • Job work on someone else's goods is a service, even though the result is a physical product.

Where an invoice mixes goods and services, each line needs its own code. If one supply naturally bundles several items, GST has rules for composite and mixed supplies that decide which rate applies; this is worth confirming with a practitioner.

Why the finder does not show GST rates

GST rates were rationalised with effect from 22 September 2025, with most goods and services moving into the 5% and 18% slabs and a 40% rate for certain luxury and sin goods. Rates for specific items continue to change by notification. Rather than risk showing a stale rate, the finder focuses on getting the code right. Once you have it, look up the rate on the CBIC website and use the GST Calculator to work out the tax.

Tips for choosing the right code

  • Describe what the item is, not what it is used for. Classification is mainly by material and nature.
  • Prefer the more specific heading when two seem to fit.
  • Check your suppliers' invoices. They often show the HSN for the same goods, though you should still verify it.
  • Keep a product master. Record each product's HSN once and reuse it in the Invoice Generator.

Common mistakes

  1. Using a SAC code for goods or vice versa.
  2. Showing only 4 digits when your turnover requires 6.
  3. Copying a rate along with an old code from a pre-September 2025 invoice.
  4. Guessing from a similar product instead of checking the heading's description.
  5. Forgetting to validate the customer's GSTIN on B2B invoices; the GSTIN Validator does this quickly.

Limits of the finder

The finder is an offline guide with HS chapters, common headings and service codes. It is not the full Customs Tariff, does not list every 6- or 8-digit sub-heading, and does not decide classification disputes. This article is general information, not legal or tax advice. Verify codes and rates on the CBIC website and the GST portal, and for doubtful or high-value classifications, consult a GST practitioner or consider an advance ruling.

Frequently asked questions

How many digits of HSN do I need on my invoice?

Under the current notification, businesses with aggregate turnover above ₹5 crore in the previous financial year must show a 6-digit HSN or SAC on all tax invoices. Those with turnover up to ₹5 crore must show 4 digits on B2B invoices, while it is optional on B2C invoices. Check the latest CBIC notification.

What is the difference between HSN and SAC?

HSN (Harmonised System of Nomenclature) is an international system for classifying goods, organised into chapters and headings. SAC (Services Accounting Code) is India's classification for services under GST, and all SAC codes begin with 99. Goods use HSN and services use SAC on invoices and returns.

Does the finder show GST rates for each code?

No. GST rates were rationalised from 22 September 2025 and continue to be revised by notification, so showing rates offline risks giving outdated information. Use the finder to identify the right code, then confirm the current rate on the CBIC GST rate pages before billing.

What if my product fits more than one HSN code?

Classification follows the General Rules of Interpretation of the Harmonised System, which look at the specific description, the essential character of the product and other tests. If two headings seem to fit, the more specific one usually applies. For high-value or disputed items, ask a GST practitioner or consider an advance ruling.

Why does the finder only show 4-digit HSN headings?

The finder lists all 97 HS chapters, around 700 common 4-digit headings and over 100 SAC groups and codes, which is enough to find the right area quickly. Six- and eight-digit sub-headings run into thousands and are best taken from the Customs Tariff or the CBIC rate schedule.
