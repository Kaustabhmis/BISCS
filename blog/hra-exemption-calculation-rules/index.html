HRA Exemption Calculator: How Tax-Free HRA Is Worked Out

Learn how the tax-exempt part of House Rent Allowance is calculated using the lowest of three amounts, with metro rules, rent receipts and worked ₹ examples.

By BISCS India Editorial Team Updated 2 November 2026 5 min read
Quick answer

Your HRA exemption is the lowest of three amounts: the HRA you actually receive, rent paid minus 10% of basic salary plus DA, and 50% of basic plus DA in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata or Chennai (40% elsewhere). The tax-free part of your House Rent Allowance (HRA) is the lowest of three amounts, all calculated for the year:

  1. The actual HRA you received from your employer.
  2. Rent paid minus 10% of your basic salary plus dearness allowance (DA).
  3. 50% of basic plus DA if you live in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata or Chennai, or 40% in any other city.

Whatever HRA is left above that lowest figure is taxable. The exemption is available only under the old tax regime. The free HRA Exemption Calculator works out all three limits and highlights the one that applies to you.

Worked example: a renter in Kolkata

Arjun works in Kolkata. His basic salary plus DA is ₹50,000 a month, his employer pays ₹20,000 as HRA, and he pays ₹18,000 a month in rent.

Condition (annual) Calculation Amount
Actual HRA received 20,000 × 12 ₹2,40,000
Rent minus 10% of basic + DA 2,16,000 − 60,000 ₹1,56,000
50% of basic + DA (metro) 6,00,000 × 50% ₹3,00,000

The lowest is ₹1,56,000, so that much of his HRA is exempt. The remaining ₹84,000 is added to his taxable salary. Monthly, that is ₹13,000 exempt and ₹7,000 taxable.

Notice that if Arjun lived in a non-metro city, the third limit would be ₹2,40,000, but his exemption would still be ₹1,56,000, because rent is the binding limit. In his case, paying more rent (genuinely) would raise the exemption; moving cities would not.

A second example: when the city limit bites

Divya works in Pune with basic plus DA of ₹40,000 a month, HRA of ₹20,000 and rent of ₹25,000.

  • Actual HRA: ₹2,40,000
  • Rent minus 10% of basic: 3,00,000 − 48,000 = ₹2,52,000
  • 40% of basic (non-metro): ₹1,92,000

Her exemption is ₹1,92,000 and ₹48,000 is taxable. Here the 40% city cap is the lowest figure, so higher rent would not increase her exemption.

What if you moved house or city during the year?

The three limits are worked out for the period you actually paid rent, so a mid-year move means calculating each period separately and adding the results.

Suppose Farhan's basic plus DA is ₹50,000 a month and his HRA is ₹20,000 a month all year. From April to September he rents in Pune for ₹15,000 a month; from October to March he rents in Mumbai for ₹22,000 a month.

Period (6 months) Actual HRA Rent − 10% of basic City limit Exempt
Apr–Sep, Pune (40%) ₹1,20,000 90,000 − 30,000 = ₹60,000 ₹1,20,000 ₹60,000
Oct–Mar, Mumbai (50%) ₹1,20,000 1,32,000 − 30,000 = ₹1,02,000 ₹1,50,000 ₹1,02,000

His total exemption is ₹1,62,000 and the remaining ₹78,000 of HRA is taxable. To do this with the calculator, enter each period's monthly figures, choose the right city type, and take half of the annual result for each six-month block. A salary change mid-year is handled the same way.

What counts as basic salary for HRA?

"Salary" for this rule means basic pay plus DA where DA forms part of retirement benefits, plus any commission fixed as a percentage of turnover. It does not include HRA itself, special allowances, bonuses or perquisites. Check your salary slip; the Salary Slip Generator can help you see components laid out clearly if you are structuring pay for a small team.

Documents checklist

  • Rent receipts for each month, signed by the landlord. The Rent Receipt Generator creates them quickly.
  • A rent agreement, especially for larger rents.
  • Landlord's PAN if annual rent exceeds ₹1 lakh.
  • Proof of payment, such as bank transfers or UPI records.
  • Your employer's declaration form for exemptions and deductions, often Form 12BB.

Submit these to your employer on time so the exemption is reflected in monthly TDS. If you miss the employer's deadline, you can usually still claim the exemption when filing your income tax return, provided you have the proof.

Common HRA mistakes

  1. Choosing the new regime and still expecting HRA relief. It does not apply there.
  2. Including allowances in "basic". Only basic plus qualifying DA counts.
  3. Assuming all large cities are metros. For this rule, only four cities get the 50% limit.
  4. Claiming for months you did not pay rent. Calculate for the actual period of renting; if you moved mid-year, work out each period separately.
  5. Cash rent without receipts. It makes the claim hard to support.
  6. Living in your own house in the same city. You cannot claim HRA for rent you do not actually pay.

Should you even claim HRA?

HRA only helps under the old regime, so the real question is which regime gives lower tax overall. Put your exemption into the Income Tax Calculator along with your other deductions and compare. For many people with moderate rent and few other deductions, the new regime is still cheaper.

This guide explains the standard calculation for FY 2025-26 and is general information, not tax advice. Rules, including which cities count as metros, can change, and the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from FY 2026-27. Check the Income Tax Department's website or speak to a tax professional, especially for unusual arrangements like paying rent to relatives.

Frequently asked questions

Can I claim HRA exemption under the new tax regime?

No. The HRA exemption is one of the benefits given up under the new regime. If you opt for the new regime, the whole HRA you receive is taxable as part of your salary. Compare both regimes using your actual exemption before choosing which one to use.

Which cities count as metro for HRA?

For the 50% limit, the rules have long named only Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai. Cities such as Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune have been treated as non-metro, with a 40% limit, for FY 2025-26. Check the rules in force for later years, as they can be revised.

Do I need my landlord's PAN to claim HRA?

If the rent you pay is more than ₹1 lakh in a year, your employer will typically ask for the landlord's PAN. Below that, rent receipts or a rent agreement are usually enough. Keep proof of payment such as bank transfers, as these help if your claim is ever questioned.

Can I claim HRA if I pay rent to my parents?

Many salaried people do, provided the arrangement is genuine: you actually pay the rent, ideally by bank transfer, the parent owns the house, and the parent shows the rent as income in their own tax return. Paying rent to a spouse is generally viewed with suspicion. When in doubt, take professional advice.

What if my employer does not pay HRA?

If you do not receive HRA but pay rent, you may be able to claim a deduction under Section 80GG in the old regime, subject to its own conditions and limits. This calculator covers HRA received from an employer only, so check the 80GG rules separately.
