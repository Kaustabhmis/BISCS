How to enhance a photo step by step

  1. Open the Photo Enhancer and choose a photo, drag it in or paste it with Ctrl + V.
  2. The Auto preset is applied straight away. Drag across the image to compare the original with the enhanced version.
  3. Try the other presets: Old photo for faded or dusty scans, Vivid for punchy colour, Subtle for a light touch.
  4. Fine-tune in Light & colour: Auto levels, Brightness, Contrast, Auto white balance, Warmth, Vibrance and Saturation.
  5. In Detail, choose Noise reduction (Off, Light, Strong or Median 3×3 for old scans) and set Sharpen.
  6. For small photos, tick Upscale 2× with AI. This downloads a 4 MB model once and is available for photos up to 1,200 pixels on the long side.
  7. Choose JPG or PNG and download. Reset to Auto takes you back to the starting point.

Restoring old family photographs

Many Indian homes have albums of prints from the 1970s to the 1990s that have faded, turned yellow or pink, and collected dust. A careful digitising workflow gets the best out of them:

  1. Scan or photograph the print flat. Use a scanner if you have one. Otherwise, photograph the print in daylight, holding the phone directly above it to avoid reflections. The Scan to PDF tool can detect the edges and flatten the perspective.
  2. Straighten and crop away the album page and borders.
  3. Apply the Old photo preset. It removes dust and noise, fixes fading and corrects colour casts.
  4. Adjust warmth. Some old prints have a pleasant warm tone you may want to keep; others have a strong magenta cast you will want to remove.
  5. Remove scratches or spots with the Smart Object Eraser.
  6. Upscale small prints with the built-in 2× option or the AI Image Upscaler.

Keep the original scan as well. Your taste in editing may change, and an untouched master lets you start again.

Fixing everyday lighting problems

Some lighting situations come up again and again in Indian homes and events, and each has a typical fix:

  • Tube lights and CFLs often give a slightly green or blue tint. Raise Auto white balance and nudge Warmth up a little.
  • Yellow bulbs at weddings and pujas turn everything orange. Lower Warmth and let auto white balance neutralise the cast, but stop before the scene loses its festive glow.
  • Diya-lit or candle-lit Diwali photos are dark and grainy. Increase Brightness gently and use Light or Strong noise reduction. Do not expect daylight clarity from a photo lit by a few flames.
  • Backlit photos against a window leave faces dark. Brightness and auto levels help, though a very bright window may stay washed out.
  • Hazy outdoor shots in winter look flat. Auto levels and a touch of contrast restore depth.

Tips for natural-looking results

  • Faces first. Judge the result by skin tones. If faces look orange or grey, adjust warmth and white balance before anything else.
  • Lower sharpening for portraits. Strong sharpening emphasises pores and wrinkles. Landscapes and buildings can take more.
  • Use Strong noise reduction only for very grainy photos. It smooths fine texture such as fabric patterns and hair.
  • Vibrance before saturation. Vibrance protects skin tones while lifting dull backgrounds; saturation boosts everything equally.
  • Check on another screen. A photo that looks perfect on a bright phone may look too dark on a laptop.

When to use a different tool

  • For creative looks, such as vintage, noir or sepia, Photo Filters has ready presets.
  • To blur or replace a background, use the AI Background Blur & Changer.
  • For large enlargements of very small images, the dedicated upscaler offers 4× as well.

Common mistakes

  • Pushing every slider to the right. Enhancement should be invisible; if people notice the editing, it is too much.
  • Enhancing a heavily compressed forward. Blocky compression artefacts get sharpened too. Find the original where possible.
  • Expecting blur from camera shake to disappear. Sharpening improves softness, but cannot undo motion blur.
  • Saving over the original file. Always keep the untouched version.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Photo Enhancer generative AI?

No. The main enhancement uses proven image-processing techniques: automatic levels, white balance, vibrance, edge-preserving noise reduction and sharpening. It improves what is in the photo rather than inventing new content. The optional 2× upscale is the only part that uses a neural network model.

Can it restore very old or damaged photographs?

The Old photo preset removes fine dust and noise, stretches faded contrast and corrects yellow or pink colour casts, which helps a lot with faded prints. It cannot repair tears, large stains or missing pieces. For a scratch or spot, try the Smart Object Eraser afterwards.

Why does my enhanced photo look over-processed?

Strong sharpening and high vibrance can create halos and unnatural colours, especially on faces. Try the Subtle preset, lower Sharpen and Vibrance, and use the comparison slider to check. Enhancement should make a photo look like it was taken in better conditions, not like it was edited.

Does it need to download anything?

The enhancement itself needs no download and runs instantly. Only the optional Upscale 2× with AI downloads a small model, about 4 MB, the first time you use it, and your browser keeps it for later. Nothing about your photo is uploaded.
