Speed Up or Slow Down a Video Online (0.25× to 4×)

Make a fast-forward or slow-motion version of a video from 0.25× to 4×, with natural-sounding audio, and see the new length before you export.

By BISCS India Editorial Team Updated 2 November 2026 4 min read
Quick answer

To change a video's speed, choose a factor such as 2× to halve its length or 0.5× for slow motion, then re-encode it so the new timing is saved in the file. BISCS India’s free Video Speed Changer does this in your browser from 0.25× to 4×, keeps the voice at a natural pitch, and shows the new length first.

Changing a video's speed is simple arithmetic: at 2× a ten-minute video becomes five minutes, and at 0.5× it becomes twenty. The part that takes care is the sound. Played faster, voices normally rise into a cartoon squeak; played slower, they drop into a growl.

The Video Speed Changer keeps speech at its natural pitch while changing the tempo, and lets you pick any speed from 0.25× to 4×. It runs in your browser on your own file.

Where a speed change helps

  • Recipe and craft videos. Stirring for five minutes becomes twenty seconds of satisfying fast-forward.
  • Screen recordings and tutorials. Speed up the slow install bar, keep the explanation at normal speed (after splitting the clip).
  • Lectures and long talks. A 1.25× copy is easier to revise from without losing clarity.
  • Sport and dance. Slow down a cover drive or a footwork sequence to study it.
  • Travel clips. A fast drive along the ghats or a sunset in time-lapse style adds variety to a reel.

How to use the Video Speed Changer

  1. Open the Video Speed Changer and choose a video (MP4, MOV, WebM or MKV), or drag it onto the page.
  2. Under Quick choice, tap 0.5×, 0.75×, 1.25×, 1.5× or 2×. The tool starts at 2×.
  3. For any other value, drag the Speed slider. It moves in steps of 0.05, from 0.25× up to 4×.
  4. Read the line underneath. It shows the new length, such as "New length: 2:30 (from 5:00)", and a note on audio or slow motion.
  5. Play the preview. It plays at the chosen speed so you can judge it before exporting, though the browser preview may sound slightly different from the final file.
  6. Tick Remove audio (mute) if you plan to add music later or the sped-up sound is not useful.
  7. Press Change speed. When it finishes, preview and download the MP4.

The first time you process a video, the browser downloads the video engine (about 32 MB) from this site and stores it for later visits. Your video is not uploaded at any point.

Choosing the right speed

Speed Effect Good for
0.25× Very slow, visibly steppy Short moments recorded at high frame rates
0.5× Classic slow motion Sport, dance, splashes, a child's first steps
0.75× Gently slowed Following fast instructions, musical practice
1.25× to 1.5× Brisk but natural Talks, tutorials, long vlogs
2× Clearly fast-forward Cooking prep, walking routes, setup steps
3× to 4× Time-lapse feel Traffic, clouds, building something over hours

Making slow motion look good

True slow motion is recorded, not created afterwards. Many phones offer a slow-motion mode that films at 120 or 240 frames per second; that footage slows down beautifully. Ordinary 30 fps video has only 30 pictures per second, so at 0.5× each picture is held for two frame slots, and at 0.25× for four. Movement then looks like a slideshow.

For the best results with regular footage:

  • Stay at 0.5× or above for anything with fast motion.
  • Choose steady shots. Camera shake becomes very obvious when slowed down.
  • Keep it short. A few seconds of slow motion has impact; a minute of it drags.

Speeding up without losing the viewer

Fast-forward works best when the viewer can still follow what is happening. At 2× most actions stay readable. Above 3×, people and hands start to blur, which can look lively in a time-lapse but confusing in a tutorial. For sped-up sections with no useful speech, consider muting the audio and adding a light background track with Add Music to Video.

If you are speeding a video up to fit a length limit, such as a 60-second Short, check the new length line before exporting. Changing speed is not always the best fix, though; trimming dull parts usually keeps the video more watchable.

A worked example

Suppose you filmed an eight-minute video of a rangoli being drawn and want it to fit a 60-second Reel. Eight minutes is 480 seconds, so you would need 8× to reach one minute, which is beyond the 4× maximum and far too fast to follow anyway. A better plan is to trim the clip to the three most interesting minutes, then apply 3×. That gives exactly 60 seconds, the pattern still builds visibly, and the speed feels lively rather than frantic. Mute the sped-up chatter and add a soft music track, and the result is ready to post.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Speeding up talking-head videos too far. Above 1.5×, many viewers find speech tiring.
  • Expecting smooth 0.25× from phone video. Without high frame rate footage, very slow speeds will stutter.
  • Choosing 1× and pressing the button. The tool asks you to pick a different speed, since 1× would change nothing.
  • Changing speed on a very long video on a phone. Re-encoding is heavy work; trim to the part you need first with the Video Trimmer.
  • Closing the tab during processing. The export stops, and you will need to start again.

What the output looks like

You get an MP4 (H.264 video, AAC audio) named after your file with the speed added, for example holiday-2x.mp4. When speeding up, the frame rate is capped at 30 fps; for slow motion it keeps the source frame rate up to 60. There is no watermark, no account to create, and the file is ready to post or share as it is.

Frequently asked questions

Will speeding up a video make voices sound squeaky?

No. The audio is time-stretched separately from the picture, so speech gets faster without rising in pitch. At 1.25× to 1.5× it usually sounds like a brisk speaker. At 3× or 4× words run together and become hard to follow, so many people tick Remove audio for those speeds.

Why does my slow-motion video look jerky?

Slow motion needs more frames than the video has. At 0.5× each original frame is shown twice as long, and at 0.25× four times as long, so movement steps instead of flowing. Footage recorded at 60 fps or higher slows down far more smoothly than ordinary 30 fps phone video.

Can I change the speed of only part of a video?

This tool applies one speed to the whole file. To speed up just one section, cut that section out with a trimmer, change its speed here, and join it back with the other parts using the Video Merger. For short clips, it is often simpler to trim first and speed up the entire clip.

What happens to the frame rate when I speed a video up?

When speeding up, the output is capped at 30 frames per second. Extra frames are dropped rather than packed in, which keeps the file a sensible size and plays smoothly on phones. Slow-motion output keeps the original frame rate up to 60 fps.
