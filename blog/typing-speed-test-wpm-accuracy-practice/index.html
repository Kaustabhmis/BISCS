Take a 1, 2 or 5 minute typing test, learn how net WPM, gross WPM and accuracy are worked out, and practise for data entry jobs and skill tests.

A typing speed test tells you how fast you type in words per minute (WPM) and how accurately. By the common convention, every five characters you type, including spaces, count as one word. Net WPM counts only correct characters, so mistakes pull your score down.

Many people who type every day score somewhere around 30 to 50 WPM, though it varies a lot. The free Typing Speed Test on BISCS India gives you a 1, 2 or 5 minute test, shows your WPM and accuracy live as you type, and tells you which level your result falls in.

How WPM and accuracy are calculated

The test reports three main numbers at the end:

  • Net WPM: correct characters ÷ 5 ÷ minutes taken. This is the large figure shown in the result and in the live WPM box.
  • Gross WPM: all characters typed ÷ 5 ÷ minutes, whether right or wrong.
  • Accuracy: the share of keystrokes that were correct when you pressed them.

Accuracy here is strict. Every key you press is counted at the moment you press it, so a wrong letter you later fix with backspace still counts as an error. This is closer to how real work feels, because each correction costs you time.

The result also lists how many correct and wrong characters remain in your final text, and the time taken. It then places you in one of five levels:

Net WPM Level
Below 25 Beginner
25 to 39 Average
40 to 59 Good
60 to 79 Excellent
80 and above Outstanding

Worked example: reading your result

Say you take the 1 minute test and type 250 characters, of which 230 match the passage. You pressed 260 keys in total because you deleted and retyped a few letters, and 13 of those key presses were wrong.

  • Gross WPM = 250 ÷ 5 ÷ 1 = 50
  • Net WPM = 230 ÷ 5 ÷ 1 = 46
  • Accuracy = (260 − 13) ÷ 260 ≈ 95%

At 46 net WPM you fall in the Good band. The gap between gross and net speed (four words here) is the real cost of your errors. If you keep the same pace on a 5 minute test, you would type about 1,150 correct characters, roughly two average paragraphs.

Typing tests for jobs and exams

Many data entry roles, office assistant posts and some government recruitment exams include a typing or skill test. Data entry employers often check both speed and accuracy, and some measure key depressions per hour rather than WPM.

Government exams vary widely. For example, some SSC exams such as CHSL and CGL commonly include a typing or data entry skill test for certain posts, with separate requirements for English and Hindi. The required speed, duration, how errors are penalised and whether the test is only qualifying all depend on the exam, the post and the year’s notification. Do not rely on numbers quoted in coaching videos or forums; read the latest official notification and practise to the standard it states.

Treat the BISCS test as practice. Exam software may use a different passage length, error rule or formula, so your score there can differ from what you see here.

Tips to improve your typing speed

  • Learn the home row. Rest your fingers on A S D F and J K L ; and return to them after each key.
  • Stop looking down. Touch typing feels slower for the first week or two but raises your ceiling a lot.
  • Accuracy first. Aim for 95% or higher before chasing speed; an accurate typist improves faster.
  • Practise little and often. Fifteen minutes a day beats an hour once a week.
  • Use longer tests. Once 1 minute feels easy, move to 2 and 5 minutes to build stamina.
  • Track progress, not single scores. Your speed will vary from day to day; look at your average over a week.

To see how much you type in a real document, paste your finished text into the Word Counter and compare its length with the time you spent.

Mistakes to avoid

  1. Rushing and correcting constantly. Every backspace costs accuracy and time. A steady rhythm is faster than bursts.
  2. Practising only one passage. Repeating the same text lets you memorise it, which inflates your score. Use New text regularly.
  3. Poor posture. Sit upright, keep wrists level and the screen at eye height to avoid strain during long tests.
  4. Ignoring punctuation and capitals. The passages include commas, full stops and capital letters, and so does real work.
  5. Comparing with a different tool’s score. WPM formulas, error rules and passage difficulty differ, so compare your results on the same test over time.

It works on phones, but a physical keyboard gives a far more realistic result, especially for exam practice. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between net WPM and gross WPM?

Gross WPM counts every character you typed, right or wrong, divided by five and by the minutes taken. Net WPM counts only the characters that match the passage. If you type 250 characters in a minute and 230 are correct, gross WPM is 50 and net WPM is 46. Net WPM is the fairer measure of usable speed.

Why is my accuracy lower than I expected after fixing my mistakes?

The test counts every keystroke as it happens. A wrong letter is recorded as an error even if you press backspace and correct it straight away. This mirrors real work, where each correction costs time. To raise accuracy, slow down slightly and aim to hit the right key the first time.

What typing speed do I need for a government exam skill test?

It depends on the exam, the post and the language. Some SSC posts, for example, commonly ask for a typing skill test at a set speed in English or Hindi, measured in their own way. The rules can change between notifications, so always read the latest official notice for the exact speed, duration and method.

Is a 1 minute test enough to know my real speed?

It gives a quick snapshot, but short tests can flatter you because you stay fresh for the whole minute. A 5 minute test shows whether you can hold your speed and accuracy as you tire, which is closer to what data entry work and most skill tests demand. Try all three lengths.

Can I take the typing test in Hindi?

Not at the moment. The passages in this test are in English only. If you are preparing for a Hindi typing test, you will need a tool that supports the specific keyboard layout your exam uses, such as Remington Gail or Inscript, and you should practise with that layout.
