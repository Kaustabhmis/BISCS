Where are mockups useful?

  • Client presentations. A web agency in Bengaluru showing a new site on a laptop and phone side by side communicates "responsive" better than any bullet point.
  • App store and landing page visuals. A clean phone frame around key screens helps people understand the app at a glance.
  • Portfolios. Freelance designers and developers look more professional when case studies show work in context.
  • Social media launches. "Our new website is live" posts get more attention with a polished image than a raw screenshot.
  • Proposals and pitch decks. Mockups make a prototype feel real to investors and stakeholders.

How to create a device mockup

  1. Take a screenshot of your app or website. On a phone, use the normal screenshot shortcut; on a computer, capture the browser window.
  2. Open the Device Mockup Generator and choose the screenshot, drag it in, or paste it with Ctrl+V.
  3. Pick a Device. Use the browser frame for websites and a phone frame for mobile apps.
  4. For a responsive website, switch the Scene to Phone + laptop and add a separate phone screenshot.
  5. Choose a Frame colour and, for phones and tablets, an Orientation.
  6. Set the Screenshot fit. "Fill (top)" is ideal for long web pages, because it shows the header and above-the-fold content.
  7. For browser frames, choose a light or dark Window style and type your domain into the Address bar text.
  8. Add Perspective tilt and a Soft shadow if you like, and set a background colour, gradient or none.
  9. Choose the Resolution and press Download PNG.

All processing happens in your browser, so unreleased designs and client dashboards are never uploaded.

Getting great-looking results

Start with a sharp screenshot. Mockups magnify blur. Capture at full resolution and avoid screenshots that have been forwarded on WhatsApp, since compression softens them.

Clean up the screen first. Hide personal notifications, set the phone clock to a neat time if you can, use sample data instead of real customer names, and close developer tools before capturing a browser.

Show the most important part. For a landing page, the top section with the headline and main button is usually the best view. For an app, choose one screen that explains its core purpose.

Match the background to your brand. A soft gradient in your brand colours looks modern. For slides and websites with their own background, export with no background to get a transparent PNG.

Use 2× resolution. It stays crisp on high-density screens and in printed proposals without creating an enormous file.

How to take clean screenshots for mockups

On most Android phones, press the power and volume-down buttons together; on an iPhone, press the side button and volume-up. For a website, open it in a desktop browser at a normal window width and use your operating system's screenshot shortcut, or the browser's own capture option in its developer tools for a precise full-width image.

To capture the mobile version of a website from a computer, open the browser's developer tools and switch on the device toolbar, choose a common phone width, then capture. That gives a correctly proportioned mobile screenshot for the phone half of a phone-plus-laptop scene.

Check the screenshot at full size before you use it. Small typos and placeholder text become very obvious once the image is framed and shared.

Common mockup mistakes

  • Wrong aspect ratio. A desktop screenshot squeezed into a phone frame looks wrong. Use "Fit whole" only when necessary, and capture mobile screens on a phone or in your browser's mobile view.
  • Real personal data on screen. Customer phone numbers, Aadhaar details or payment information should never appear in a marketing image. Blur them first or use dummy data.
  • Too much tilt. A slight angle adds depth; a steep one makes text unreadable.
  • Busy backgrounds. A photographic background competes with the screenshot. Keep it simple.
  • Huge file sizes on websites. If you place a 3× PNG on a web page, compress it first with the Image Compressor or convert it to WebP with the Image Converter.

Putting your mockup to work

Once exported, a mockup slots into many places. Add it to a LinkedIn or Instagram announcement using the Instagram Grid & Story Maker, use it as the hero image of a case study, or set it as the share image for a launch page so link previews on WhatsApp look polished.

If you need to remove sensitive details from a screenshot before framing it, crop or edit it first, then bring it back into the Device Mockup Generator for the final, presentation-ready image.

Frequently asked questions

What screenshot size works best for a phone mockup?

Use a full-resolution screenshot taken on a phone, typically a tall portrait image. The generator can fit the whole screenshot, fill the screen from the top, or fill from the centre, so slightly different aspect ratios still look right. Avoid small or heavily compressed screenshots.

Can I show a website on a laptop and phone together?

Yes. Choose the Phone + laptop scene, add your desktop screenshot for the laptop and a separate phone screenshot for the phone. This combined view is a common way to show that a website is responsive.

Can I export a mockup with a transparent background?

Yes. Set the background to none and the PNG is exported with transparency, so you can place the device on any slide, web page or design. The tool shows the export size and confirms when the background is transparent.

Are the device frames copies of real branded products?

The frames are generic, drawn illustrations such as an iPhone-style phone, an Android phone, a tablet and a laptop. They are not official product images, which keeps them suitable for general presentations and portfolios.

Are my screenshots uploaded anywhere?

No. Screenshots are opened and drawn into the mockup using your browser’s canvas, and the PNG is created on your device. That makes it safe for unreleased apps, client dashboards and internal tools.
