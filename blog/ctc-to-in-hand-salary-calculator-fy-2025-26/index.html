CTC to In-Hand Salary: Monthly Take-Home for FY 2025-26

Turn your annual CTC into monthly take-home pay after PF, gratuity, professional tax and income tax under the FY 2025-26 new or old tax regime.

By BISCS India Editorial Team Updated 2 November 2026 5 min read
Quick answer

Your in-hand salary is your CTC minus employer PF, gratuity, any variable bonus, your own PF, professional tax and income tax, divided over 12 months. BISCS India’s free CTC to In-Hand Salary Calculator does this for FY 2025-26, so a ₹12 lakh CTC with 50% basic works out to about ₹85,395 a month.

To find your in-hand salary, start from your annual CTC, remove the parts you never receive as monthly pay (employer PF, gratuity and any variable bonus), then subtract your own PF, professional tax and income tax. Divide what is left by 12.

For FY 2025-26, a ₹12,00,000 CTC with basic at 50% gives a gross salary of ₹10,99,140, no income tax under the new regime and a monthly in-hand salary of about ₹85,395. A ₹20,00,000 CTC on the same structure pays ₹1,57,435 in tax and takes home about ₹1,29,339 a month. The free CTC to In-Hand Salary Calculator works this out for your own offer.

What is CTC, and what is not part of take-home?

CTC (cost to company) is everything your employer spends on you in a year. Some of it reaches your bank account every month and some of it does not:

  • Employer PF: usually 12% of basic, deposited in your provident fund account.
  • Gratuity: about 4.81% of basic, set aside and paid only when you leave after the qualifying service.
  • Variable pay or bonus: paid once or twice a year, often linked to performance, so it is not in the monthly salary.

What remains after employer PF and gratuity is your gross salary. From this, three main deductions come out:

  • Employee PF: another 12% of basic, or a smaller amount if PF is capped.
  • Professional tax: a state levy, often ₹200 a month, with a maximum of ₹2,500 a year.
  • Income tax (TDS): worked out on the year’s taxable salary and spread across your payslips.

FY 2025-26 tax rules the calculator uses

The tool uses the same rules as the BISCS Income Tax Calculator. Under the new regime, which is the default, the slabs are:

Taxable income Rate
Up to ₹4,00,000 Nil
₹4,00,001 to ₹8,00,000 5%
₹8,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 10%
₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000 15%
₹16,00,001 to ₹20,00,000 20%
₹20,00,001 to ₹24,00,000 25%
Above ₹24,00,000 30%

Salaried employees get a standard deduction of ₹75,000 in the new regime and ₹50,000 in the old one. The section 87A rebate makes tax nil when new regime taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less, and 4% health and education cess is added to the final tax. The calculator works out both regimes and uses whichever is lower.

Worked example: ₹12 lakh and ₹20 lakh CTC

Both cases use 50% basic, full PF at 12% of basic, gratuity in CTC, ₹2,400 professional tax and no bonus.

Item ₹12 lakh CTC ₹20 lakh CTC
Basic salary ₹6,00,000 ₹10,00,000
Employer PF + gratuity ₹1,00,860 ₹1,68,100
Gross salary ₹10,99,140 ₹18,31,900
Employee PF ₹72,000 ₹1,20,000
Income tax (new regime) ₹0 ₹1,57,435
Monthly in-hand ₹85,395 ₹1,29,339

At ₹12 lakh CTC, taxable income after the standard deduction is about ₹10.24 lakh, so the rebate wipes out the tax. At ₹20 lakh, taxable income is about ₹17.57 lakh, so the top part of it is taxed in the 20% slab. Notice that a CTC rise of ₹8 lakh adds about ₹44,000 to monthly pay, because PF and tax both grow with salary.

If the ₹12 lakh employee’s company caps PF at ₹1,800 a month, in-hand pay rises to about ₹93,795. The ₹8,400 a month difference is money that would otherwise have gone into the PF account.

Tips for reading an offer letter

  • Separate fixed and variable pay. Ask what portion of the CTC is guaranteed. Only the fixed part reaches you every month.
  • Check the basic percentage. A higher basic means more PF and gratuity, which lowers monthly pay but builds savings.
  • Ask about the PF rule. Some employers calculate PF on the full basic; others cap it at the statutory wage ceiling of ₹15,000 a month.
  • Look for one-time items. Joining bonuses, relocation and stock grants are sometimes shown inside CTC but are not regular salary.
  • Work out HRA if you rent. In the old regime, the HRA Calculator shows how much of your house rent allowance is exempt, which you can then enter here.

Common mistakes when estimating take-home pay

  1. Dividing CTC by 12. This ignores PF, gratuity, bonus and tax, and can overstate monthly pay by about ₹14,600 on a ₹12 lakh package.
  2. Treating the bonus as monthly income. Variable pay depends on performance and arrives later; budget on fixed pay alone.
  3. Assuming the old regime is better. Without large deductions such as HRA or home loan interest, the new regime is usually cheaper at most salary levels.
  4. Forgetting the bonus month. The tool spreads tax evenly across 12 months, but employers often deduct extra TDS in the month a bonus is paid.
  5. Ignoring state differences. Professional tax varies, and a few states do not charge it.

Limits of the estimate

The calculator assumes a salaried employee below 60 with no other income, and it does not model employer NPS contributions, ESI, leave encashment or a mid-year joining date. Payslips vary with each company’s salary structure, so treat the result as an estimate and confirm the details with HR. For major tax decisions, a chartered accountant can review your full situation.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my in-hand salary so much lower than my CTC?

CTC is the total cost to the company, not your pay. It includes employer PF and gratuity, which go to your retirement savings, and often a variable bonus paid once a year. Your own PF, professional tax and income tax are then deducted from each salary. Together these can take 15–30% off the CTC figure.

Is there any income tax on a ₹12 lakh CTC under the new regime?

Usually not. With 50% basic, a ₹12 lakh CTC gives a gross salary of about ₹10.99 lakh. After the ₹75,000 standard deduction, taxable income is about ₹10.24 lakh, which is below ₹12 lakh, so the section 87A rebate brings the tax to nil in the new regime.

Does capping PF at ₹1,800 a month increase take-home pay?

Yes, if your employer allows it. PF is then worked out on a basic of ₹15,000 a month instead of your full basic. On a ₹12 lakh CTC with 50% basic, the calculator shows monthly in-hand rising from ₹85,395 to ₹93,795, though your retirement savings grow more slowly.

How much professional tax should I enter?

It depends on your state. Many states that levy it charge up to ₹2,500 a year, and the calculator uses ₹2,400 as a starting value. Some states and union territories have no professional tax at all, in which case enter 0. Your payslip shows the exact monthly amount deducted.

Why does my first payslip not match the calculator?

Companies structure pay differently. Meal cards, insurance premiums, NPS, HRA rules, joining-month proration and how TDS is spread across the year all change the monthly figure. Employers also collect your investment declarations, which affect old regime TDS. Use the tool as an estimate and compare it with your offer letter breakdown.
