PPF Calculator: Public Provident Fund Maturity Over 15 Years

Estimate your PPF maturity value over 15 years or with 5-year extensions, see the year-wise balance and learn the deposit limits, tax benefits and timing tips.

A PPF (Public Provident Fund) calculator estimates how much your account will hold after 15 years of yearly deposits. Each year, your opening balance plus that year's deposit earns interest at the PPF rate, and the interest is added to the balance. If you invest ₹1.5 lakh every year at 7.1%, the account grows to about ₹40.68 lakh after 15 years.

Of that, ₹22.5 lakh is what you put in and about ₹18.18 lakh is interest, all of it tax-free. The free PPF Calculator shows this growth year by year and lets you test 5-year extensions.

How is PPF interest calculated?

The scheme calculates interest on the lowest balance between the 5th and the last day of each month and credits it once a year, at the end of the financial year. If you deposit before 5 April, your money earns interest for all twelve months.

The BISCS calculator assumes exactly that: one deposit at the start of each financial year. Each year it works out:

closing balance = (opening balance + deposit) × (1 + rate ÷ 100)

For ₹1,50,000 at 7.1%, year one ends at ₹1,60,650. Year two starts with that, adds another ₹1,50,000 and earns 7.1% on ₹3,10,650, and so on. By year 15, the interest credited for that single year is about ₹2.7 lakh.

PPF rules worth knowing

  • Deposit limits: minimum ₹500 and maximum ₹1,50,000 per financial year.
  • Tenure: the account matures after 15 full financial years, and can then be extended in 5-year blocks.
  • Tax treatment: interest and maturity are tax-free; deposits qualify for Section 80C in the old regime only. You can compare regimes in the Income Tax Calculator.
  • Rate changes: the government notifies small savings rates quarterly.
  • Liquidity: loans and partial withdrawals are allowed only after certain years and within limits, so treat PPF as long-term money.

Because these rules are set by the government and can change, confirm current details with India Post, your bank or the National Savings Institute before acting.

Why tax-free interest matters

A PPF rate can look similar to an FD rate, but the comparison changes after tax. FD interest is taxed every year at your slab rate. For someone in the 30% slab, adding 4% cess, a 7% FD keeps only about 4.8% after tax. PPF interest at 7.1% is tax-free, so the full amount stays in the account and compounds.

Over 15 years, that gap compounds into a large difference. This is why PPF remains popular with salaried people in higher tax brackets who want a guaranteed-return component in their savings, even though the money is locked in for a long time. For people with income below the taxable limit, the tax advantage matters less, and liquidity may matter more. If you are comparing options, run the deposit through the FD Calculator and then reduce the interest by your tax rate to compare fairly.

Tips to get more from your PPF

  1. Deposit early in the year. Paying by 5 April each year earns interest for twelve months rather than fewer.
  2. If you deposit monthly, pay by the 5th. A deposit on the 6th misses that month's interest.
  3. Do not skip the minimum. Missing the ₹500 minimum makes the account inactive until you regularise it with a penalty.
  4. Decide on extension before maturity. Extending with deposits requires a timely request under the scheme rules.
  5. Use PPF as the stable part of a portfolio. For higher-risk, higher-potential growth, compare with a SIP Calculator estimate.

Common mistakes

  • Depositing more than ₹1.5 lakh in a year, expecting extra interest.
  • Assuming the 80C deduction applies under the new tax regime.
  • Planning on a single rate for 15 years without testing a lower one.
  • Treating PPF like a savings account and expecting easy withdrawals in the early years.

Limitations and a quick caveat

The calculator assumes one deposit before 5 April every year and the same rate throughout. If you deposit in instalments or later in the year, your actual balance will be a little lower. The real rate will change over 15 years, sometimes up and sometimes down.

Frequently asked questions

What is the maximum I can deposit in PPF in a year?

The limit is ₹1,50,000 per financial year across your own PPF account and any account you hold for a minor child, and the minimum is ₹500. Deposits above the limit do not earn interest and are not eligible for a tax deduction. Check the current rules on the India Post or National Savings Institute website.

Why should I deposit before 5 April?

PPF interest for each month is calculated on the lowest balance between the 5th and the last day of that month. A deposit made by the 5th of April earns interest for all twelve months of the financial year. A deposit made later in the year misses interest for the months before it.

What happens when my PPF account completes 15 years?

You can close the account and withdraw the full balance, or extend it in blocks of five years. During an extension you can continue depositing or stop, and the balance keeps earning interest either way. The form and deadline for choosing an extension with deposits are set by the scheme rules.

Is PPF interest really tax-free?

Yes, PPF interest and the maturity amount are exempt from income tax. Deposits qualify for a deduction under Section 80C within the ₹1.5 lakh limit, but only if you choose the old tax regime. Under the new regime, there is no 80C deduction, though the interest and maturity remain tax-free.

Is the PPF interest rate fixed for 15 years?

No. The government reviews small savings rates every quarter, and the rate in force applies to your whole balance for that period. The calculator uses one rate throughout, so treat its result as an estimate and try a slightly lower rate to see a cautious outcome.
