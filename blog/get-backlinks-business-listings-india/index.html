The honest way to build backlinks is to be listed where your customers already look and to earn links from websites that find your content genuinely useful. There is no shortcut that is both fast and safe. Bought links, link farms and automated directory blasts break Google's spam policies and can undo years of work.

The Backlink & Listing Helper on BISCS India is built around that white-hat approach. It gives you a checklist of legitimate places to list your business, with notes on why each one matters, and a set of polite, personal outreach email templates.

What actually counts as a good backlink?

A good link comes from a real website, on a relevant page, placed by a person who chose to link to you because it helps their readers. A link from your city's chamber of commerce member list, a supplier's partners page or an industry blog that cites your guide is worth far more than hundreds of links from unrelated, low-quality directories.

For local businesses, citations matter too. A citation is any mention of your business name, address and phone number, with or without a link. Consistent citations across trusted platforms help search engines confirm that your business is real and where it operates.

Writing outreach emails that people answer

The templates are deliberately short and polite, because that is what works. Some principles built into them:

  • Lead with their benefit. A broken link on their page is a problem you are helping them fix.
  • Be specific. Mention the exact page and why your resource suits their readers, for example "it explains GST rates for small shops in plain language, with a free calculator".
  • Respect their policy. The guest post template states that any link to you can carry whatever rel attribute their editorial policy requires.
  • Offer something real in return. For partners, a genuine testimonial or a listing on your own clients page.
  • Follow up once, then stop. If someone says no or does not reply, respect that.

Link building tactics to avoid

These are common offers in India's SEO market, and all of them carry risk:

  • Paid "high DA" backlink packages sold by the hundred.
  • Private blog networks, groups of sites built only to link to clients.
  • Automated submissions to hundreds of irrelevant directories.
  • Link exchanges at scale ("you link to me, I link to you" schemes).
  • Spammy comments and forum signatures dropped on unrelated sites.
  • Fake reviews on any platform. They break platform rules and consumer protection expectations, and destroy trust if discovered.

If you pay for a placement as advertising, the link should carry rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow". That keeps it within the rules.

Ideas that earn links naturally

The most durable links come from creating something worth referencing:

  • Local data or guides. A Pune property consultant publishing a clear guide to stamp duty and registration charges, kept up to date, is the kind of page others cite.
  • Free tools. A simple calculator or template relevant to your industry.
  • Real stories. A launch, a milestone or community work can earn coverage from local news and industry blogs.
  • Expert answers. Helpful answers in relevant communities, with links only where they truly add value.

Before you pitch any page, make sure it deserves the link. Run it through the On-Page SEO Checker and check that its share preview looks good with the Social Share Preview, since many people will first see your link in a chat or feed.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to buy backlinks?

No. Buying or selling links that pass ranking credit violates Google’s spam policies. It can lead to a manual action or simply be ignored by Google’s systems. Paid placements are fine for advertising only if the links carry rel="sponsored" or nofollow attributes.

Do nofollow links from directories help SEO?

Many profile and directory links are nofollow, so they pass little or no direct ranking value. Their real worth is visibility, consistent business details for local search, trust signals, and visitors who find you there. That is still valuable for a local business.

What is NAP consistency?

NAP stands for name, address and phone number. Using exactly the same details on your website, Google Business Profile and every listing helps search engines confirm your business is real and reduces confusion for customers. Small differences, such as old phone numbers, are worth fixing.

How many outreach emails should I send?

Quality matters far more than volume. Send personalised emails one at a time to sites that are genuinely relevant, and follow up at most once. Mass, templated emails tend to be ignored or marked as spam and can harm your reputation.
