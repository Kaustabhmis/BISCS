What the AI does for you

Making a passport photo by hand involves several fiddly steps. Here, they happen automatically:

  1. Face detection finds your face and the position of your eyes and mouth.
  2. Automatic framing crops to 35 × 45 mm with the head centred and sized to a set percentage of the photo height.
  3. Tilt check estimates whether your head is level and warns if it looks tilted.
  4. Background replacement separates you from the wall and fills the background with a plain colour.
  5. Photo check lists what looks right and what needs attention, including resolution for printing.
  6. Print sheet lays out 8 copies on 4 × 6 inch paper or 30 on A4, with cut lines.

How to make a passport photo step by step

  1. Take a photo facing the camera in good, even light, ideally against a light wall, with your shoulders visible.
  2. Open AI Passport Photo and choose the photo, drop it in or paste it with Ctrl + V.
  3. On first use, wait for the AI engine and models to download once (about 26 MB). Your browser keeps them for next time.
  4. Review the automatic crop. Use Head size to set how much of the height your head fills, and Move left / right or Move up / down to fine-tune. You can also drag the preview, use arrow keys, and press plus or minus to zoom. Re-centre resets the position.
  5. Keep Replace background on and choose White, Off-white, Light blue or Pale grey-blue. Adjust Edge softness if the outline looks harsh.
  6. Read the Photo check list beside the preview and fix anything marked.
  7. Download the single photo (JPG) for digital use, or the print sheet (JPG) on 4 × 6 inch or A4.

Indian passport photo requirements: what to check

The commonly stated format for Indian passport photographs is 35 × 45 mm, in colour, with a plain white background, a front-facing view of the full face, and a neutral expression with eyes open. For many Indian passport applications, the photograph is captured at the Passport Seva Kendra during the appointment, but printed photos are often needed for related forms, police verification paperwork, visas and other applications.

Requirements can be revised, and different authorities add their own details on head size, glasses, borders and how recent the photo must be. Before printing, check the current instructions on the official Passport Seva website or the embassy or authority you are applying to.

Photo checklist before you take the picture

  • Light: stand facing a window in daylight. Avoid a single overhead bulb that throws shadows under your eyes and nose.
  • Distance: ask someone to take the photo from about one to two metres away, then let the tool crop. Selfies at arm’s length distort the face.
  • Expression: neutral, mouth closed, both eyes open and looking straight at the camera.
  • Glasses: many authorities prefer none. If you wear them, make sure there is no glare.
  • Head coverings: generally not allowed except for religious reasons, with the full face visible.
  • Hair: keep it away from your eyes and face.
  • Clothing: avoid white tops against a white background; a mid or dark colour gives a clean outline.

Printing the sheet

Send the print sheet JPG to any photo shop for a standard 4 × 6 inch print, or print the A4 sheet at home on photo paper. In either case:

  • Print at 100% or Actual size, with scaling and fit-to-page turned off. The sheet is designed at 300 DPI.
  • Measure a printed photo; it should be 35 mm wide and 45 mm tall.
  • Cut along the grey lines with a paper trimmer or sharp scissors for straight edges.

Honest limits

  • AI edges are not perfect. Fine flyaway hair can look slightly soft after background replacement. A plain, light wall in the original photo makes the biggest difference.
  • Face detection needs a clear face. Very dark, blurry or angled photos may not be detected; the tool then lets you position the crop by hand.
  • Resolution matters. If your photo is too small for a 300 DPI print, the photo check warns you. Use your phone’s main camera at full resolution.
  • The tool does not guarantee acceptance. Officials make the final decision based on current rules.

Common mistakes

  • Smiling broadly or tilting the head. Both are common reasons for rejection.
  • Shadows on the wall behind you. Background replacement helps, but the shadow on your face itself remains.
  • Printing with fit to page, which shrinks every photo.
  • Uploading the print sheet to an online form. Forms need the single photo, usually with their own pixel and KB limits. Adjust it with Resize Image and Compress Image according to the official notice.

Frequently asked questions

Will a photo made this way be accepted for my Indian passport?

The tool helps you meet the common 35 × 45 mm, white-background format, but acceptance depends on the rules of the authority at the time you apply. For many Indian passport applications the photo is captured at the Passport Seva Kendra, so check the current Passport Seva guidelines before relying on a printed photo.

What head size does a passport photo need?

Many authorities ask for the head, from chin to the top of the hair, to fill roughly 70 to 80 percent of the photo height. The tool’s Head size slider sets this, and the photo check list warns you if you go outside that range. Always confirm the exact figure in the official instructions.

How well does the AI replace the background?

It works best with a photo taken against a reasonably plain wall in even light. Hair edges and loose strands may look slightly soft, and dark clothing against a dark wall can confuse the boundary. Check the edges carefully and adjust Edge softness if they look harsh.

How many photos fit on the print sheet?

The 4 × 6 inch sheet holds 8 photos and the A4 sheet holds 30, both laid out at 300 DPI with grey cut lines. Print at 100 percent or actual size so each photo measures exactly 35 × 45 mm.

Can I make a 2×2 inch US passport photo with this tool?

This tool is designed for the 35 × 45 mm format used in India, the UK, the Schengen area and many other places. For 2 × 2 inch photos, use the Passport Photo Maker, which offers that size, after preparing a white-background cut-out with the AI Background Remover.
