Formats and bitrates, simply explained

MP3 is the universal format, compatible with practically every player. M4A uses the AAC codec, which typically sounds a bit better than MP3 at the same bitrate. WAV is uncompressed: perfect quality, but around 10 MB per minute.

The bitrate, in kilobits per second, controls the trade-off between size and quality for MP3 and M4A. As a rough guide, 128 kbps is plenty for speech, 192 kbps is good for most music, and higher settings suit carefully recorded music. A higher bitrate cannot add quality that the microphone never captured, so a cheap laptop microphone will not sound better at 320 kbps.

Tips for a cleaner recording

  • Get close to the microphone. About a hand’s width from a headset or USB microphone gives clear speech with less room echo.
  • Pick a soft room. Curtains, beds and sofas absorb echo; bare walls and tiled floors reflect it. A wardrobe full of clothes makes a surprisingly good vocal booth.
  • Turn off fans and AC if you can. Noise suppression helps, but less noise to begin with always sounds better.
  • Watch the level meter. Aim for a healthy level that does not constantly hit the red. If it does, move back a little or speak more softly.
  • Record a ten-second test. Listen with headphones before recording something long.
  • Silence your phone. Notification sounds and vibration on the desk get recorded.

Common mistakes

  • Closing the tab before downloading. Takes live only in the browser tab. Download or export every take you want before you leave.
  • Leaving noise suppression on for music. It is tuned for voices and can make instruments and singing sound odd.
  • Exporting WAV to share. WAV files are large; use MP3 for sending on WhatsApp or by email.
  • Recording in the background on a phone. Some phones pause web pages when you switch apps or the screen locks.
  • Recording others without consent. Always ask before recording calls, lectures or conversations.

Frequently asked questions

Why can’t the recorder hear my microphone?

Most often the browser has not been given permission. Click the lock or microphone icon in the address bar and allow the microphone, then reload. Also check that the right input device is selected in your system sound settings, and that another app such as a video-call program is not holding the microphone.

Which format should I save my recording in?

MP3 is the best all-round choice: it plays on every phone, computer and messaging app. M4A (AAC) gives similar quality at a slightly smaller size and suits Apple devices. WAV is uncompressed, about 10 MB per minute, and is best only if you will edit the audio further in other software.

Should I turn off noise suppression?

Keep it on for speech in ordinary rooms, such as voice notes, interviews or narration, because it reduces fan hum and echo. Turn it off for music, singing or instrument practice, since the processing can make notes sound unnatural or cut out quiet passages.

Can I record on my phone?

Yes. Microphone recording works in modern mobile browsers on Android and iPhone. Keep the screen on and the tab in front while recording, because some phones pause web pages that go into the background. For long recordings, the phone’s built-in voice recorder app can be more reliable.

Is my recording uploaded or stored online?

No. The audio is captured and kept in your browser tab, and MP3 or M4A export runs on your device. Nothing is sent to a server. Recordings disappear when you close the tab, so download every take you want to keep before leaving the page.
