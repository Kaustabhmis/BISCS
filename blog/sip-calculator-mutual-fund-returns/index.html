SIP Calculator: How Much Will Your Monthly SIP Grow To?

Estimate the future value of a monthly mutual fund SIP, see the effect of an annual step-up and understand the formula, assumptions and limits behind SIP returns.

A SIP (systematic investment plan) calculator estimates what a fixed monthly investment could grow to if it earns a steady return. Each instalment compounds for the months it stays invested, so early instalments grow the most. At an assumed 12% a year, ₹10,000 a month for 10 years builds to about ₹23.2 lakh, from ₹12 lakh invested.

The free SIP Calculator shows this in seconds, along with a step-up option and a year-by-year table. The key word is "assumed": mutual fund returns are market-linked, so the result is a planning estimate, not a promise.

How does a SIP calculator work?

The BISCS tool adds your instalment at the start of each month and then grows the whole balance by one month's interest, which is the annual rate divided by 12. It repeats that for every month of the period. In formula terms, without a step-up, this equals:

FV = P × [((1 + i)^n − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)

where P is the monthly amount, i is the annual return ÷ 12 ÷ 100 and n is the number of months. The extra (1 + i) at the end reflects investing at the start of the month rather than the end.

One subtle point: compounding 1% a month (12% ÷ 12) is slightly more than 12% a year in effective terms, about 12.68%. This is the convention most SIP calculators use, but it is worth knowing when you compare with an annual figure from a fund factsheet.

Compare rows one and three. Both invest ₹12 lakh in total, but spreading it over 20 years at half the monthly amount roughly doubles the end value, because money has longer to compound. Doubling the SIP for 20 years (row four) takes the estimate close to ₹1 crore.

The step-up row shows a practical habit. If your salary rises each April, raising your SIP by 10% keeps your savings rate steady. In this example the monthly instalment reaches about ₹23,579 in the tenth year.

When is a SIP calculator useful?

  • Goal planning: working backwards from a target, such as a child's college fund, by adjusting the monthly amount until the estimate meets it.
  • Choosing a step-up: seeing whether a 5% or 10% yearly increase closes the gap to your goal.
  • Comparing with a lump sum: if you have a bonus to invest, compare it with the Lumpsum Calculator. Over the same period and return, a lump sum invested on day one earns more, but a SIP spreads your entry over time and fits a monthly income.
  • Checking a fund's past growth: use the CAGR Calculator to turn a starting and ending NAV into an annual growth rate, then test that rate here with caution.

How much SIP do you need for a goal?

The calculator projects forward, but you can use it backwards with a little trial and error. Say Neha in Hyderabad wants ₹50 lakh in 15 years for her daughter's higher education, and assumes 12% a year.

  1. Set the period to 15 years and the return to 12%.
  2. Start with a round figure, such as ₹10,000 a month. The estimate comes to about ₹50.46 lakh.
  3. Nudge the amount down until the estimate just meets the goal. The answer is a little under ₹9,910 a month.

Now repeat at 10%. The required SIP rises noticeably, which is the point of the exercise: a cautious return assumption tells you how much buffer to build in. If the higher amount is not affordable today, try a smaller SIP with a 5% or 10% yearly step-up instead. And remember that ₹50 lakh in 15 years is a nominal target; set it after adjusting today's cost for inflation.

Common SIP planning mistakes

  1. Using a high return because a fund did well recently. A few strong years can make 15% or more look normal. Test lower rates too.
  2. Ignoring inflation. ₹1 crore in 20 years will not buy what ₹1 crore buys today. Run your target through the Inflation Calculator first.
  3. Stopping SIPs in a falling market. The calculator's smooth growth hides that falls are when instalments buy more units. Stopping then can hurt long-term results.
  4. Forgetting costs and taxes. Expense ratios are already reflected in a fund's NAV, but exit loads and capital gains tax on redemption are not in this estimate.
  5. Mixing goals in one SIP. Separate SIPs for separate goals make it easier to choose suitable funds and timelines.

Honest limits of the estimate

The calculator assumes the same return every month, no missed instalments and no withdrawals. Real portfolios are bumpy, and the order of good and bad years matters, particularly near the end of a goal. It also does not know which fund you hold or its risk level.

So use it to understand scale and trade-offs, not to predict an exact figure. This article is general information and not investment advice. Read the scheme documents, check details on AMFI or your fund house's website, and consider a SEBI-registered investment adviser for decisions that matter to your family's future.

Frequently asked questions

Is the SIP calculator result guaranteed?

No. The calculator applies a steady assumed return every month, while real mutual fund returns move up and down with the market. Use the figure as a planning estimate, try a few different return rates, and remember that past performance of any fund does not promise future results.

What return rate should I assume for an equity SIP?

There is no correct number. Many people test a conservative, a moderate and an optimistic rate, such as 8%, 10% and 12%, to see a range of outcomes. For debt or hybrid funds, lower assumptions are more sensible. Avoid planning important goals around the most optimistic case.

What is a step-up SIP?

A step-up SIP increases the monthly instalment by a fixed percentage once a year, usually to keep pace with salary hikes. In the calculator, a 10% step-up turns a ₹10,000 SIP into about ₹23,579 a month by year ten, and the 10-year corpus rises from about ₹23.2 lakh to about ₹33.7 lakh at 12%.

Why does my fund statement show a different return from the calculator?

Statements usually report XIRR, which accounts for the exact dates and amounts of every instalment and the actual fund value. The calculator assumes identical monthly growth and investment at the start of each month. Missed instalments, different dates and market swings all make the two figures differ.

Are SIP returns taxable?

Yes, gains are taxed when you redeem units, under capital gains rules that depend on the type of fund and how long you held each instalment. Each SIP instalment has its own holding period. Rates and exemptions have changed in recent budgets, so check the current rules with the Income Tax Department or your adviser.
