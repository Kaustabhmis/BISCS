How to Split a Restaurant Bill and Tip Fairly Between Friends

Split any bill evenly with tip and tax, round each share for easy UPI payments, and avoid awkward maths at the table. Worked ₹ examples and fair-split tips.

How do you split a bill with a tip?

The method has three steps:

  1. Tip = bill × tip % ÷ 100.
  2. Total = bill + tip + any tax or charge not already on the bill.
  3. Each share = total ÷ number of people.

With the ₹2,450 example: tip = ₹245, total = ₹2,695, each share = ₹673.75.

Rounding matters in real life. If everyone pays ₹674, the group collects ₹2,696, which is ₹1 more than the total. Round up to the nearest ₹10 and everyone pays ₹680, collecting ₹2,720, so there is ₹25 extra. You can treat that as a slightly bigger tip.

Tipping and service charges in India

Tipping in India is customary in many sit-down restaurants but not compulsory. Many people leave around 5% to 10% for good service, though there is no fixed rule.

Service charge is different from a tip. Under guidelines from the Central Consumer Protection Authority issued in 2022, restaurants and hotels cannot add a service charge to the bill automatically or by default, and cannot refuse service if you decline it. If you see one, check the bill and ask for it to be removed if you do not wish to pay. If you do pay it, you probably do not need to tip again.

GST is normally printed on the bill already. Leave the tax field at zero in that case, or you will charge it twice.

How to split unevenly but fairly

Even splits are easy, but they are not always fair. Someone may have skipped dessert, or ordered an expensive drink. A simple approach:

  1. Subtract any individual items from the bill and note who had them.
  2. Use the calculator to split the shared amount and tip equally.
  3. Add each person's individual items to their share.

For a trip with many shared costs, such as a hotel, cabs and meals, it is often easiest to let one person pay and have everyone settle at the end with a single UPI transfer each.

Splitting other shared expenses

The same approach works well beyond restaurants:

  • Flatmates' utilities. Enter the electricity, internet or maid's salary as the bill, set the tip to none, and split by the number of flatmates.
  • Group gifts. Collecting for a colleague's farewell or a friend's wedding gift is simply total ÷ number of contributors. Round up to ₹50 or ₹100 to keep transfers simple, and use any extra for a card or flowers.
  • Cab and auto rides. Split the final fare, including tolls and parking, so nobody pays for the detour alone.
  • Trip bookings. For a shared homestay or hotel, split the room charges equally, then handle meals and activities separately if spending differs.

Common bill-splitting mistakes

  • Adding GST twice, once from the bill and again in the tax field.
  • Tipping on top of a service charge without noticing it is already there.
  • Rounding down, which leaves the payer short.
  • Forgetting delivery or packing charges on food orders.
  • Splitting by headcount when children ate little. Agree how to handle it before the bill arrives.

Tips for smooth group payments

  • Decide the tip percentage before you start dividing, so no one debates it later.
  • Round up to an amount that is easy to send, such as the nearest ₹10, and treat the extra as tip.
  • Share a screenshot of the result in the group chat, so everyone sees the same number.
  • If someone settles later, note it in the chat to avoid confusion.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay a service charge at restaurants in India?

Guidelines issued by the Central Consumer Protection Authority in 2022 say restaurants cannot add a service charge automatically or by default, and cannot make it a condition of service. Tipping is voluntary. If a service charge appears on your bill and you do not wish to pay it, you can ask for it to be removed.

Should the tip be calculated before or after GST?

Most people calculate the tip on the food and drink amount before tax, since GST goes to the government, not the staff. Either way is acceptable as long as the group agrees. In the calculator, enter the pre-tax amount as the bill and put the GST percentage in the tax field to tip on the pre-tax figure.

What does the extra collected amount mean when rounding?

When each share is rounded up, say to the nearest ₹10, the group pays slightly more than the bill. The calculator shows this difference as extra collected. You can leave it as an additional tip or have the person who paid return it.

Can the calculator split a bill unevenly?

It splits the total equally. For uneven bills, first subtract items that only one person had, split the shared amount equally with the tool, then add each person’s own items to their share. This keeps things fair without complicated maths.
