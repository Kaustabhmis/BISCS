Resize Video to 9:16, 1:1 or 4:5 for Instagram and YouTube

Change a video's aspect ratio for Reels, Shorts, the Instagram feed or YouTube. Compare crop, bars and blur fill, and pick 1080p, 720p or 480p output.

By BISCS India Editorial Team Updated 2 November 2026 4 min read
Quick answer

To resize a video for social media, choose the target shape, such as 9:16 for Reels or 4:5 for the feed, then decide whether to crop, add bars or fill the gaps with a blurred copy. BISCS India’s free Video Resizer for Social Media does all three in your browser and exports an MP4 at 1080p, 720p or 480p.

Every platform prefers its own shape. Reels, Shorts and WhatsApp Status fill a phone screen in 9:16, the Instagram and Facebook feeds give the most space to 4:5 or 1:1, and YouTube's regular player is 16:9. Converting between them means deciding what to do with the part of the picture that no longer fits.

The Video Resizer for Social Media gives you three answers to that question, crop, bars or blur, and shows a live preview before you export. The work happens on your own device.

Common shapes and where they go

Shape Output at 1080p Typical use
9:16 Vertical 1080 × 1920 Shorts, Reels, Status, Stories
1:1 Square 1080 × 1080 Instagram and Facebook feed
4:5 Portrait 1080 × 1350 Feed posts that take up more of the screen
16:9 Landscape 1920 × 1080 YouTube videos, Facebook, presentations
4:3 Classic 1440 × 1080 Older TVs, some slide decks

The Resolution setting (1080p, 720p or 480p) sets the shorter side, and the longer side follows from the shape. So 9:16 at 720p gives 720 × 1280. Platform recommendations change from time to time, so check the app's help pages if you need an exact current figure.

Crop, bars or blur: which should you pick?

Crop cuts the picture to fill the new shape completely. Nothing looks empty, but you lose the edges. Going from 16:9 to 9:16 removes roughly two thirds of the width, so it only works when the subject sits in a narrow area.

Fit with bars shrinks the whole frame to fit inside the new shape and fills the gaps with a solid Bar colour. Black is the default; a brand colour or white can look tidier. Nothing is lost, but on a vertical screen a landscape video becomes a small strip in the middle.

Blur fill also keeps the whole frame, but fills the gaps with a zoomed, blurred copy of the same video. This is the familiar look of many Reels made from landscape footage. It feels fuller than bars and avoids the harsh lines.

A rough guide:

  • One person talking in the centre: Crop.
  • Slides, screen recordings, text-heavy footage: Fit with bars or Blur fill.
  • Group photos, wide scenery, sport: Blur fill.
  • A brand video with strict colours: Fit with bars with your own colour.

How to use the Video Resizer

  1. Open the Video Resizer for Social Media and choose a video (MP4, MOV, WebM or MKV), or drag it onto the page.
  2. Under Target shape, pick 9:16 Vertical, 1:1 Square, 4:5 Portrait, 16:9 Landscape or 4:3 Classic. Landscape videos start with 9:16 selected, and vertical videos with 16:9.
  3. Under How to fit, choose Crop, Fit with bars or Blur fill.
  4. For Crop, drag the box on the preview or use the Crop position slider to keep the important part in frame. For bars, set the Bar colour.
  5. Pick a Resolution: 1080p, 720p or 480p. The line underneath shows the exact output size.
  6. Press Resize video and keep the tab open while it works.
  7. Preview the result, then download the MP4. Use Resize another to start again.

The output is an H.264 MP4 with AAC audio, which every major platform accepts. The video engine downloads once (about 32 MB) and your browser keeps it for next time; your file itself is never uploaded.

Picking a resolution

1080p is the usual choice for anything you plan to post. It is also the slowest to process, because every frame is re-encoded in the browser. On a phone or an older laptop, a long 1080p conversion can take a while, and 720p finishes noticeably faster. 480p is useful for quick previews or sending a draft to someone on a slow connection, but it will look soft when posted.

If file size matters more than shape, for example to fit a WhatsApp or email limit, use the Video Compressor after resizing.

Tips for a cleaner result

  • Shoot with the final shape in mind. If you know a clip will become vertical, keep the subject near the centre while recording.
  • Check text near the edges. Captions or lower-thirds burned into the original can be cut off by Crop. Switch to Blur fill if they matter.
  • Trim first. Resizing a ten-minute file to use thirty seconds wastes time. Cut the part you need with the Video Trimmer, then resize the short clip.
  • Do not upscale small videos. Turning a 480p clip into 1080p adds pixels but no detail. Choose a resolution close to the original.
  • Preview on a phone. A 4:5 post can look fine on a monitor but crowded on a small screen.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Cropping a moving subject. The crop position is fixed for the whole video. If the person walks across the frame, they will drift out. For clips like that, the Shorts & Reels Maker can follow faces instead.
  • Using bars for vertical platforms without thinking. Thick bars on a Reel leave most of the screen empty. Blur fill usually looks better.
  • Forgetting the original audio settings. The tool keeps the soundtrack as it is; it does not add or remove music.
  • Closing the tab mid-way. Processing stops if the page is closed or the phone locks for a long time.

Resizing whole videos versus making clips

This tool converts an entire video, from first frame to last, into a new shape. If your aim is to pull several vertical clips out of a long recording, with titles and highlight suggestions, the Shorts & Reels Maker is built for that job. For a single file that just needs a new shape, this resizer is quicker and has fewer settings to think about.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between resizing and changing aspect ratio?

Resizing changes the number of pixels, for example 1920×1080 down to 1280×720, while keeping the same shape. Changing aspect ratio changes the shape itself, such as wide 16:9 to tall 9:16. This tool always outputs the shape you pick, and the Resolution setting controls the pixel size of the shorter side.

Which mode keeps my whole picture?

Fit with bars and Blur fill both keep every part of the original frame. Bars fills the leftover space with a solid colour you choose, and Blur fill uses an enlarged, blurred copy of the same video instead. Crop is the only mode that removes part of the picture.

What happens if my video is already the shape I picked?

The tool notices and tells you that it will only be resized. For example, a 1920×1080 clip set to 16:9 at 720p simply becomes 1280×720. This is a quick way to make a smaller copy for sharing, though a dedicated compressor gives more control over file size.

Can I choose which part of the frame stays when cropping?

Yes. With How to fit set to Crop, a box appears on the preview showing what will remain. Drag it, or move the Crop position slider, to keep a person or object in view. The position is fixed for the whole video, so it suits shots where the subject stays roughly in one place.
