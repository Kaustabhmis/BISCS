Income Tax Calculator FY 2025-26: New vs Old Regime Compared

Compare new and old regime income tax for FY 2025-26 with slabs, the ₹75,000 standard deduction, 87A rebate up to ₹12 lakh, marginal relief and worked examples.

By BISCS India Editorial Team Updated 2 November 2026 4 min read
Quick answer

For FY 2025-26, the new regime taxes income above ₹4 lakh in slabs from 5% to 30%, gives salaried people a ₹75,000 standard deduction and a Section 87A rebate that makes tax nil up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income. For FY 2025-26 (assessment year 2026-27), most salaried people in India pay less tax under the new regime unless they claim large deductions. The new regime has wider slabs, a ₹75,000 standard deduction and a Section 87A rebate that makes tax nil when taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh. In practice, a salary of up to ₹12.75 lakh with no other income pays no tax.

The old regime has higher rates but allows deductions such as HRA, 80C, 80D and home loan interest. Which one is better depends on your numbers. The free Income Tax Calculator works out both and tells you which saves more.

What are the tax slabs for FY 2025-26?

Up to ₹2.5 lakh Nil Nil
₹2.5 lakh – ₹4 lakh Nil 5%
₹4 lakh – ₹5 lakh 5% 5%
₹5 lakh – ₹8 lakh 5% 20%
₹8 lakh – ₹10 lakh 10% 20%
₹10 lakh – ₹12 lakh 10% 30%
₹12 lakh – ₹16 lakh 15% 30%
₹16 lakh – ₹20 lakh 20% 30%
₹20 lakh – ₹24 lakh 25% 30%
Above ₹24 lakh 30% 30%

Under the old regime, the nil band is ₹3 lakh for those aged 60 to 79 and ₹5 lakh for those 80 and above. The new regime slabs are the same for all ages.

Other key figures used by the calculator:

  • Standard deduction: ₹75,000 (new) or ₹50,000 (old) for salaried people and pensioners.
  • Section 87A rebate: up to ₹60,000 if new-regime taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less; up to ₹12,500 if old-regime taxable income is ₹5 lakh or less.
  • Cess: 4% on tax plus surcharge.
  • Surcharge: 10% above ₹50 lakh, 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore, and 37% above ₹5 crore in the old regime (capped at 25% in the new regime).

Worked examples

1. Salary ₹12,75,000, no other income. New regime: 12,75,000 − 75,000 = ₹12,00,000 taxable. Slab tax is ₹20,000 (4–8 lakh at 5%) + ₹40,000 (8–12 lakh at 10%) = ₹60,000. The 87A rebate cancels it: tax nil. Old regime with ₹1.5 lakh 80C and ₹25,000 80D: taxable ₹10,50,000, tax ₹1,27,500 + ₹5,100 cess = ₹1,32,600.

2. Salary ₹13,00,000. New regime taxable income is ₹12,25,000. Slab tax is ₹63,750, but marginal relief caps it at the ₹25,000 earned above ₹12 lakh. With cess, tax is ₹26,000, not ₹66,300.

3. Salary ₹18,00,000 with heavy deductions. Suppose HRA exemption ₹2.4 lakh, home loan interest ₹2 lakh, 80C ₹1.5 lakh, 80D ₹50,000 and NPS ₹50,000.

  • New regime: taxable ₹17,25,000, tax ₹1,45,000 + cess = ₹1,50,800
  • Old regime: taxable ₹10,60,000, tax ₹1,30,500 + cess = ₹1,35,720

Here the old regime saves about ₹15,080. Drop the HRA, home loan and NPS, and the old regime tax rises to ₹2,96,400, so the new regime wins by a wide margin.

How do you decide between the regimes?

  • Add up deductions you will actually claim with proof, not ones you might make.
  • Your HRA exemption depends on rent, salary and city; work it out first in the HRA Exemption Calculator.
  • Compulsory contributions such as employee PF already count towards 80C, while voluntary ones like PPF top it up to the ₹1.5 lakh limit.
  • Re-run the comparison each year, because your salary and deductions change.

Common mistakes

  1. Assuming ₹12 lakh salary is the limit. The ₹12 lakh figure is taxable income; salaried people get the ₹75,000 deduction first.
  2. Forgetting other income. FD and savings interest are taxable and can push you over ₹12 lakh, triggering slab tax.
  3. Claiming old regime deductions under the new regime. Most, including 80C, 80D and HRA, are not available.
  4. Ignoring cess. Every rupee of tax carries 4% cess.
  5. Treating capital gains like salary. Special-rate income is outside this calculator and has its own rules.

What the calculator does not cover

The tool is built for resident individuals for FY 2025-26. It does not handle capital gains or other special-rate income, business income computations, employer NPS contributions, losses carried forward or marginal relief on surcharge. It also uses familiar section numbers from the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect from 1 April 2026 for later years, with new section numbering, so rules for FY 2026-27 onwards should be checked separately.

These results are estimates to help you plan and are not tax advice. Verify your figures on the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in), and speak to a chartered accountant if your situation is complex. Frequently asked questions

Is income up to ₹12.75 lakh tax-free under the new regime?

For a salaried resident individual with only salary income, yes in most cases. The ₹75,000 standard deduction brings ₹12.75 lakh down to ₹12 lakh of taxable income, and the Section 87A rebate of up to ₹60,000 cancels the slab tax. Special-rate income such as certain capital gains is treated differently, so check if you have any.

What is marginal relief under the new regime?

If taxable income is just above ₹12 lakh, the rebate is lost, which could make tax jump sharply. Marginal relief caps the tax so that it does not exceed the income above ₹12 lakh. At ₹12.25 lakh taxable, slab tax is ₹63,750 but relief limits it to ₹25,000, plus 4% cess.

When does the old regime save more tax?

The old regime tends to win when you have large deductions and exemptions, such as HRA, home loan interest, full 80C investments, health insurance and NPS. In one example in this guide, an ₹18 lakh salary with about ₹6.9 lakh of deductions pays about ₹15,000 less under the old regime.

Can I switch between the new and old regime every year?

Salaried individuals without business income can generally choose the regime each year when filing their return. People with business or professional income face stricter rules on switching back. Your employer will also ask for your choice for TDS purposes. Check the current rules on the Income Tax Department website before deciding.

Does the calculator include surcharge and cess?

Yes. It adds 4% health and education cess on all tax and applies surcharge at 10%, 15%, 25% or 37% above ₹50 lakh, ₹1 crore, ₹2 crore and ₹5 crore of taxable income, with the new regime capped at 25%. It does not apply marginal relief on surcharge, so very high incomes near a threshold may see a small difference.
