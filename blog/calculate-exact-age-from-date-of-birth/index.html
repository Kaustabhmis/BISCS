Calculate Exact Age from Date of Birth in Years, Months, Days

Find your exact age in years, months and days on any date, for forms, exams and admissions. Learn the method, leap-year rules and common mistakes to avoid.

By BISCS India Editorial Team Updated 2 November 2026 5 min read
Quick answer

To calculate exact age, count the completed years since the date of birth, then the completed months after the last birthday, then the remaining days. BISCS India's free Age Calculator does this for any “age as on” date, and also shows total days, weeks and a countdown to your next birthday.

Your exact age is the number of completed years since your date of birth, plus the completed months since your last birthday, plus the leftover days. Someone born on 15 August 1995 is 31 years, 2 months and 18 days old on 2 November 2026.

Doing this by hand is easy to get wrong, because months have different lengths and leap years add a day. The free Age Calculator handles both, and lets you choose any "age as on" date, which is what most Indian forms actually ask for.

Why "age as on" a date matters in India

Many forms do not ask how old you are today. They ask your age on a specific cut-off date:

  • Government recruitment notices usually fix a date, often the first day of a month in the exam year, and set minimum and maximum ages on that date.
  • School admissions set a minimum age for nursery or Class 1, measured on a cut-off date that varies by state and school board.
  • Insurance and pension forms may need your age on the policy or joining date.
  • Senior citizen benefits, such as certain bank deposit rates and income tax slabs, generally begin at age 60.

Being one day short of a limit can make an application invalid. That is why the calculator has a separate "Age on" field rather than always using today.

How exact age is calculated, step by step

Here is the method the tool follows, using a birth date of 15 August 1995 and a target date of 2 November 2026.

  1. Completed years. The last birthday before the target date is 15 August 2026. From 1995 to 2026 is 31 completed years.
  2. Completed months. From 15 August 2026, add months while you stay on or before 2 November: 15 September, 15 October. That is 2 months. Adding one more would reach 15 November, which is too far.
  3. Remaining days. From 15 October to 2 November is 18 days (16 days left in October, plus 2 in November).
  4. Result. 31 years, 2 months, 18 days.

For the totals, the tool counts every calendar day between the dates: 11,402 days in this example, which is 1,628 weeks and 6 days. The next birthday, 15 August 2027, is 286 days away.

The month-end problem

What if you were born on 31 January? There is no 31 February, so when counting months the calculator uses the last day of shorter months instead. This keeps the result sensible and matches how most people think about month-end birthdays.

Leap-day birthdays

If you were born on 29 February, your birthday only exists in leap years. In other years, the tool counts 28 February as your birthday and shows a note to explain this. Some institutions may use 1 March instead, so for legal documents follow the rule of the body that asked.

Common mistakes when calculating age

  • Subtracting only the years. 2026 − 1995 = 31, but if the birthday has not arrived yet this year, the person is still 30.
  • Using today instead of the cut-off date. For exam eligibility, the date in the notice is what counts.
  • Assuming every month has 30 days. This can throw off the day count by one or two days, which matters near age limits.
  • Ignoring leap years when converting age to days. A flat 365 per year undercounts.
  • Entering the date in the wrong format. If you type a date manually elsewhere, 05/08 can mean 5 August or 8 May. The date picker avoids this confusion.

Quick checklist before submitting a form

  • Does the date of birth match your official document, such as the Class 10 certificate, birth certificate or passport?
  • Is the "Age on" date exactly the one stated in the notice?
  • Have you noted the result as years, months and days if the form asks for all three?
  • If you are close to a limit, have you checked any age relaxation for your category?

Other ways people use an age calculator

Beyond forms, an age calculator is handy for:

  • Milestones. Find when you will be 10,000 days old, or how many weeks a baby has been born.
  • Family records. Work out grandparents' ages for insurance renewals or pension paperwork.
  • Pets and vehicles. The same maths gives the age of a car from its registration date, or a dog from its adoption day.
  • Event planning. Check which year someone turns 50 or 60 and plan a celebration ahead.

Is an online age calculator accurate?

A good one is exact, because it counts real calendar dates rather than approximating. The BISCS tool works in whole days using the calendar, so time zones and daylight saving cannot shift the answer. It does not consider the time of birth, so on your birthday itself the result shows exactly the new age from the start of the day.

For legal or official purposes, the age shown on your documents and the organisation's own rules always take priority. Use the calculator to check and plan, then confirm against the official notice. When you are ready, open the Age Calculator and get your answer in a couple of taps.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate age as on a specific date for a government exam?

Enter your date of birth and set the “Age on” field to the cut-off date given in the recruitment notice, not today’s date. The result in years, months and days is what you compare with the minimum and maximum age limits. Always read the notice for relaxation rules for reserved categories.

How is age counted for someone born on 29 February?

In non-leap years, the BISCS Age Calculator treats 28 February as the birthday, which is the convention most people follow. Legal treatment can differ between countries and institutions, so for official purposes check the rule used by the organisation asking for your age.

Why does my age in days not match years multiplied by 365?

Because of leap years. Every four years or so an extra day is added, so a 31-year span contains about seven or eight leap days. The calculator counts actual calendar days between the two dates, so its total is exact.

Does the calculator store my date of birth?

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser, and the dates you enter are not uploaded or saved on a server. Closing the page clears them, so you can safely use it for family members’ details too.
