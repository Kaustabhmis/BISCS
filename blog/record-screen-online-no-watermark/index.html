How to Record Your Screen Online Free (No Watermark)

Record your screen, a window or a browser tab with microphone and webcam, right in your browser. No install, no watermark, no upload. Tips for clear tutorials.

By BISCS India Editorial Team Updated 2 November 2026 4 min read
Quick answer

To record your screen online, open a browser-based screen recorder in Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a computer, choose whether to include your microphone and webcam, click Start, pick a screen, window or tab, and stop when done. Open Screen Recorder

You can record your screen without installing any software. Modern desktop browsers include a screen capture feature that websites can use with your permission, and the Screen Recorder on BISCS India builds on it: record your whole screen, one window or a single browser tab, add your voice and a webcam bubble, and download the video with no watermark.

It works best in Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a Windows, Mac or Linux computer. Safari on a Mac can also record. Phones and tablets are the exception: mobile browsers currently do not let websites capture the screen, so on a phone you should use its built-in screen recorder instead.

What can you record?

When you start a recording, your browser asks what to share. You usually get three choices:

  • Entire screen. Everything on one monitor, including notifications. Best for demonstrating work across several apps.
  • A window. Just one application, such as Excel, Tally or a code editor. Other windows and pop-ups stay private.
  • A browser tab. Just one tab. Ideal for walking through a website, a Google Sheet or an online form, and the most reliable way to include the page’s sound.

Typical uses include recording a how-to for colleagues, reporting a bug with steps, explaining an assignment to students, capturing an online class you are allowed to record, or sending a client a walkthrough of a website draft.

How to record your screen with BISCS India

  1. Open the Screen Recorder on a computer.
  2. Choose your Recording options:
    • Record system / tab sound to capture audio playing on the screen.
    • Record my microphone (voice-over) to narrate as you go. Echo cancellation and noise suppression are applied.
    • Show my webcam (picture-in-picture bubble), then pick the Bubble position (any corner), Bubble size and whether it should be a Round (circle) bubble.
    • 3-2-1 countdown before recording, so you have a moment to switch windows.
  3. Click Start recording. Allow camera and microphone access if you ticked those options.
  4. In the browser’s sharing dialog, choose a screen, window or tab. For tab sound, make sure the "share audio" option in that dialog is ticked.
  5. Record. Use Pause and Resume to skip the boring bits.
  6. Click Stop in the recorder tab, or the browser’s own Stop sharing button.
  7. Preview the result and download it. If the file is WebM and you need MP4, click Convert to MP4.

WebM or MP4: which do you need?

Browsers record using the formats they support natively. Most produce WebM, usually with the VP9 or VP8 video codec and Opus audio. Some newer browsers can produce MP4 directly.

A quick explanation of the terms: the container (WebM, MP4) is the file wrapper, and the codec (VP9, H.264) is how the pictures are actually compressed inside it. WebM plays well in Chrome, Firefox, Edge and VLC, and on YouTube. MP4 with H.264 is the safest choice for WhatsApp, PowerPoint, iPhones and older smart TVs.

The Convert to MP4 button re-encodes your recording to H.264 on your own computer. The first time, it downloads a video engine of about 32 MB, which your browser then caches. For other formats, or to pull out just the audio as MP3, use the Video Converter & MP3.

Tips for clear, professional recordings

  • Record a tab or window, not the whole screen, unless you need to switch apps. It avoids accidentally showing WhatsApp Web, email previews or other private notifications.
  • Turn on Do Not Disturb in Windows or macOS before you start, for the same reason.
  • Zoom the browser to 125% or more when recording web pages, so text is readable on a phone.
  • Use a headset microphone if you can. Laptop microphones pick up fan noise and room echo. Speak a little slower than normal.
  • Write three bullet points before you hit record. Short, planned videos are far more useful than long, wandering ones.
  • Keep the webcam bubble small and in a corner that does not cover buttons you will click.
  • Close heavy tabs and apps. Recording and encoding use your processor, and a slow laptop may drop frames if it is also running many other things.

After recording: trim, shrink and share

Most recordings have a few seconds of fumbling at the start and end. The Video Trimmer cuts those off without re-encoding, so it is fast and loses no quality.

Screen recordings can also be large, especially at high resolution. If you plan to send one on WhatsApp or by email, the Video Compressor has presets aimed at common app and email limits. Those limits change from time to time, so check the current limit in your app.

Common problems and fixes

  • "This browser does not let websites record the screen." You are probably on a phone or tablet, or using an older browser. Switch to a desktop browser.
  • No sound in the recording. Check that Record system / tab sound was ticked and that you ticked "share audio" in the browser’s dialog. If you shared a whole screen on a Mac, try sharing the specific tab instead.
  • Microphone or camera blocked. Click the camera or lock icon in the address bar and allow access, then try again. Make sure another app such as Zoom or Teams is not holding the camera.
  • Recording is shorter than expected. If you clicked Stop sharing or closed the shared window, recording ends automatically.
  • Choppy video. Lower your screen resolution, close other apps, or record a single tab instead of the whole screen.

Privacy

Your recording is captured and encoded by your own browser and stays in that tab until you download it. Nothing is uploaded, there is no account, and no watermark is added. Remember to respect other people’s privacy too: get consent before recording meetings or calls that include others.

Frequently asked questions

Can I record my screen on an Android phone or iPhone with this tool?

Generally no. Mobile browsers on Android, iPhone and iPad do not currently let websites capture the screen. Use your phone’s built-in screen recording feature instead, found in the quick settings panel on most Android phones and in Control Centre on iPhone. Then trim or compress the result with the BISCS India video tools.

Why is there no sound from my computer in the recording?

Capturing system or tab sound depends on your browser and operating system. In Chrome and Edge, sharing a browser tab and ticking the share audio option usually works. Sharing the entire screen with system audio is mainly available on Windows. Firefox and Safari may not offer screen audio at all. Your microphone is recorded separately.

Is there a time limit on recordings?

The tool sets no time limit. In practice, the recording is held in your browser’s memory until you download it, so very long recordings at high resolution need plenty of free RAM. For recordings longer than about an hour, it is safer to record in parts and keep other heavy tabs closed.

What format is the recording saved in?

Most browsers record in WebM, a modern open video format that plays in Chrome, Firefox, Edge and VLC. Some browsers can record MP4 directly. If you get WebM and need MP4 for WhatsApp, PowerPoint or an iPhone, click Convert to MP4, which runs on your device after a one-time engine download.

Does the recording get uploaded to your servers?

No. The browser captures the screen and encodes the video on your own computer. The file exists only in that browser tab until you download it. Nothing is sent to BISCS India or anyone else, which makes it suitable for recording internal dashboards or confidential documents.
