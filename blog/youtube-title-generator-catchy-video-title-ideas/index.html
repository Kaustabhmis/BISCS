YouTube Title Generator: Catchy Title Ideas That Fit Search

Get dozens of YouTube title ideas from your topic, check the length against the 100-character limit and learn how to write honest, clickable titles.

A strong YouTube title tells a viewer, in a glance, what the video is about and why it is worth their time. Put the main keyword near the start, keep the important part within about 60–70 characters, and make a promise the video actually keeps.

That sounds simple until you are staring at an empty title box after a long edit. The YouTube Title Generator gives you a page of ideas from your topic in a second, so you can pick, tweak and check one instead of starting from nothing.

What makes a YouTube title work

Viewers decide whether to click in a fraction of a second, usually while also looking at the thumbnail. Titles that do well tend to share a few traits:

  • The topic comes first. "Excel Pivot Tables for Beginners" is clearer than "Here's Something Every Office Worker Should Learn About Excel Pivot Tables".
  • There is a clear benefit or angle. A how-to, a list of mistakes, an honest review or a comparison each sets an expectation.
  • It matches the video. If the title promises ten tips, deliver ten tips. Viewers who feel tricked leave early, and YouTube's policies discourage misleading metadata.
  • It reads naturally. Write for a person scrolling on a phone, not for a keyword list.

The thumbnail and title work as a pair. Avoid repeating the exact thumbnail text in the title; use the title to add the detail the image cannot.

How to use the YouTube Title Generator

  1. Open the YouTube Title Generator and type your Topic or main keyword, for example "budget smartphone" or "Kolkata street food". A short noun phrase works best.
  2. Choose a Video type: Tutorial / how-to, Product review, Vlog, List / tips, Comparison (A vs B), News / update or Shorts.
  3. Optionally pick an Audience, such as Beginners, Students, Viewers in India or Small businesses. This adds phrases like "for Beginners" or "in India" to suitable titles.
  4. Adjust the Number (for list titles) if your video has, say, 5 tips rather than 7. Add a Year only if the video is tied to one.
  5. Fill in What will viewers achieve? with a verb phrase such as "edit videos faster" to get "How to …" titles, and Compare with if you are making an A vs B video.
  6. Tick Add Hinglish-friendly titles (Roman Hindi) if your audience searches that way. Leave Title Case my topic on unless your topic needs special capitalisation.
  7. Press Generate titles. Use Shuffle to see the list in a different order.

Each result shows its angle (How-to, List, Review, Curiosity and so on) and a length badge. Use the filter chips to see one angle at a time, star the ones you like, and then use Copy starred, Copy all, TXT or CSV to take them away. The Sort menu can show the shortest titles first or bring starred ones to the top.

Reading the length badges

YouTube accepts titles of up to 100 characters, but far fewer are visible in most places. The generator labels each idea so you can see this at once:

Badge Characters What it means
fits in search up to 60 Likely to show in full almost everywhere
near the search limit 61–70 Usually fine, but check the ending
may be cut off in search 71–100 Keep key words at the front
too long for YouTube over 100 YouTube will not accept it

Below the ideas, the Check your own title box counts characters as you type, shows a meter against the 100-character limit, and warns about lots of ALL-CAPS words, repeated "!!" or "??", and the characters < and >, which YouTube does not allow in titles.

Turning an idea into your final title

The generated titles come from templates, so treat them as drafts. A good routine:

  • Star five or six that fit your video, then read them aloud. Drop any that sound like a sales pitch.
  • Replace generic words with specifics from your video: a model name, a price, a place, a result.
  • Check that the first four or five words still make sense on their own, since that is all some viewers will see.
  • Make sure the title and thumbnail together answer "what is this and why should I watch?"

If you have two strong options, note both. You can change a title later in YouTube Studio, and some channels have access to YouTube's built-in title and thumbnail testing.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Clickbait that the video does not support. It may win a click but tends to lose the viewer quickly, which hurts more than it helps.
  • Stuffing several keywords in a row. "Excel Tutorial Excel Tips Excel for Beginners" reads like spam.
  • Burying the topic after a long hook. On narrow screens the hook is all anyone sees.
  • Adding a year to evergreen content. A 2024 label on a timeless tutorial makes it look stale in 2026.
  • Copying a viral title word for word. Borrow the structure, not the wording, and make it true to your own video.
  • Ignoring Shorts conventions. Shorts titles are often seen briefly while swiping, so short and direct usually works best there.

Tips for Indian creators

Many Indian viewers search in English, Hindi or a mix, sometimes in the same session. If your video is in Hindi or Hinglish, try a Roman Hindi title alongside an English one and see which feels more natural for your audience. Adding "in India" makes sense when prices, rules or availability are India-specific, such as a phone launch or a tax change, but it adds little to a general cooking or fitness video.

Regional creators can also use the audience and topic fields to mention a city or state, for example "Kolkata Street Food Tour" or "Darjeeling Weekend Trip on a Budget". Specific titles often face less competition than broad ones.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a YouTube title be?

YouTube allows up to 100 characters, but search results and suggested videos often show only around 60–70 characters, and phones may show fewer. Keep the main keyword and the promise inside that first stretch. Anything after it should be a bonus that still makes sense if it gets cut off.

Does the title generator use AI or YouTube search data?

No. The BISCS tool fills your topic, audience, number and goal into a curated set of title templates on your own device. It does not call any AI service or YouTube API, so it cannot tell you search volume. Treat the results as starting points to edit, not as ranked data.

Should I put the year in my YouTube title?

Only if the video is genuinely tied to that year, such as a budget update or a yearly buying guide. A year can make a title look current, but it also makes the video look outdated a few months later. The generator leaves the year out unless you fill in the optional Year field.

Can I write titles in Hinglish?

Yes. Tick “Add Hinglish-friendly titles (Roman Hindi)” and the generator adds patterns such as “kaise sikhe” and “ki puri jankari”. Many Indian viewers search in Roman Hindi, so a mixed title can match how your audience actually types, provided the video itself is in Hindi or Hinglish.

Is it bad to use ALL CAPS in a YouTube title?

One or two capitalised words for emphasis are fine. A title full of capitals tends to look like clickbait and is harder to read. The “Check your own title” box warns you when it finds several all-caps words, along with repeated punctuation like “!!”.
