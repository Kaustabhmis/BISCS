Rotating a photo turns it around its centre, usually by 90° or 180°, while flipping mirrors it from left to right or top to bottom. Together they fix the most common annoyances with phone pictures: sideways shots, upside-down scans, reversed selfies and slightly crooked horizons.

Why do photos end up sideways in the first place?

When you take a picture, your phone records which way it was held. Rather than rotating the pixels, many phones save them in the sensor’s natural orientation and add a small piece of metadata, the EXIF orientation tag, telling apps how to display it.

Your gallery app reads that tag and shows the photo correctly. But some websites, older desktop programs, document portals and print kiosks ignore it. The result: a photo that looked fine on your phone appears sideways after upload.

The fix is to rotate the photo and save a fresh copy, so the correct orientation is part of the image itself rather than a hint in its metadata.

How to rotate or flip an image

  1. Open Rotate & Flip Image and choose a photo, drag it in, or paste it with Ctrl + V.
  2. Use Rotate left or Rotate right to turn it by 90°, or Rotate 180° for upside-down images.
  3. Use Flip horizontal to mirror it left to right, or Flip vertical to mirror it top to bottom.
  4. If the horizon or a document edge is slightly tilted, drag the Straighten (fine angle) slider, which moves in half-degree steps between −45° and +45°.
  5. Check the output size shown under the preview. Choose the format and quality if you want something other than the original format.
  6. Press Reset to start again at any point, or download when you are happy.

Straightening a crooked photo the right way

A small tilt, even two or three degrees, makes buildings look like they are leaning and the sea look like it is draining off one side of the frame. To straighten:

  • Find a line that should be level or vertical: a horizon, a door frame, the edge of a table, the top of a document.
  • Move the slider until that line is parallel with the edge of the preview.
  • Because the picture has turned, the corners of the frame now contain empty space. Use Crop Image to trim those angled edges away.

Fine rotation resamples the pixels, so there is a very slight softening. For exact 90° turns and flips there is no such loss.

Practical uses in everyday life

  • Documents for upload. A photographed Aadhaar card, mark sheet or rent agreement that appears sideways on a portal looks careless and may be harder for the reviewer to read. Rotate it upright before converting it with JPG to PDF.
  • Scanned PDFs. If the problem is a page inside a PDF rather than an image, Rotate PDF fixes pages without converting them.
  • Mirror selfies. Your name on a T-shirt or a shop sign behind you reading backwards is a giveaway. One horizontal flip fixes it.
  • Product photography. Online sellers sometimes need left- and right-facing versions of the same product image for a layout. Flipping gives you both in seconds.
  • Old family photos. Scans of prints are often slightly crooked on the scanner bed. Straighten, crop and then improve them with the Photo Enhancer.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Flipping when you meant to rotate. A flipped portrait looks subtly wrong because faces are not perfectly symmetrical, and any text becomes mirrored. If you only wanted the photo upright, use rotate.
  • Rotating a photo that only looks wrong in one app. If it appears correctly in your gallery but sideways on one website, the website is ignoring the orientation tag. Saving a rotated copy here fixes it everywhere.
  • Straightening without cropping. The angled empty corners look odd in the final image.
  • Mirroring photos with logos or text for official use. Mirrored certificates and ID documents will not be accepted.
  • Repeated JPG saves. Rotate once, from the original, rather than saving and re-rotating a JPG many times.

Is it private?

Yes. All the turning, flipping and straightening happens on your device using your browser’s canvas. The image is not sent anywhere, so it is fine for personal photos and documents. On older phones, very large photos may take a moment longer to save, simply because the work is done locally.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my photo appear sideways after uploading it?

Phones often store the picture in one orientation and add a small EXIF tag telling apps to turn it. Some websites ignore that tag, so the photo shows sideways. Rotating it properly and saving a new copy bakes the correct orientation into the pixels themselves.

What is the difference between rotating and flipping?

Rotating turns the whole picture around its centre, like turning a printed photo on a table. Flipping mirrors it, so the left side becomes the right side or the top becomes the bottom. Text reads backwards after a horizontal flip, which is how you spot a mirrored selfie.

Why do my selfies look reversed and how do I fix it?

Many front cameras show a mirror image in the preview, and some also save it that way. Writing on clothes or signs then appears backwards. Use Flip horizontal once to mirror the photo back so it matches how other people see you.

Does rotating a photo lose quality?

Turning by exact 90 degree steps or flipping moves pixels without blending them, so the picture stays sharp. Small straightening angles require the pixels to be resampled, which softens the image very slightly. Saving as JPG also applies normal compression, so keep quality high.
