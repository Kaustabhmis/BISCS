YouTube Description Template: Chapters, Links and Hashtags

Write a YouTube description that hooks viewers in the first lines, adds valid chapters, links and hashtags, and stays under the 5,000-character limit.

By BISCS India Editorial Team Updated 2 November 2026 4 min read
Quick answer

A useful YouTube description opens with one or two sentences that explain the video, then adds chapters, links, credits and a few hashtags, all within YouTube’s 5,000-character limit. Most viewers read only the first line or two of a YouTube description, so that opening must explain the video on its own. Everything below it, such as chapters, links, credits and hashtags, is reference material for the people who expand the box and for YouTube's understanding of the video.

The free YouTube Description Generator puts those pieces in a sensible order for you. You fill in what you have, leave the rest blank, and copy a finished description that fits within YouTube's 5,000-character limit.

The anatomy of a good description

Think of a description in three layers.

The hook (first ~150 characters). This shows before the "…more" link and often in search results. Say what the viewer will learn or see, using the words they would search for.

The body. Key points, chapters with timestamps, and the links you mentioned in the video. This is where a viewer comes to find the product you reviewed or the template you promised.

The footer. Subscribe link, social profiles, credits for music or footage, business contact details and a handful of hashtags.

A description does not need to be long. A two-line summary with chapters and three links is better than 4,000 characters of repeated keywords.

Step-by-step: building a description

  1. Open the YouTube Description Generator and choose a Template style: Tutorial, Product review, Vlog or Business. Each one uses slightly different headings, such as "In this video:" for tutorials or "Links mentioned:" for reviews.
  2. Write the Video summary in one or two sentences. A character counter sits beside the label.
  3. Add Key points, one per line. They appear as bullet points.
  4. Enter Chapters / timestamps, one per line in the form 0:00 Intro. Press Example to see a sample set, or Tidy to clean up and sort what you pasted.
  5. In the Links card, add your Website, Subscribe / channel link, Instagram, Facebook, X (Twitter) and LinkedIn. Put product or resource links in Other links, one per line as Label | URL.
  6. If any links are affiliate links or the video is a paid promotion, tick the checkbox and review the Disclosure text.
  7. Under Extras, add Hashtags, edit the Call to action, paste any Credits / music attribution, and fill in a Contact email or Business details if you want enquiries.
  8. Watch the preview update on the right, then press Copy description or Download .txt.

Empty fields are simply skipped, so a vlog with no links still produces a tidy result.

Getting chapters right the first time

Chapters split the video timeline into labelled sections, which helps viewers jump to the part they need. YouTube only turns timestamps into chapters when they follow its rules, and the generator checks each one live:

  • The first timestamp is 0:00.
  • There are at least three timestamps.
  • They are in increasing order.
  • Each chapter is at least 10 seconds long.

A green tick appears next to each rule you meet and a cross next to any you miss, with the name of a chapter that is too short. If a line cannot be read, the tool tells you its line number. Formats like 1:30 Setup, Setup - 1:30 and pasted spreadsheet rows all work. Keep chapter titles short and descriptive, such as "Installing the app" rather than "Part 2".

If you want to plan chapters separately, the YouTube Chapters Generator is built just for that.

What the warnings mean

Under the preview, the tool flags common problems before you paste:

  • Over 5,000 characters, with the number to remove.
  • The characters < and >, which YouTube does not accept in descriptions.
  • More than 15 hashtags, as a gentle nudge, and more than 60, because YouTube then ignores every hashtag on the video.
  • Links without https://, which may not become clickable.
  • An invalid-looking contact email or affiliate links with no disclosure text.

The meter turns amber as you approach the limit and red when you pass it.

Habits that make descriptions more useful

  • Write the summary for a human first. If it reads naturally and includes the topic, it is doing its job.
  • Repeat the most important link near the top for tutorials that depend on a download or a form.
  • Use the same footer on every video so regular viewers know where to look.
  • Credit music and footage exactly as the licence asks. A CC BY track, for instance, usually needs the title, artist, licence and a link.
  • For business channels, the Business template puts contact details and highlights first, which suits service videos aimed at local customers.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Starting with "Hi guys, welcome back". It wastes the only lines most people see.
  • Keyword stuffing. Long lists of unrelated search terms can break YouTube's spam policies.
  • Dead or untracked links. Check each one before publishing; for campaign links, add UTM parameters with the UTM Link Builder so you can see what YouTube traffic does on your site.
  • Forgetting the disclosure. Affiliate and sponsored content should be clearly marked, both in the text and in YouTube Studio where it applies.
  • Hashtags that do not match the video. They confuse viewers and can count as misleading metadata.

Frequently asked questions

What should go in the first two lines of a YouTube description?

A plain summary of what the video covers, with your main keyword used naturally. Only around the first 150 characters appear before “…more” and in many search results, so links, greetings and hashtags should come later. The generator highlights this opening stretch in the preview so you can see exactly what shows.

Why are my YouTube chapters not showing?

Usually one rule is broken: the first timestamp must be 0:00, there must be at least three timestamps in increasing order, and each chapter must last at least 10 seconds. The generator runs these four checks live. YouTube can also turn chapters off for some videos, and you can disable automatic chapters in Studio.

How do I disclose affiliate links in a description?

Tick the affiliate box in the Links section and the generator adds a short disclosure line, which you can edit. If the video itself is sponsored, also tick the paid promotion option in YouTube Studio, because a description note alone does not replace YouTube’s own disclosure setting.

Can I paste chapters from Excel or Google Sheets?

Yes. Copy two columns, time and title, and paste them into the Chapters / timestamps box. Press Tidy and the tool reformats each row as “0:00 Title”, sorts them by time, and flags any line it cannot read.

Do hashtags in the description matter?

YouTube can show the first three hashtags from your description above the title, which gives viewers a quick way to find related videos. They are a small extra, not a ranking lever. Keep to a few relevant ones, because if a video has more than 60, YouTube ignores all of them.
