Roman Numerals Explained: Rules, Chart and Easy Conversion

Learn how Roman numerals work, the subtraction rule, and how to convert years like 1947 and 2026. Includes a symbol chart, examples and mistakes to avoid.

By BISCS India Editorial Team Updated 2 November 2026 5 min read
Quick answer

Roman numerals use seven letters, I (1), V (5), X (10), L (50), C (100), D (500) and M (1,000), added together from largest to smallest, with a smaller letter before a larger one meaning subtraction. Roman numerals write numbers with seven letters: I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100, D = 500, M = 1,000. You read them from left to right and add the values, except when a smaller letter comes before a larger one, which means subtract. So VI is 6, IV is 4, and 2026 is MMXXVI.

Where do we still use Roman numerals?

Roman numerals are far from dead. In India you see them every day:

  • School classes such as Class VIII, Class X and Class XII.
  • Sections and parts of documents, like Part III of the Constitution of India on Fundamental Rights.
  • Preface pages of books, numbered i, ii, iii before the main chapters begin.
  • Clock and watch faces, often with IIII instead of IV for design reasons.
  • Names of kings and popes, sequels and event editions.
  • Years on monuments, film credits, wedding invitations and tattoos.

The Roman numeral chart

Symbol Value Subtractive pair Value
I 1 IV 4
V 5 IX 9
X 10 XL 40
L 50 XC 90
C 100 CD 400
D 500 CM 900
M 1,000

Those six subtractive pairs are the only ones allowed in standard form. Learn them and you can write any number up to 3,999.

The rules in plain language

  1. Write from largest to smallest. MDC is 1,000 + 500 + 100 = 1,600.
  2. Repeat a symbol up to three times to add it: III is 3, XXX is 30, CCC is 300.
  3. Never repeat V, L or D. VV is not 10; use X.
  4. Subtract only with the six pairs above. I before V or X, X before L or C, C before D or M.
  5. Only one smaller symbol can come before a larger one. 8 is VIII, not IIX.

Worked examples: converting years and numbers

1947 to Roman numerals

Split it by place value: 1000 + 900 + 40 + 7.

  • 1000 = M
  • 900 = CM
  • 40 = XL
  • 7 = VII

Result: MCMXLVII.

2026 to Roman numerals

2000 + 20 + 6 = MM + XX + VI = MMXXVI.

1999, the classic trap

It is tempting to write MIM (1000 − 1 + 1000), but I can only be placed before V or X. The standard form is 1000 + 900 + 90 + 9 = M + CM + XC + IX = MCMXCIX.

Reading MDCCCLVII

M (1000) + D (500) + CCC (300) + L (50) + V (5) + II (2) = 1857.

More quick examples

  • 14 = XIV
  • 49 = XLIX (not IL)
  • 90 = XC
  • 444 = CDXLIV
  • 3,999 = MMMCMXCIX, the largest standard numeral

How to read any Roman numeral, step by step

Converting from Roman numerals back to a normal number is just as mechanical:

  1. Write down the value of each letter underneath it. For MCMXC: 1000, 100, 1000, 10, 100.
  2. Move from left to right. If a value is smaller than the one after it, subtract it; otherwise add it.
  3. Here, M adds 1000. C is smaller than the next M, so it subtracts 100. M adds 1000. X is smaller than the next C, so it subtracts 10. C adds 100.
  4. Total: 1000 − 100 + 1000 − 10 + 100 = 1990.

With practice you start to see pairs like CM and XC as single chunks, which makes reading much faster.

Handy reference: tens and hundreds

Most numerals are built from these three ladders, so they are worth memorising:

  • Units: I, II, III, IV, V, VI, VII, VIII, IX
  • Tens: X, XX, XXX, XL, L, LX, LXX, LXXX, XC
  • Hundreds: C, CC, CCC, CD, D, DC, DCC, DCCC, CM

To write any number up to 3,999, take the thousands as M, MM or MMM, then pick one item from each ladder. For 2,748, that is MM + DCC + XL + VIII = MMDCCXLVIII.

Common Roman numeral mistakes

  • Using IL for 49 or IC for 99. The correct forms are XLIX and XCIX.
  • Writing IIII for 4 outside of clock faces. In text, use IV.
  • Repeating V, L or D, such as VV or LL.
  • Putting two smaller symbols before a larger one, such as IIX for 8.
  • Trying to write zero or negative numbers. The Roman system has no zero.
  • Mixing up D and M, which is easy when reading quickly on a building or certificate.

Tips for designers and writers

  • For tattoos, invitations and engravings, convert with a tool and double-check the result before it becomes permanent.
  • Use a serif font for a classic look, and keep letter spacing even so each symbol is clear.
  • When numbering sections, be consistent: either Roman throughout (I, II, III) or Arabic (1, 2, 3).
  • For dates, you can write each part separately, such as XV · VIII · MCMXLVII for 15 August 1947.

Why the Roman system faded

Roman numerals are fine for labels but awkward for arithmetic. Try multiplying XLVII by XIX without converting first. The decimal place-value system with zero, which developed in India and later spread through the Arab world to Europe, made calculation far easier, and it is the system the world uses today.

Frequently asked questions

How do you write 2026 in Roman numerals?

2026 is MMXXVI. Break it into 2000 + 20 + 6. Two thousand is MM, twenty is XX and six is VI. Put them together from largest to smallest to get MMXXVI.

Why is 4 written as IV and not IIII?

Standard Roman numerals use subtractive notation: placing I before V means 5 minus 1. It keeps numbers shorter and avoids repeating a symbol four times. Some clock faces still show IIII for four, which is a long-standing design tradition, but IV is the standard form.

Is there a Roman numeral for zero?

No. The Roman system had no symbol for zero and no way to write negative numbers. That is why the converter accepts whole numbers from 1 upwards. Zero as a number with its own symbol came from the Indian number system.

Why does the converter stop at 3,999?

With the standard seven symbols and the rule that a symbol repeats at most three times, the largest number you can write is MMMCMXCIX, which is 3,999. Larger values historically used a bar over a letter to multiply it by 1,000, but there is no single standard for that.

Why is MIM not accepted for 1999?

Standard subtraction only allows I before V and X, X before L and C, and C before D and M. So 1999 must be written as 1000 + 900 + 90 + 9, which is MCMXCIX. Shortcuts like MIM or IL for 49 are not standard and the converter flags them.
