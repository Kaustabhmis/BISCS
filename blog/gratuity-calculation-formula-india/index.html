Gratuity Calculator: The 15/26 Formula, Eligibility and Tax

Calculate gratuity under the Payment of Gratuity Act with the 15/26 formula, see how part-years are rounded, who is eligible and how the ₹20 lakh tax exemption works.

By BISCS India Editorial Team Updated 2 November 2026 5 min read
Quick answer

For employees covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, gratuity = 15 × last drawn monthly basic plus DA × years of service ÷ 26, with a final part-year of six months or more counted as a full year. ₹60,000 a month for 10 years 7 months gives about ₹3.81 lakh. Gratuity is a lump sum an employer pays when you leave after long service, as a thank-you for the years you worked there. If your employer is covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, the formula is:

Gratuity = 15 × last drawn monthly (basic + DA) × years of service ÷ 26

A final part-year of six months or more counts as a full year. So someone earning ₹60,000 a month in basic plus DA who leaves after 10 years and 7 months is credited with 11 years, and their gratuity is about ₹3,80,769. The free Gratuity Calculator does this sum and shows how much is tax-exempt.

Why 15 and 26?

The Act grants 15 days' wages for every completed year of service. It treats a month as 26 working days. So one year's gratuity equals 15 ÷ 26 of a month's basic plus DA, about 57.7% of it.

If your employer is not covered by the Act, gratuity is often calculated with half a month's salary per completed year, based on a 30-day month (15 ÷ 30), and part-years are ignored. That gives a lower figure, as the examples below show.

Worked examples

Situation Years counted Calculation Gratuity
Covered, ₹60,000, 10 years 7 months 11 15 × 60,000 × 11 ÷ 26 ₹3,80,769
Covered, ₹60,000, 10 years 5 months 10 15 × 60,000 × 10 ÷ 26 ₹3,46,154
Not covered, ₹60,000, 10 years 7 months 10 15 × 60,000 × 10 ÷ 30 ₹3,00,000
Covered, ₹1,50,000, 25 years 25 15 × 1,50,000 × 25 ÷ 26 ₹21,63,462

The first two rows show why timing matters. Leaving at 10 years 7 months instead of 10 years 5 months adds a full year, about ₹34,600 here. If you are close to a six-month mark, it may be worth knowing before you hand in your notice.

For a feel of the scale, here is gratuity after exactly 20 years of service with a covered employer at different levels of basic plus DA: ₹30,000 gives about ₹3,46,154; ₹50,000 gives about ₹5,76,923; and ₹1,00,000 gives about ₹11,53,846. Gratuity rises in direct proportion to both salary and years, so a pay rise late in your career lifts the whole amount, because the last drawn salary is used for every year of service.

Who is eligible for gratuity?

  • You must normally complete five years of continuous service with the same employer.
  • The five-year condition does not apply on death or disablement; gratuity is then paid to you or your nominee.
  • Some court rulings have treated four years and 240 days as five years, especially where the employer works a six-day week, but this is not automatic.
  • The Act applies to factories, mines, plantations, shops and establishments with ten or more employees on any day in the previous twelve months.

The Code on Social Security, 2020 consolidates the Gratuity Act with other labour laws and brings changes such as gratuity for fixed-term employees after one year. Check how its provisions apply to your job on the Ministry of Labour and Employment's website.

How is gratuity taxed?

For private-sector employees, gratuity is tax-free up to ₹20 lakh across their whole career, and the calculator applies this limit. Anything above it is taxed as salary income in the year you receive it. Gratuity received by central and state government employees is fully exempt.

In the last example in the table, ₹20 lakh is exempt and about ₹1,63,462 would be taxable. Note too that the Act caps the gratuity an employer is legally required to pay at ₹20 lakh, although some employers pay more under their own schemes. To see how a taxable portion affects your overall tax, try the Income Tax Calculator.

Common gratuity mistakes

  1. Using CTC or gross salary. Only basic plus DA counts. A ₹1 lakh gross salary might have only ₹40,000 basic.
  2. Rounding up every part-year. Only six months or more rounds up, and only for covered employers.
  3. Forgetting the nominee form. Keep your gratuity nomination up to date so your family can claim without delay.
  4. Assuming gratuity is in your PF. It is a separate benefit, paid by the employer.
  5. Not following up. The employer must pay within a set time after it becomes payable; delays can attract interest under the Act.

Tips before you resign or retire

  • Check your appointment letter and HR policy to see whether your employer follows the Act or a more generous scheme.
  • Look at your last few salary slips to confirm basic and DA.
  • Plan what to do with the lump sum. The Lumpsum Calculator shows how it might grow if invested.

A word of caution

This calculator follows the standard statutory formula and the ₹20 lakh tax exemption, but it does not know your employer's policy, any disputes over continuity of service, or how labour-code changes apply to your role. Treat the result as an estimate and general information, not legal or tax advice. For an exact figure, ask your HR or payroll team for their calculation, and check tax treatment with the Income Tax Department or a qualified professional.

Frequently asked questions

Is gratuity paid if I resign before 5 years?

Normally gratuity becomes payable only after five years of continuous service, except on death or disablement, where the five-year condition does not apply. Some court rulings have treated four years and 240 working days as meeting the requirement. Changes under the newer labour codes may also affect certain categories, so check your case.

Why is the formula divided by 26?

The Act treats a month as 26 working days, excluding four Sundays. Fifteen days' wages are therefore 15 ÷ 26 of a monthly salary. For employers not covered by the Act, a 30-day month is commonly used, which gives a smaller amount, and only completed years are counted.

Which salary is used to calculate gratuity?

The last drawn monthly basic salary plus dearness allowance. Other allowances such as HRA, special allowance, bonus and overtime are not included. If your basic is a small share of your total pay, your gratuity will be correspondingly lower than a calculation on gross salary would suggest.

How much gratuity is tax-free?

For private-sector employees, gratuity is exempt up to ₹20 lakh over their working life, subject to the formula limits under the Income-tax law. Any excess is taxed as salary. Gratuity received by central and state government employees is fully exempt. Check the current limit with the Income Tax Department before planning around it.

Does the employer pay gratuity or is it deducted from my salary?

Gratuity is a benefit paid by the employer and is not deducted from your salary. Some companies show it as part of cost to company (CTC), which is why your gross salary is lower than CTC. Many employers fund it through a group gratuity policy with an insurer.
