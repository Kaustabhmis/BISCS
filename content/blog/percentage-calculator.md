Percentages answer one question: how big is one number compared with another, if the whole is 100? To find X% of a number, multiply the number by X and divide by 100. To find what percent one number is of another, divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100.

That covers most everyday needs, from exam marks and salary hikes to sale discounts and GST. The rest of this guide shows the formulas for each situation, with worked examples you can check in the free [Percentage Calculator](/tools/percentage-calculator/).

## What are the basic percentage formulas?

Almost every percentage problem is a rearrangement of one relationship: **part = whole × percent ÷ 100**. Knowing which of the three values you are missing tells you which formula to use.

| What you want | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| X% of a number | number × X ÷ 100 | 15% of 2,400 = 360 |
| What % is A of B | A ÷ B × 100 | 360 of 1,200 = 30% |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 | 50,000 to 65,000 = +30% |
| Increase by X% | value × (1 + X ÷ 100) | 1,000 + 18% = 1,180 |
| Decrease by X% | value × (1 − X ÷ 100) | 2,999 − 20% = 2,399.20 |
| A is P% of what | A ÷ P × 100 | 45 is 30% of 150 |
| Percentage difference | difference ÷ average × 100 | 80 and 120 = 40% |

The last row trips people up. Percentage *difference* compares two values with no "before" or "after", so it divides by their average. Percentage *change* has a clear starting point and divides by that.

## Worked examples from everyday life in India

### Salary hike

Your monthly salary is ₹42,000 and you are offered an 8% increment. 8% of 42,000 is 3,360, so the new salary is ₹45,360. In the tool, use "Increase or decrease by %" with 42,000 and 8.

### Exam marks

You scored 437 out of 500. 437 ÷ 500 × 100 = 87.4%. Use "What percent is X of Y" with 437 and 500. Some universities convert CGPA to percentage using their own multiplier, so check your institution's rule for official forms.

### Sale discount

A kurta priced at ₹2,999 is on 20% off. The discount is ₹599.80 and you pay ₹2,399.20. A bill shows ₹1,180 including 18% GST and you want the pre-tax price. The final amount is 118% of the base, so the base is 1,180 ÷ 118 × 100 = ₹1,000. Use the "X is P% of what?" card with 1,180 and 118. For full CGST and SGST splits, the [GST Calculator](/tools/gst-calculator/) is quicker.

## Common percentage mistakes to avoid

- **Using the wrong base.** A fall from 120 to 80 is −33.33%, but a rise from 80 to 120 is +50%. Always divide by the starting value.
- **Assuming rises and falls cancel.** ₹1,000 up 50% is ₹1,500; down 50% from there is ₹750, not ₹1,000.
- **Adding stacked discounts.** "20% + 10% off" is a 28% total discount, because the second discount applies to a smaller price.
- **Mixing up percent and percentage points.** A home loan rate going from 6.5% to 7% is a rise of 0.5 percentage points, which is about 7.7% in relative terms.
- **Removing GST by subtracting 18%.** Taking 18% off ₹1,180 gives ₹967.60, which is wrong. Divide by 1.18 instead to get ₹1,000.
- **Confusing profit margin and markup.** A product bought at ₹800 and sold at ₹1,000 has a 25% markup on cost but a 20% margin on the selling price. The [Profit Margin Calculator](/tools/profit-margin-calculator/) shows both.

## Mental maths shortcuts worth knowing

You can do many percentages in your head with a few tricks:

- **10%** is the number divided by 10. 10% of 3,450 is 345.
- **5%** is half of 10%. So 5% of 3,450 is 172.50.
- **1%** is the number divided by 100. Build other rates from it: 18% is 10% + 5% + 3 × 1%.
- **Swap the numbers.** X% of Y equals Y% of X. 8% of 50 is the same as 50% of 8, which is 4.
- **25% and 50%** are a quarter and a half, and 20% is a fifth.

These are handy for quick checks at a shop counter. For anything that matters, such as an invoice, a loan comparison or official marks, confirm the figure with a calculator.

## When should you use a percentage calculator?

Mental maths is fine for round numbers. A calculator earns its place when:

- the numbers have decimals or are large, such as ₹18,47,250;
- you need to work backwards, such as finding the original price;
- you are comparing two values and need to know which to divide by;
- you want a quick check before sending a quotation, salary letter or report.

## Tips for accurate results

- Keep full precision until the final step. Rounding 33.333% to 33% early can shift a large total noticeably.
- When reporting change, say what it is relative to: "sales rose 30% compared with April".
- For money, round only the final amount to two decimal places.
- If the starting value is zero, percentage change is undefined. The tool warns you instead of showing a misleading figure.
