To write a number in words in the Indian system, read the digits in groups of thousands, lakhs and crores. **12,34,567** is *Twelve Lakh Thirty Four Thousand Five Hundred Sixty Seven*. In the international system, the same number is *One Million Two Hundred Thirty Four Thousand Five Hundred Sixty Seven*.

Both are correct; they simply group digits differently. The free [Number to Words](/tools/number-to-words/) converter shows both at once, and adds a cheque-ready line such as "Rupees Twelve Lakh Thirty Four Thousand Five Hundred Sixty Seven and Fifty Paise Only".

## Indian vs international number system

The difference is all in how commas are placed after the first thousand.

- **Indian system:** the first group has three digits, then every group after it has two. 1,00,000 is one lakh, 1,00,00,000 is one crore.
- **International system:** every group has three digits. 100,000 is one hundred thousand, 10,000,000 is ten million.

| Number | Indian grouping | Indian words | International words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,00,000 | 1,00,000 | One Lakh | One Hundred Thousand |
| 10,00,000 | 10,00,000 | Ten Lakh | One Million |
| 1,50,00,000 | 1,50,00,000 | One Crore Fifty Lakh | Fifteen Million |
| 25,00,00,000 | 25,00,00,000 | Twenty Five Crore | Two Hundred Fifty Million |
| 1,00,00,00,000 | 1,00,00,00,000 | One Hundred Crore | One Billion |
| 10,00,00,00,00,000 | 10,00,00,00,00,000 | One Lakh Crore | One Trillion |

Two handy rules: **10 lakh = 1 million**, and **100 crore = 1 billion**. Large government figures such as budgets are often quoted in "lakh crore", which is the same as trillions.

## How to read a big number in words, step by step

If you ever need to do it without a tool, this method works every time for the Indian system:

1. **Add Indian commas.** From the right, mark off three digits, then every two digits: 98765432 becomes 9,87,65,432.
2. **Name each group from the left.** The groups are crore, lakh, thousand and the last three digits (hundreds).
3. **Read each group as a small number.** 9 crore, 87 lakh, 65 thousand, 432.
4. **Join them up.** Nine Crore Eighty Seven Lakh Sixty Five Thousand Four Hundred Thirty Two.

For the international system, mark off groups of three: 98,765,432 is Ninety Eight Million Seven Hundred Sixty Five Thousand Four Hundred Thirty Two.

Skip any group that is all zeros. 7,05,000 has no hundreds, so it is simply *Seven Lakh Five Thousand*. Beyond 99 crore, Indian usage carries on with the crore unit, so 1,00,00,00,000 is *One Hundred Crore*, and very large figures are often expressed as *lakh crore*. Older words such as arab and kharab exist but are rarely used in banking or official writing today.

## How to write the amount on a cheque in India

Writing a cheque is one of the most common reasons people need numbers in words. A few conventions keep your cheque clear and harder to tamper with.

### The amount in words

- Start writing right after the printed word "Rupees", with no gap.
- Write the amount in words, for example *Seven Lakh Five Thousand*.
- End with **"Only"**, so nothing can be added after it.
- Draw a line through any unused space on the line.
- If there are paise, write them as *and Fifty Paise Only*.

### The amount in figures

- Write close to the ₹ symbol, using Indian commas: **7,05,000/-**. The "/-" shows there are no paise.
- If there are paise, write them after a decimal point, such as **2,450.75**.
- Do not leave space before the first digit.

### Worked example

For ₹7,05,000:

- Words: **Rupees Seven Lakh Five Thousand Only**
- Figures: **7,05,000/-**

For ₹12,34,567.50:

- Words: **Rupees Twelve Lakh Thirty Four Thousand Five Hundred Sixty Seven and Fifty Paise Only**
- Figures: **12,34,567.50**

## Common mistakes when writing numbers in words

- **Mixing the systems,** such as "Twelve Lakh Thirty Four Thousand" in one place and "1.23 million" in another on the same document.
- **Writing "Lakhs" or "Crores"** in the amount. The usual style is singular: *Five Lakh*, not *Five Lakhs*.
- **Leaving gaps** before or after the amount on a cheque.
- **Words and figures not matching.** The bank may return a cheque if they differ, so double-check both.
- **Overwriting a mistake.** Cheques cleared under the CTS-2010 standard do not accept alterations to most details. Cancel and write a new cheque.
- **Misplacing commas** in figures: 1,234,567 (international) and 12,34,567 (Indian) are the same number, but mixing styles confuses readers.
