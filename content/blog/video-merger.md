Merging videos sounds like sticking files end to end, but clips from different phones, apps and cameras rarely match. One is vertical, one is landscape; one runs at 30 frames per second, another at 25; one has no sound. A reliable merger first makes them all the same, then joins them.

The [Video Merger](/tools/video-merger/) does both in your browser. Add two or more clips, arrange them, choose an output size, and get one MP4 back. Nothing leaves your device.

## When you might merge clips

- **Family events.** Several short clips of a wedding, a birthday or a Diwali evening become one video for the family group.
- **Trips.** Clips from each day of a holiday, in order, make a simple travel diary.
- **Lessons and demos.** Record a tutorial in parts, then join them.
- **Shorts series.** Combine three related Shorts into one longer landscape video for your main channel.
- **School and office projects.** Put each team member's recorded section into a single submission.

## How to merge videos step by step

1. Open the [Video Merger](/tools/video-merger/) and click **Choose two or more videos**. You can select several at once, or drag them onto the page.
2. The clips appear under **Clips (in order)**, each with its length, size and file size. Drag a row to move it, or use the up and down arrow buttons. The bin button removes a clip.
3. Need more? Use **Add more clips** below the list.
4. Choose an **Output size**: **Match first clip**, **1920×1080** (16:9, YouTube), **1080×1920** (9:16, Shorts and Reels), **1080×1080** (square) or **1280×720** (smaller and faster).
5. Under **Clips with a different shape**, pick **Fit with bars**, **Blur fill** or **Crop to fill**. With bars, you can set the **Bar colour**.
6. Pick a **Frame rate**: 24, 25, 30 or 60. 30 suits most phone video.
7. Tick **Smooth 0.5 s fade between clips** if you want soft transitions.
8. Check the summary line, which shows the clip count and total length, then press **Merge videos**.
9. Preview the result and download `merged-video.mp4`.

The first merge downloads the browser video engine (about 32 MB), which is then cached. Keep the tab open while the clips are processed.

## Choosing an output size

**Match first clip** is the simplest: if your first clip is 1080×1920 from a phone, the whole video will be too. Reorder your clips if you want a different clip to set the size.

Pick a fixed size when you know where the video is going. For YouTube, 1920×1080 is standard. For Reels or Shorts, choose 1080×1920. If the merge is slow on your device, 1280×720 cuts processing time substantially and is still sharp on phones.

## Mixed shapes: bars, blur or crop

When a landscape clip goes into a vertical video (or the reverse), something has to give.

- **Fit with bars** keeps the whole picture and fills the gaps with a plain colour.
- **Blur fill** keeps the whole picture and fills the gaps with a soft, blurred copy of the clip. Most viewers find this easier on the eye.
- **Crop to fill** zooms in until there are no gaps, cutting off edges. It works when the action is in the centre.

If almost all your clips share one shape, choose that shape as the output and the setting only affects the odd ones out.

## Fades and frame rate

The fade option blends half a second of each clip into the next, for both picture and sound. It makes a family video feel more finished and hides small jumps. Because of the overlap, the final video is half a second shorter per join; the summary line accounts for this.

Frame rate matters less than people think. Choosing 30 is safe for nearly all phone footage. Choose 25 if most clips came from a camera set to 25 fps, and 60 only if your clips were recorded at 60 and contain fast action.

## Tips before you merge

- **Trim first.** Remove dead time from each clip with the [Video Trimmer](/tools/video-trimmer/). Merging is faster with shorter clips, and the result is tighter.
- **Name files in order.** Selecting `01.mp4`, `02.mp4`, `03.mp4` together usually lists them in sequence, saving dragging later.
- **Watch the total size.** Above about 500 MB the tool warns that your browser could run short of memory. A laptop handles more than a phone.
- **Add music afterwards.** Join the clips first, then lay one track over the whole video with [Add Music to Video](/tools/add-music-to-video/).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Mixing very different quality clips at 1080p.** A blurry 480p clip will not look better when scaled up; consider 1280×720 instead.
- **Forgetting the order.** Double-check the numbers in the list before pressing merge.
- **Using clips you do not have rights to.** Compilations of other people's videos can be removed by platforms. Merge your own recordings, or footage you are allowed to use.
- **Closing the browser mid-way.** The work happens on your device and stops if the page closes.

## A quick example

Say you have four clips from a school annual day: two vertical clips from your phone, one landscape clip from a relative's phone, and one short silent clip of the stage decoration. Choose **1080×1920** as the output, since most clips are vertical, and **Blur fill** for the landscape one. Leave the frame rate at 30 and tick the fade option. The silent clip gets a quiet soundtrack automatically, the landscape clip sits in the middle with a soft blurred border, and the whole video plays smoothly from start to finish.
