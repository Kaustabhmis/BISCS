Estimated ad income = monthly views ÷ 1,000 × your RPM. If a channel gets 1,00,000 long-form views a month at an RPM of ₹50, ad revenue would be about ₹5,000 for that month. The difficult part is not the maths but the RPM, which differs hugely between channels and is usually much lower in India than in the US.

## RPM, CPM and the revenue split

Three terms cause most of the confusion around YouTube income:

- **CPM (cost per mille)** is what an advertiser pays for 1,000 ad impressions. It is the advertiser's price, not your income.
- **Revenue share** is the part of ad revenue that goes to the creator. For long-form videos in the YouTube Partner Programme, creators receive 55% of net ad revenue from ads shown on their videos. Shorts work differently: ad revenue goes into a pool that is shared out by views.
- **RPM (revenue per mille)** is what you actually earn per 1,000 views, after YouTube's share, counted across all views, including those where no ad was shown.

Because RPM already accounts for the split and for views without ads, it is the number to use for planning. You can see yours in YouTube Studio under Analytics, then the Revenue tab, once your channel is monetised.

## What affects your RPM

- **Niche.** Finance, technology and business topics often attract higher-paying advertisers than entertainment or general vlogs.
- **Viewer location.** Advertisers in some countries pay much more per impression. A channel with a largely Indian audience usually has a lower RPM than one watched mostly in the US or UK, even with identical views.
- **Video length.** Long videos can carry mid-roll ads, which can raise revenue per view.
- **Season.** Ad rates often rise before major festivals and towards the year end, and can dip at the start of the year.
- **Format.** Shorts RPM is typically far lower than long-form RPM.

## When ad income starts

Ad revenue requires the YouTube Partner Programme. The well-known threshold for ad revenue sharing is 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months or 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days. YouTube also offers a lower entry tier in many countries, including India, that unlocks fan-funding features such as channel memberships and Super Thanks before ad revenue. You also need to follow YouTube's monetisation policies and link an AdSense account. Requirements change, so check the Earn section in YouTube Studio for the current rules.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Using a CPM figure from the internet as your RPM.** CPM is higher by design; using it will overstate your income.
- **Assuming US earnings stories apply in India.** Viewer location changes RPM dramatically.
- **Counting all views as monetised.** Views before you joined YPP, views on videos with limited ads and many Shorts views earn little or nothing.
- **Planning a budget on the high end.** Use the low figure for anything you depend on.
- **Ignoring tax.** YouTube income is generally taxable in India, and US withholding may apply to earnings from US viewers. A chartered accountant can advise on your case.
