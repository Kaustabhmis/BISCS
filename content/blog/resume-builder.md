A good resume is clear, honest and easy to scan, both for a recruiter who spends a short time on it and for the software many companies use to sort applications. ## What does "ATS-friendly" actually mean?

An applicant tracking system (ATS) is software that companies and job portals use to collect, store and search applications. Many systems pull text out of your resume to fill in fields and let recruiters search by keywords. If the software cannot read your resume properly, a recruiter searching for "Tally" or "React" may never see you.

You do not need tricks to pass an ATS. You need a resume that is simple to read for both machines and people:

- **Use a single-column layout** for the safest parsing. In the builder, Classic and Minimal are single-column; Modern uses a side column, which many systems handle but some do not.
- **Use standard section headings** like "Experience", "Education" and "Skills" rather than creative labels.
- **Keep contact details in the main body**, not in a page header or an image.
- **Use real text, not images of text.** The PDF from the builder contains selectable text.
- **Match the wording of the job description** where it honestly applies. If the post says "accounts payable", use that phrase rather than only "vendor payments".
- **Avoid graphics for skills.** Rating bars and dots look neat but mean little to software. The builder's skill levels are optional for that reason.

The builder's checklist covers these points and others: a headline under your name, a summary of 25 to 90 words, at least two bullets per job, numbers in at least half your bullets, six or more relevant skills, dates on every job, and no photo.

## Fresher vs experienced: how should the resume differ?

| Area | Fresher or recent graduate | Experienced professional |
|---|---|---|
| Length | One page | One to two pages |
| Section order | Summary, Education, Projects, Internships, Skills | Summary, Experience, Skills, Education |
| Main evidence | Projects, internships, certifications, college roles | Achievements in past jobs, with numbers |
| Education detail | Degree, college, year, marks or CGPA if good | Degree, college, year; marks usually not needed |
| Summary focus | Skills and the role you want | Years of experience, specialism, key results |

### Tips for freshers

- **Lead with what you can prove.** A final-year project, an internship, a hackathon or a certification shows more than a list of subjects.
- **Describe projects like jobs.** What you built, the tools you used, and the result.
- **Include 10th and 12th marks only if asked** or if they are strong; many employers now focus on the degree.
- **Avoid generic objectives** like "seeking a challenging position in a reputed organisation". Write two lines about the role you want and what you bring.

### Tips for experienced candidates

- **Write achievements, not duties.** "Reduced month-end closing from 7 days to 4 by automating bank reconciliation" beats "Responsible for bank reconciliation".
- **Use numbers where they are true**: ₹ values, percentages, team sizes, turnaround times.
- **Trim older roles.** Jobs from 10 or more years ago need only a line or two.
- **Explain gaps briefly if asked**, but you do not need to justify every month on the page.

## What should you leave out of a resume?

Indian resumes often carry details that recruiters do not need and that can invite bias. Unless an employer or a government form specifically asks, leave out:

- **Date of birth and age**
- **Marital status, religion, caste and father's name**
- **Full residential address** (your city is enough)
- **Aadhaar, PAN or passport numbers**, which are sensitive identifiers
- **A photo**, for most private-sector roles
- **Current and expected salary**, which belong in the application form or conversation
- **"References available on request"**, which is assumed
- **The declaration paragraph**, unless requested
- **Hobbies that say nothing**, like "listening to music"; keep only interests that add something relevant

Government, PSU and some overseas applications have their own required formats. Always follow the employer's instructions over general advice.

## Common resume mistakes

- **Typos in your email or phone number.** Check them twice; a recruiter cannot call a wrong number.
- **An unprofessional email address.** Create a simple one with your name if needed.
- **One resume for every job.** Adjust your summary and skills for each application.
- **Dense paragraphs.** Use short bullets, ideally under two lines each.
- **Inconsistent dates and formatting.** Pick one date style, such as "Jun 2023", and use it everywhere.
- **Exaggeration.** Background checks are common, and interviews quickly expose inflated claims.
