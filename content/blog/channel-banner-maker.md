Make your YouTube banner 2560 × 1440 pixels, keep it under 6 MB, and put every important word and logo inside the centre 1546 × 423 area. That safe area is the only part visible on every device; the rest acts as background that some screens show and others crop.

Most banner frustration comes from ignoring that last point. The [Channel Banner & Cover Maker](/tools/channel-banner-maker/) draws the safe area for you, previews the result on TV, desktop, tablet and mobile, and exports at the correct size.

## Why a YouTube banner gets cropped

A single banner image has to fit very different screens. YouTube handles this by showing different slices of the same picture:

| Device | Visible area (approx.) | What it means for your design |
|---|---|---|
| TV | 2560 × 1440 (whole image) | The full picture, rarely seen by most viewers |
| Desktop | 2560 × 423 | A wide strip across the vertical middle |
| Tablet | 1855 × 423 | A shorter strip, sides trimmed |
| Mobile | 1546 × 423 | Only the centre box, the safe area |

So a 2560 × 1440 canvas is really a 1546 × 423 design with generous bleed. Treat the top and bottom thirds as decoration, never as space for text.

## Designing a banner step by step

1. Open the [Channel Banner & Cover Maker](/tools/channel-banner-maker/). It starts on **YouTube channel banner** under **Banner type**, with **Show safe-area guides** ticked.
2. Pick a starting point under **Templates**: Creator, Tech, Food & travel, Business, Hindi or Bengali. Each one places text and a sticker inside the safe area.
3. In **Background**, choose **Colour**, **Gradient**, **Glow** or **Photo**. Gradients have two colour pickers, an angle slider and a row of preset combinations. For Photo, press **Choose photo**, then use **Blur** and **Darken** so text stays readable on top.
4. Under **Layers**, add **Text**, a **Sticker** (searchable icons), a **Shape** or your **Logo**. Each layer appears in a list where you can move it down, duplicate it or delete it.
5. Click a layer to edit it in **Selected layer**. Text layers have font, size, weight, alignment, text colour, and checkboxes for **Shadow**, **Label background** and **Italic**. **Centre in safe area** snaps any layer into the middle of the safe zone.
6. Drag layers on the canvas to move them and drag a corner to resize. With the canvas focused, arrow keys nudge the selected layer and Delete removes it.
7. Check **How it looks on each device** below the canvas.
8. In **Export**, choose **JPG** or **PNG** and press **Download**.

After download, a line under the button shows the file size and confirms whether it is within YouTube's 6 MB limit.

## Staying under 6 MB

For the YouTube preset, the export adjusts itself. It starts with high-quality JPG and, if the file would exceed 6 MB, steps the quality down until it fits. If you chose PNG and the result is too large, it switches to JPG and tells you. A heavily detailed, unblurred photo background is the usual reason for a large file; a little blur or a gradient fixes it.

## Design tips that work on small screens

- **Two lines of text at most.** A channel name and a short promise, such as "New videos every Friday", is plenty.
- **Big type.** On a phone, the safe area is shown only a few centimetres tall. Text that looks bold on a monitor can become tiny.
- **Strong contrast.** White text on a mid-tone photo often disappears; use the Darken slider, a shadow or a label background.
- **Leave space around the edges of the safe area.** Hugging the guide line risks clipping on unusual screen sizes.
- **Match your thumbnails.** Reuse colours and fonts from your [YouTube Thumbnail Maker](/tools/youtube-thumbnail-maker/) designs so the channel feels consistent.
- **Mention your upload schedule only if you keep it.** An outdated promise looks worse than none.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Putting the channel name at the top of the canvas.** On desktop and mobile it will be cut off completely.
- **Using a low-resolution photo.** A small image stretched to 2560 pixels wide looks blurry on TVs and large monitors. Use one at least as wide as the banner.
- **Copying images you do not have rights to.** Use your own photos or properly licensed ones.
- **Relying on fine detail in the corners.** Most viewers never see them.
- **Placing contact details as text in the image.** YouTube lets you add clickable links to your channel profile, whereas text inside an image cannot be clicked.
