The tax-free part of your House Rent Allowance (HRA) is the **lowest** of three amounts, all calculated for the year:

1. The actual HRA you received from your employer.
2. Rent paid minus 10% of your basic salary plus dearness allowance (DA).
3. 50% of basic plus DA if you live in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata or Chennai, or 40% in any other city.

Whatever HRA is left above that lowest figure is taxable. The exemption is available only under the old tax regime. The free [HRA Exemption Calculator](/tools/hra-calculator/) works out all three limits and highlights the one that applies to you.

## Worked example: a renter in Kolkata

Arjun works in Kolkata. His basic salary plus DA is ₹50,000 a month, his employer pays ₹20,000 as HRA, and he pays ₹18,000 a month in rent.

| Condition (annual) | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Actual HRA received | 20,000 × 12 | ₹2,40,000 |
| Rent minus 10% of basic + DA | 2,16,000 − 60,000 | ₹1,56,000 |
| 50% of basic + DA (metro) | 6,00,000 × 50% | ₹3,00,000 |

The lowest is ₹1,56,000, so that much of his HRA is exempt. The remaining ₹84,000 is added to his taxable salary. Monthly, that is ₹13,000 exempt and ₹7,000 taxable.

Notice that if Arjun lived in a non-metro city, the third limit would be ₹2,40,000, but his exemption would still be ₹1,56,000, because rent is the binding limit. In his case, paying more rent (genuinely) would raise the exemption; moving cities would not.

## A second example: when the city limit bites

Divya works in Pune with basic plus DA of ₹40,000 a month, HRA of ₹20,000 and rent of ₹25,000.

- Actual HRA: ₹2,40,000
- Rent minus 10% of basic: 3,00,000 − 48,000 = ₹2,52,000
- 40% of basic (non-metro): ₹1,92,000

Her exemption is ₹1,92,000 and ₹48,000 is taxable. Here the 40% city cap is the lowest figure, so higher rent would not increase her exemption.

## What counts as basic salary for HRA?

"Salary" for this rule means basic pay plus DA where DA forms part of retirement benefits, plus any commission fixed as a percentage of turnover. It does not include HRA itself, special allowances, bonuses or perquisites. Check your salary slip; the [Salary Slip Generator](/tools/salary-slip-generator/) can help you see components laid out clearly if you are structuring pay for a small team.

## Documents checklist

- Rent receipts for each month, signed by the landlord. The [Rent Receipt Generator](/tools/rent-receipt-generator/) creates them quickly.
- A rent agreement, especially for larger rents.
- Landlord's PAN if annual rent exceeds ₹1 lakh.
- Proof of payment, such as bank transfers or UPI records.
- Your employer's declaration form for exemptions and deductions, often Form 12BB.

Submit these to your employer on time so the exemption is reflected in monthly TDS. If you miss the employer's deadline, you can usually still claim the exemption when filing your income tax return, provided you have the proof.

## Common HRA mistakes

1. **Choosing the new regime and still expecting HRA relief.** It does not apply there.
2. **Including allowances in "basic".** Only basic plus qualifying DA counts.
3. **Assuming all large cities are metros.** For this rule, only four cities get the 50% limit.
4. **Claiming for months you did not pay rent.** Calculate for the actual period of renting; if you moved mid-year, work out each period separately.
5. **Cash rent without receipts.** It makes the claim hard to support.
6. **Living in your own house in the same city.** You cannot claim HRA for rent you do not actually pay.

## Should you even claim HRA?

HRA only helps under the old regime, so the real question is which regime gives lower tax overall. Put your exemption into the [Income Tax Calculator](/tools/income-tax-calculator/) along with your other deductions and compare. For many people with moderate rent and few other deductions, the new regime is still cheaper.

This guide explains the standard calculation for FY 2025-26 and is general information, not tax advice. Rules, including which cities count as metros, can change, and the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from FY 2026-27. Check the Income Tax Department's website or speak to a tax professional, especially for unusual arrangements like paying rent to relatives.
