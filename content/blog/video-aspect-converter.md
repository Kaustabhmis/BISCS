Every platform prefers its own shape. Reels, Shorts and WhatsApp Status fill a phone screen in 9:16, the Instagram and Facebook feeds give the most space to 4:5 or 1:1, and YouTube's regular player is 16:9. Converting between them means deciding what to do with the part of the picture that no longer fits.

The [Video Resizer for Social Media](/tools/video-aspect-converter/) gives you three answers to that question, crop, bars or blur, and shows a live preview before you export. The work happens on your own device.

## Common shapes and where they go

| Shape | Output at 1080p | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 9:16 Vertical | 1080 × 1920 | Shorts, Reels, Status, Stories |
| 1:1 Square | 1080 × 1080 | Instagram and Facebook feed |
| 4:5 Portrait | 1080 × 1350 | Feed posts that take up more of the screen |
| 16:9 Landscape | 1920 × 1080 | YouTube videos, Facebook, presentations |
| 4:3 Classic | 1440 × 1080 | Older TVs, some slide decks |

The Resolution setting (1080p, 720p or 480p) sets the shorter side, and the longer side follows from the shape. So 9:16 at 720p gives 720 × 1280. Platform recommendations change from time to time, so check the app's help pages if you need an exact current figure.

## Crop, bars or blur: which should you pick?

**Crop** cuts the picture to fill the new shape completely. Nothing looks empty, but you lose the edges. Going from 16:9 to 9:16 removes roughly two thirds of the width, so it only works when the subject sits in a narrow area.

**Fit with bars** shrinks the whole frame to fit inside the new shape and fills the gaps with a solid **Bar colour**. Black is the default; a brand colour or white can look tidier. Nothing is lost, but on a vertical screen a landscape video becomes a small strip in the middle.

**Blur fill** also keeps the whole frame, but fills the gaps with a zoomed, blurred copy of the same video. This is the familiar look of many Reels made from landscape footage. It feels fuller than bars and avoids the harsh lines.

A rough guide:

- One person talking in the centre: **Crop**.
- Slides, screen recordings, text-heavy footage: **Fit with bars** or **Blur fill**.
- Group photos, wide scenery, sport: **Blur fill**.
- A brand video with strict colours: **Fit with bars** with your own colour.

## Picking a resolution

1080p is the usual choice for anything you plan to post. It is also the slowest to process, because every frame is re-encoded in the browser. On a phone or an older laptop, a long 1080p conversion can take a while, and 720p finishes noticeably faster. 480p is useful for quick previews or sending a draft to someone on a slow connection, but it will look soft when posted.

If file size matters more than shape, for example to fit a WhatsApp or email limit, use the [Video Compressor](/tools/video-compressor/) after resizing.

## Tips for a cleaner result

- **Shoot with the final shape in mind.** If you know a clip will become vertical, keep the subject near the centre while recording.
- **Check text near the edges.** Captions or lower-thirds burned into the original can be cut off by Crop. Switch to Blur fill if they matter.
- **Trim first.** Resizing a ten-minute file to use thirty seconds wastes time. Cut the part you need with the Video Trimmer, then resize the short clip.
- **Do not upscale small videos.** Turning a 480p clip into 1080p adds pixels but no detail. Choose a resolution close to the original.
- **Preview on a phone.** A 4:5 post can look fine on a monitor but crowded on a small screen.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Cropping a moving subject.** The crop position is fixed for the whole video. If the person walks across the frame, they will drift out. For clips like that, the Shorts & Reels Maker can follow faces instead.
- **Using bars for vertical platforms without thinking.** Thick bars on a Reel leave most of the screen empty. Blur fill usually looks better.
- **Forgetting the original audio settings.** The tool keeps the soundtrack as it is; it does not add or remove music.
- **Closing the tab mid-way.** Processing stops if the page is closed or the phone locks for a long time.
