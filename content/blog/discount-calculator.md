To find the price after a discount, multiply the original price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). A ₹1,499 shirt at 40% off costs 1,499 × 0.60 = ₹899.40. Your saving is the original price minus the final price.

When a shop offers an "extra" discount on top of a sale price, the second discount applies to the reduced price, not the original. So 20% off followed by an extra 10% off is **28% off in total, not 30%**. ## Worked example: a festive sale

A pair of running shoes has an MRP of ₹2,999. The store offers 20% off, and your bank card gives an extra 10% off.

1. After 20% off: 2,999 × 0.80 = ₹2,399.20
2. After the extra 10%: 2,399.20 × 0.90 = ₹2,159.28
3. Total saving: 2,999 − 2,159.28 = ₹839.72
4. Effective discount: 839.72 ÷ 2,999 × 100 = **28%**

A straight 30% discount would have cost ₹2,099.30, about ₹60 less. That gap grows with bigger discounts.

## The stacked discount formula

For two discounts a% and b%, the effective discount is:

**Effective % = 1 − (1 − a ÷ 100) × (1 − b ÷ 100)**, then × 100

| Offer | Looks like | Actual effective discount |
|---|---|---|
| 10% + 10% | 20% | 19% |
| 20% + 10% | 30% | 28% |
| 30% + 20% | 50% | 44% |
| 50% + 20% | 70% | 60% |
| 50% + 50% | 100% | 75% |

On a ₹4,000 item, "50% + extra 20% off" means paying ₹1,600, while a true 70% off would mean ₹1,200. Reading offers this way helps you compare deals across stores and apps.

## How do you find the original price from a sale price?

Sometimes you only see the sale price and the percentage. Divide the sale price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). A dress tagged ₹1,799 "after 40% off" should have an original price of 1,799 ÷ 0.60 = ₹2,998.33, so roughly ₹2,999. If the tag claims an MRP far above that, the advertised discount does not add up.

## Are "buy one get one" offers really 50% off?

Only if you wanted two of the item. Buy one get one free on two items priced the same is a 50% discount per item. "Buy 2 get 1 free" is one free item in three, or 33.33% off. "Buy 3 get 1 free" is 25% off. If you only needed one, a straight 20% discount may save you more money than a bigger-sounding multi-buy deal. To compare, work out the price per item you actually need and run the straight-discount option through the calculator.

|---|---|
| Buy 1 get 1 free | 50% |
| Buy 2 get 1 free | 33.33% |
| Buy 3 get 1 free | 25% |

## Where does GST come in?

For goods sold in shops and online to consumers, the MRP printed on the pack is inclusive of all taxes, and the sale price you pay is also tax-inclusive. You do not add GST on top.

The GST option is useful in other situations: a supplier quoting "₹50,000 less 10%, plus GST", or a service priced before tax. In those cases, GST is generally charged on the discounted value shown on the invoice. With 18% GST, the shoes in our example would come to ₹2,159.28 + ₹388.67 = ₹2,547.95. ## Smart shopping checklist

- Is the discount on the MRP or on an already-inflated "original" price? Compare with the price a few weeks earlier if you can.
- Are the two offers stacked or combined? Most "extra" discounts are stacked.
- Is a bank or wallet discount capped at a maximum amount? A "10% up to ₹500" offer gives only ₹500 off a ₹10,000 purchase, which is 5%.
- Do delivery charges, convenience fees or cash-on-delivery fees reduce the saving?
- Is the item returnable at the sale price, and are exchange terms the same?

## Common mistakes

1. **Adding discounts together.** 20% + 10% is 28% off, not 30%.
2. **Ignoring caps on card offers.** The headline percentage may apply only up to a small rupee limit.
3. **Adding GST to an MRP-based price.** MRP already includes tax.
4. **Comparing deals by percentage alone.** Compare the final rupee price, including fees.
5. **Rounding the first step.** Keep paise until the end for an accurate final price.

## A note on accuracy

The calculator applies discounts in the order entered and GST on the final discounted amount. Actual offers can include caps, minimum order values, or conditions that change the result.
