Gratuity is a lump sum an employer pays when you leave after long service, as a thank-you for the years you worked there. If your employer is covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, the formula is:

**Gratuity = 15 × last drawn monthly (basic + DA) × years of service ÷ 26**

A final part-year of six months or more counts as a full year. So someone earning ₹60,000 a month in basic plus DA who leaves after 10 years and 7 months is credited with 11 years, and their gratuity is about ₹3,80,769. The free [Gratuity Calculator](/tools/gratuity-calculator/) does this sum and shows how much is tax-exempt.

## Why 15 and 26?

The Act grants **15 days' wages for every completed year** of service. It treats a month as **26 working days**. So one year's gratuity equals 15 ÷ 26 of a month's basic plus DA, about 57.7% of it.

If your employer is not covered by the Act, gratuity is often calculated with half a month's salary per completed year, based on a 30-day month (15 ÷ 30), and part-years are ignored. That gives a lower figure, as the examples below show.

## Worked examples

| Situation | Years counted | Calculation | Gratuity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Covered, ₹60,000, 10 years 7 months | 11 | 15 × 60,000 × 11 ÷ 26 | ₹3,80,769 |
| Covered, ₹60,000, 10 years 5 months | 10 | 15 × 60,000 × 10 ÷ 26 | ₹3,46,154 |
| Not covered, ₹60,000, 10 years 7 months | 10 | 15 × 60,000 × 10 ÷ 30 | ₹3,00,000 |
| Covered, ₹1,50,000, 25 years | 25 | 15 × 1,50,000 × 25 ÷ 26 | ₹21,63,462 |

The first two rows show why timing matters. Leaving at 10 years 7 months instead of 10 years 5 months adds a full year, about ₹34,600 here. If you are close to a six-month mark, it may be worth knowing before you hand in your notice.

## Who is eligible for gratuity?

- You must normally complete **five years of continuous service** with the same employer.
- The five-year condition does not apply on **death or disablement**; gratuity is then paid to you or your nominee.
- Some court rulings have treated **four years and 240 days** as five years, especially where the employer works a six-day week, but this is not automatic.
- The Act applies to factories, mines, plantations, shops and establishments with **ten or more employees** on any day in the previous twelve months.

The Code on Social Security, 2020 consolidates the Gratuity Act with other labour laws and brings changes such as gratuity for fixed-term employees after one year. Check how its provisions apply to your job on the Ministry of Labour and Employment's website.

## How is gratuity taxed?

For private-sector employees, gratuity is tax-free up to **₹20 lakh** across their whole career, and the calculator applies this limit. Anything above it is taxed as salary income in the year you receive it. Gratuity received by **central and state government employees is fully exempt**.

In the last example in the table, ₹20 lakh is exempt and about ₹1,63,462 would be taxable. Note too that the Act caps the gratuity an employer is legally required to pay at ₹20 lakh, although some employers pay more under their own schemes. ## Common gratuity mistakes

1. **Using CTC or gross salary.** Only basic plus DA counts. A ₹1 lakh gross salary might have only ₹40,000 basic.
2. **Rounding up every part-year.** Only six months or more rounds up, and only for covered employers.
3. **Forgetting the nominee form.** Keep your gratuity nomination up to date so your family can claim without delay.
4. **Assuming gratuity is in your PF.** It is a separate benefit, paid by the employer.
5. **Not following up.** The employer must pay within a set time after it becomes payable; delays can attract interest under the Act.

## Tips before you resign or retire

- Check your appointment letter and HR policy to see whether your employer follows the Act or a more generous scheme.
- Look at your last few [salary slips](/tools/salary-slip-generator/) to confirm basic and DA.
- Plan what to do with the lump sum. The [Lumpsum Calculator](/tools/lumpsum-calculator/) shows how it might grow if invested.

## A word of caution

This calculator follows the standard statutory formula and the ₹20 lakh tax exemption, but it does not know your employer's policy, any disputes over continuity of service, or how labour-code changes apply to your role. Treat the result as an estimate and general information, not legal or tax advice.
