To fill a PDF form for free, open [Fill PDF Form](/tools/fill-pdf-form/), choose your PDF, type into the text fields, tick the checkboxes and pick options from radio buttons and dropdowns, then download. Tick **Flatten** if you want the answers fixed permanently on the page.

The form is filled in your browser and never uploaded, which matters when it asks for your PAN, Aadhaar number, bank details or salary.

## Fillable form or flat PDF?

PDF forms come in two kinds, and knowing which you have saves time.

| | Fillable (interactive) form | Flat PDF |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The PDF contains real form fields (AcroForm) | Just a picture or layout of a form |
| How to spot it | Clicking in a box in a PDF reader lets you type | Clicking does nothing; you can only print |
| Best tool | Fill PDF Form | [Edit PDF](/tools/pdf-editor/) (add text and ticks anywhere) |

When you open a flat PDF in the form filler, it tells you no fillable fields were found and offers a link to the editor. Many bank, insurance and HR forms are fillable; many scanned government forms are flat.

## How to fill a PDF form step by step

1. Open [Fill PDF Form](/tools/fill-pdf-form/) and choose your PDF.
2. The tool lists every field it finds, with a count of fields. Field names are tidied up to be readable.
3. Fill in the text fields. Multi-line fields accept longer answers.
4. Tick checkboxes and choose options for radio buttons and dropdowns.
5. Fields marked **read-only** are locked by the form's author and cannot be changed.
6. Decide whether to **Flatten** the form (see below).
7. Download the filled PDF and open it to check every answer.

## To flatten or not to flatten?

Flattening "prints" your answers onto the page, removing the interactive fields.

**Flatten when:**

- You are submitting the final version and want nobody (including you, by accident) to change the answers.
- The recipient may open the file in a viewer that displays form fields badly, such as some mobile apps.
- You are going to merge, compress or convert the form, because some of those steps remove form fields.

**Do not flatten when:**

- You may need to edit the answers later.
- The organisation asks for the form to stay fillable, for example so their system can read the field values.

A sensible habit: keep an unflattened copy for yourself and send the flattened one.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Typing in Indian scripts or using ₹.** The standard PDF fonts cannot display them, and they turn into "?". Use English and write "Rs. 25,000" instead of "₹25,000".
- **Overflowing text boxes.** Long answers in a small single-line field can be cut off or shrunk. Abbreviate sensibly or use an attached sheet if the form allows it.
- **Forgetting the signature.** Interactive signature fields are digital-signature fields and are skipped here. For a signature image, open the filled form in [Sign PDF](/tools/sign-pdf/) afterwards.
- **Flattening too early.** If you notice a mistake after flattening, you have to start again from the original form.
- **Compressing a fillable form with image-based levels.** Strong and Extreme compression remove form fields. Flatten first, or use the Basic level.

## Government and office forms

Fillable forms are common in HR onboarding packs, bank account updates, insurance claims and some government and tax-related downloads. Requirements vary: some offices want a printed and hand-signed copy, some accept a signed PDF by email, and online portals often have their own web forms instead of PDFs. Follow the instructions given with the form, and when a document must be signed with a Digital Signature Certificate, use the appropriate DSC software for that step.

## What kinds of fields are supported?

| Field type | What you can do |
|---|---|
| Text field (single or multi-line) | Type names, addresses, amounts, dates |
| Checkbox | Tick or untick |
| Radio group | Choose one option, such as Male / Female / Other |
| Dropdown and list | Pick from the options the form provides |
| Signature field | Skipped; add a signature image with Sign PDF instead |
| Buttons | Skipped; they usually run scripts that browsers don't execute here |

If the tool reports that some fields could not be updated, the form may use an unusual structure. In that case, check those fields in the downloaded PDF and, if needed, add the missing answers with Edit PDF.

## Dates, amounts and formatting tips

Forms rarely validate what you type in this tool, so be consistent. Use the date format the form shows (DD/MM/YYYY is most common in India), write amounts with "Rs." and Indian digit grouping if the form expects it (for example Rs. 1,25,000), and match capital letters if the form says "IN BLOCK LETTERS".

## Checklist before you submit

- Every mandatory field filled, including dates and codes.
- Checkboxes and radio options double-checked.
- No "?" characters where you typed special symbols.
- Signature added where required, in the format the recipient accepts.
- Flattened copy for sending, unflattened copy saved for yourself.

## Privacy

Forms collect precisely the information you should be careful with: identity numbers, bank accounts, addresses, health details. Here, the form is read and filled by a PDF library running in your browser, and the completed file is saved straight to your device. Nothing is uploaded or kept anywhere else, and closing the tab clears everything.
