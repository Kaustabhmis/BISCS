A watermark is text placed on top of a photo to show who it belongs to or what it is for. Photographers sign their work in a corner, online sellers stamp their shop name on product shots, and careful people mark copies of their ID documents with the purpose they were shared for.

The [Watermark Image](/tools/image-watermark/) tool from BISCS India lets you add a single mark in any of nine positions or a repeating tiled pattern across the whole picture. The watermark is drawn in your browser at full resolution, and your photo stays on your device.

## Single mark or tiled pattern: which do you need?

| Style | How it looks | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Single, in a corner | Small, neat signature | Photography portfolios, Instagram posts, blog images |
| Single, centred and large | Bold and obvious | Previews for clients before payment |
| Tiled pattern, rotated | Diagonal repeated text over everything | ID copies, documents, stock-style previews |
| Tiled, very faint | Subtle texture | Product catalogues where you want protection without distraction |

A corner mark is the least intrusive but the easiest to crop away. A tiled pattern is much harder to remove and makes misuse obvious, at the cost of being more visible.

## How to watermark a photo step by step

1. Open [Watermark Image](/tools/image-watermark/) and choose your photo, drop it in or paste it with Ctrl + V.
2. Type your **Watermark text**. It starts as a copyright line with the current year, which you can change to your name, shop, website or any message. Press Enter for a second line.
3. Pick a **Font** (sans-serif, serif, monospace, Impact or script) and a **Colour**, and toggle **Bold** and **Soft shadow**.
4. Set the **Size**, **Opacity** and **Rotation**.
5. Choose **Single** and click one of the nine positions in the grid, adjusting the **Margin** from the edge. Or choose **Tiled pattern** and set the **Spacing** between repeats.
6. Check the preview and download the watermarked image.

## Watermarking ID copies before sharing them

Indians share copies of Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID, driving licence and bank passbooks constantly: for SIM cards, rental agreements, KYC at a new job or a loan application. Each copy could be misused if it ends up in the wrong hands.

A practical habit is to stamp a tiled watermark that states who it is for and when. For example: “For KYC at XYZ Housing Society only, 12/03”. Use:

- **Tiled pattern** so it cannot be cropped out.
- **Rotation** around −30° so it crosses lines of text diagonally.
- **Opacity** around 20–30% so your details stay readable.
- **A dark colour** on light cards, or white with shadow on dark backgrounds.

This does not make a copy impossible to misuse, but it makes it far harder to pass off for a different purpose. Ask the receiver whether they accept watermarked copies; most do. If you are sharing a PDF rather than an image, use [Watermark PDF](/tools/watermark-pdf/) instead.

## What should your watermark say?

The text depends on why you are marking the image:

- **Photographers and artists:** your name or studio name, optionally with the © symbol and year, such as “© 2026 Ananya Sen Photography”. Your website or Instagram handle is even more useful, because people who like the photo can find you.
- **Online sellers and home businesses:** your shop name and the platform where you sell, for example “Kolkata Kantha Store, on Instagram”. This helps when resellers copy product photos to their own listings.
- **Drafts and previews:** a plain word such as “PREVIEW”, “DRAFT” or “SAMPLE” in a tiled pattern, so a client can judge the work but cannot use it before paying.
- **ID and document copies:** the purpose, the receiver and the date, as described above.

Keep it short. Two lines is the practical maximum; anything longer becomes hard to read and starts to look like a paragraph stamped across your image.

## Tips for professional-looking watermarks

### Match the colour to the background

White with a soft shadow is readable on almost any photo. On bright, white product backgrounds, a mid-grey works better than black, which can look heavy.

### Keep it small for portfolios

Viewers come to see the photograph, not the signature. A corner mark sized around 3–5% of the image is enough to carry your name without spoiling the shot.

### Use a consistent style

If you post regularly on Instagram or run an online store, use the same text, font, position and opacity on every image. It becomes part of your brand. A simple logo-style text such as your shop name and website works well; you can design a matching logo with the [Logo Maker](/tools/logo-maker/).

### Place it where cropping hurts

A mark in the far corner is the easiest to crop. Placing it slightly inwards, or over part of the subject, means cropping it out would damage the picture.

## Common mistakes

- **Opacity at 100% for a large centred mark.** The photo becomes unusable for its real purpose.
- **Tiny text on a large photo.** It looks fine in the editor but disappears when the image is viewed on a phone. Zoom out and check.
- **Watermarking a document so heavily that the receiver cannot read it.** They will simply ask for another copy.
- **Forgetting the year or website changes.** Update the default text before you start a batch.
- **Watermarking the only copy.** Always keep your original unmarked photo safely stored; the tool saves a new file, so your original is untouched.
