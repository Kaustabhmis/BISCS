Passport-size photos are small portrait photos with fixed dimensions, most commonly 35 × 45 mm in India and many other countries. Making them yourself means cropping a good portrait to that exact shape and printing several copies on one sheet, which you then cut out.

The [Passport Photo Maker](/tools/passport-photo-maker/) from BISCS India does the cropping at print resolution and lays out as many copies as fit on a 4 × 6 inch photo print or an A4 sheet, complete with cut lines. You can print at home or take the file to any photo shop.

## Which passport photo size do you need?

| Size | Pixels at 300 DPI | Commonly used for |
|---|---|---|
| 35 × 45 mm | 413 × 531 | India, UK, EU and Schengen visas, many application forms |
| 2 × 2 in (51 × 51 mm) | 600 × 600 | United States passports and visas |

Always confirm the size in the instructions of the authority you are applying to. Visa requirements are set by each country and can change.

### A note on Indian passport applications

For an Indian passport, the commonly stated requirement is a 35 × 45 mm colour photograph with a plain white background, the full face visible from the front, and neutral expression. For many applications, the photograph is captured at the Passport Seva Kendra during your appointment, but printed photos are still asked for in other situations. Rules can be revised, so check the current guidelines on the official Passport Seva website rather than relying on any third-party summary, including this one.

Printed passport-size photos are also routinely needed for school and college admissions, bank account opening, SIM cards, ID cards, visa applications, gym memberships and government forms.

## How to make passport photos step by step

1. Take a photo against a plain, light wall in good, even daylight. Face the camera directly, with shoulders visible.
2. Open the [Passport Photo Maker](/tools/passport-photo-maker/) and choose the photo, drag it in or paste it with Ctrl + V.
3. Choose the **Photo size**: 35 × 45 mm or 2 × 2 inch.
4. **Frame the face**: drag the frame to centre your head and pull a corner to zoom. Keep the chin near the bottom guide and some space above the hair. The face should fill roughly 70–80% of the photo height.
5. If your photo is a transparent cut-out, pick a **Background fill**: White, Light blue or Custom.
6. Choose the **Print sheet**: 4 × 6 inch or A4. Set the **Number of copies** or leave it empty to fill the sheet, adjust the **Gap between photos**, and keep **Draw cut lines** on.
7. Download the single **photo (JPG)**, the **Sheet (JPG)** for a photo lab, or the **Sheet (PDF)** for a home printer.

## Printing: home printer or photo shop?

### At a photo shop

Most photo studios and print kiosks in India print 4 × 6 inch photos cheaply. Send them the sheet JPG on WhatsApp or a pen drive and ask for a normal 4 × 6 print without cropping or resizing. You get several passport photos for the price of a single print, often less than what a studio charges for a set.

### On a home printer

Use the PDF version and print on photo paper if you have it. In the print dialog:

- Set scale to **100%** or **Actual size**. Turn off fit to page.
- Choose the correct paper size and the highest photo quality setting.
- After printing, measure one photo with a ruler. It should be exactly 35 mm wide. If it is not, the printer scaled the page.

Plain paper prints are usually not accepted for official photos, so use photo paper or a lab for anything formal.

## Tips for a photo that gets accepted

- **Lighting:** face a window during the day. Avoid overhead tube lights that cast shadows under the eyes and nose.
- **Background:** a plain white or very light wall. Patterns, doors and furniture behind you will lead to rejection.
- **Expression:** neutral, mouth closed, both eyes open and looking at the camera.
- **Glasses:** many authorities now prefer no glasses. If you wear them, avoid reflections and tinted lenses.
- **Clothing:** a darker top contrasts well against a white background.
- **Recent photo:** most applications expect a photo taken within the last few months.

## Common mistakes

- **Head too small or too large.** A head that fills only half the frame or touches the top edge is a common reason for rejection.
- **Printing at the wrong scale.** A fit-to-page setting can shrink every photo by several millimetres.
- **Busy background.** This tool only fills transparent areas; it does not remove a real wall. If your background is not plain, use the [AI Passport Photo](/tools/ai-passport-photo/) tool, which detects your face and makes the background white automatically.
- **Selfie distortion.** A phone held at arm’s length enlarges the nose. Ask someone to take the photo from about a metre or two away.
- **Using an old, low-resolution photo.** The tool needs enough pixels for a sharp 300 DPI print.

## What about digital uploads?

Online forms usually need a digital file rather than a print, often with a specific pixel size and a KB limit. Use the single photo download, then adjust it with [Resize Image](/tools/image-resizer/) and [Compress Image](/tools/image-compressor/) to match the numbers in the official notice.
