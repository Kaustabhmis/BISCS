You can record your screen without installing any software. Modern desktop browsers include a screen capture feature that websites can use with your permission, and the [Screen Recorder](/tools/screen-recorder/) on BISCS India builds on it: record your whole screen, one window or a single browser tab, add your voice and a webcam bubble, and download the video with no watermark.

It works best in **Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a Windows, Mac or Linux computer**. Safari on a Mac can also record. Phones and tablets are the exception: mobile browsers currently do not let websites capture the screen, so on a phone you should use its built-in screen recorder instead.

## What can you record?

When you start a recording, your browser asks what to share. You usually get three choices:

- **Entire screen.** Everything on one monitor, including notifications. Best for demonstrating work across several apps.
- **A window.** Just one application, such as Excel, Tally or a code editor. Other windows and pop-ups stay private.
- **A browser tab.** Just one tab. Ideal for walking through a website, a Google Sheet or an online form, and the most reliable way to include the page’s sound.

Typical uses include recording a how-to for colleagues, reporting a bug with steps, explaining an assignment to students, capturing an online class you are allowed to record, or sending a client a walkthrough of a website draft.

6. Click **Stop** in the recorder tab, or the browser’s own **Stop sharing** button.
7. Preview the result and download it. If the file is WebM and you need MP4, click **Convert to MP4**.

## WebM or MP4: which do you need?

Browsers record using the formats they support natively. Most produce **WebM**, usually with the VP9 or VP8 video codec and Opus audio. Some newer browsers can produce **MP4** directly.

A quick explanation of the terms: the **container** (WebM, MP4) is the file wrapper, and the **codec** (VP9, H.264) is how the pictures are actually compressed inside it. WebM plays well in Chrome, Firefox, Edge and VLC, and on YouTube. MP4 with H.264 is the safest choice for WhatsApp, PowerPoint, iPhones and older smart TVs.

The **Convert to MP4** button re-encodes your recording to H.264 on your own computer. The first time, it downloads a video engine of about 32 MB, which your browser then caches. For other formats, or to pull out just the audio as MP3, use the [Video Converter & MP3](/tools/video-converter/).

## Tips for clear, professional recordings

- **Record a tab or window, not the whole screen,** unless you need to switch apps. It avoids accidentally showing WhatsApp Web, email previews or other private notifications.
- **Turn on Do Not Disturb** in Windows or macOS before you start, for the same reason.
- **Zoom the browser to 125% or more** when recording web pages, so text is readable on a phone.
- **Use a headset microphone if you can.** Laptop microphones pick up fan noise and room echo. Speak a little slower than normal.
- **Write three bullet points before you hit record.** Short, planned videos are far more useful than long, wandering ones.
- **Keep the webcam bubble small** and in a corner that does not cover buttons you will click.
- **Close heavy tabs and apps.** Recording and encoding use your processor, and a slow laptop may drop frames if it is also running many other things.

## Common problems and fixes

- **"This browser does not let websites record the screen."** You are probably on a phone or tablet, or using an older browser. Switch to a desktop browser.
- **No sound in the recording.** Check that **Record system / tab sound** was ticked and that you ticked "share audio" in the browser’s dialog. If you shared a whole screen on a Mac, try sharing the specific tab instead.
- **Microphone or camera blocked.** Click the camera or lock icon in the address bar and allow access, then try again. Make sure another app such as Zoom or Teams is not holding the camera.
- **Recording is shorter than expected.** If you clicked **Stop sharing** or closed the shared window, recording ends automatically.
- **Choppy video.** Lower your screen resolution, close other apps, or record a single tab instead of the whole screen.

## Privacy

Your recording is captured and encoded by your own browser and stays in that tab until you download it. Nothing is uploaded, there is no account, and no watermark is added. Remember to respect other people’s privacy too: get consent before recording meetings or calls that include others.
