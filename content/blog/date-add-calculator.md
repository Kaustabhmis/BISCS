To add days to a date, count forward that many calendar days. 90 days after 2 November 2026 is 31 January 2027. To add months or years, move the month or year forward and keep the same day of the month where it exists.

Doing this on a calendar is slow and easy to get wrong across month ends. The free [Add or Subtract Days](/tools/date-add-calculator/) calculator gives you the exact date and weekday instantly, and can count business days that skip weekends.

## Why add or subtract days?

Most deadlines in work and personal life are written as "within N days" or "N months from". Common examples in India include:

- Replying to a legal or tax notice within a stated number of days.
- A 30, 60 or 90-day notice period at work.
- Product warranties of one or two years from the purchase date.
- Payment terms such as "Net 30" on invoices.
- Cheque validity, which is three months from the date on the cheque under current RBI rules.
- Planning backwards: sending wedding invitations 45 days before the event.

## Worked examples

### 90 days from a date

Start: Monday, 2 November 2026. Add 90 days. Result: **Sunday, 31 January 2027**. Because it is a Sunday, you might plan to finish the task by Friday, 29 January.

### 30 days for payment terms

An invoice dated 2 November 2026 with "Net 30" terms is due on **Wednesday, 2 December 2026**.

### Counting back from an event

A wedding is on Sunday, 20 December 2026, and you want invitations out 45 days before. Choose Subtract and 45 days. The answer is **Thursday, 5 November 2026**.

### Ten business days

A vendor promises delivery in 10 business days from Friday, 6 November 2026. With business days ticked, the result is **Friday, 20 November 2026**, because the two weekends in between are skipped. Subtract any holidays yourself.

### Adding a month at month-end

31 January 2027 plus one month gives **28 February 2027**, since February 2027 has 28 days. In a leap year such as 2028, the result would be 29 February.

## How months, weeks and days are combined

When you fill in more than one box, the tool applies them in a fixed order:

1. **Years and months first.** It moves the month forward and keeps the day number. If that day does not exist in the new month, it uses the last day of the month.
2. **Then weeks**, as blocks of 7 days.
3. **Then days**, either as calendar days or as business days if you ticked that option.

This order matters. "1 month and 1 day" from 31 January becomes 28 February, then 1 March. Doing the day first would give a different answer.

## Days, months or business days: which to use?

| Wording you see | What to enter | Note |
|---|---|---|
| "within 30 days" | 30 in Days | Calendar days, weekends included |
| "within one month" | 1 in Months | Same date next month, or month-end |
| "within 10 working days" | 10 in Days, business days ticked | Remove holidays separately |
| "valid for 1 year" | 1 in Years | 29 February start dates move to 28 February |
| "6 weeks from" | 6 in Weeks | Exactly 42 days |

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Treating 30 days as a month.** They differ in most months.
- **Counting the start date as day one.** Usually, "within 7 days of 1 March" means up to 8 March. Check the wording of your document.
- **Forgetting weekends** for working-day promises.
- **Ignoring holidays,** especially around Diwali, Durga Puja, Eid, Christmas and regional festivals, when offices and banks may be closed.
- **Assuming the deadline moves** if it falls on a weekend. Some rules extend to the next working day, others do not. Read the rule that applies to you.

## What about leap years and long durations?

Leap years add a 29 February every four years, with the century rule that 1900 and 2100 are not leap years but 2000 was. The calculator follows the real calendar, so adding 365 days does not always land on the same date next year. From 1 March 2027, 365 days later is 29 February 2028, while adding 1 year gives 1 March 2028.

For long plans, such as a five-year bond maturity or a ten-year document renewal, use the Years box rather than converting to days. It keeps the same calendar date, which is what most agreements mean. If you started on 29 February, a year later the tool uses 28 February.
