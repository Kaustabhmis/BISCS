To reduce a PDF's file size, open [Compress PDF](/tools/compress-pdf/), choose a level (Basic, Strong or Extreme) and download the smaller file. Basic is lossless and keeps text perfect; Strong and Extreme turn pages into compressed images, which shrinks scanned PDFs dramatically.

Everything happens in your browser, so even a bank statement or an Aadhaar scan is compressed without being uploaded anywhere.

## Why PDFs are often too big

PDF size mostly comes from images. A typed 20-page document might be 200 KB, while a 3-page scan from a phone or copier can easily be several MB, because each page is a high-resolution photo.

That becomes a problem when:

- An email bounces because the attachment is over the provider's limit.
- A government, university or recruitment portal rejects your upload for being "too large".
- A WhatsApp group admin asks you to stop sending 20 MB files.
- Your cloud storage or phone fills up with old scans.

## The three compression levels explained

| Level | What it does | Text stays selectable? | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic (lossless) | Re-packs the file structure; content untouched | Yes | Digital PDFs, already-optimised files, when quality must not change |
| Strong | Each page becomes a 150 DPI image at 70% JPEG quality | No | Scans, phone photos, image-heavy PDFs |
| Extreme | Each page becomes a 100 DPI image at 50% JPEG quality | No | When you must hit a very small size and quality is secondary |

Basic also offers an option to **remove document metadata** (title, author, software names), which shaves a little more and removes details you may not want to share.

With Strong and Extreme, the tool warns you clearly that text will no longer be selectable or searchable and that links and form fields are removed. That trade-off is fine for a scanned mark sheet, but not for a contract someone needs to search.

## How to compress a PDF step by step

1. Open [Compress PDF](/tools/compress-pdf/) and choose your PDF.
2. Pick a level. Start with **Basic** for digital documents, or **Strong** for scans.
3. For Basic, decide whether to also strip metadata.
4. Click compress. A progress bar shows each page being processed.
5. Compare the **Original**, **Compressed** and **Saved** figures on the result screen.
6. Open the file and zoom in on small text to make sure it is still legible.
7. If it is still too large, try the next level, or remove unneeded pages first.

## Meeting government portal limits

Indian portals for exams, scholarships, recruitment, passports, tax and company filings commonly cap file sizes, and the caps differ widely. Some allow only a few hundred KB per document, others a few MB, and many also specify the format (PDF only, or JPG for photos and signatures). There is no national standard, and limits can change, so always check the instructions on the exact portal and upload field you are using.

A practical approach:

1. Note the exact limit and format from the portal.
2. Remove pages you do not need with [Remove PDF Pages](/tools/remove-pdf-pages/).
3. Compress with Strong if the document is a scan.
4. Aim for a size comfortably below the limit, not just under it.
5. Open the result and check that stamps, signatures and small print are readable.

If a portal wants an image rather than a PDF, convert a page with [PDF to Images](/tools/pdf-to-jpg/) and then use the image tools.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Using Extreme by default.** It can make fine print blurry. A rejected document because a seal is unreadable is worse than a slightly larger file.
- **Compressing a document that must stay searchable.** Use Basic for contracts, reports and anything others will copy text from.
- **Compressing the same file repeatedly.** Each image-based pass lowers quality again. Always compress from the original.
- **Ignoring a larger result.** If the tool says the output is bigger, keep your original.
- **Compressing fillable forms with Strong.** Form fields are removed. Fill the form first with Fill PDF Form, or compress with Basic only.

## Tips for the smallest file with readable text

- Scan at a sensible resolution in the first place. For forms and letters, very high scanner settings mostly add size.
- Black-and-white documents compress well. If colour is not required, [Grayscale PDF](/tools/grayscale-pdf/) can help, and it also redraws pages as images, so compare results.
- Split very large documents if the portal allows multiple uploads.
- Remove blank pages from double-sided scans before compressing.

## Example: fitting a scanned certificate under a portal limit

Sneha in Jaipur is applying for a state government job. The portal asks for her graduation certificate as a PDF, with a maximum size stated on the upload page. Her phone scan of the two-page certificate is 3.8 MB, well over the limit.

She tries Basic first. It saves only a little, because almost all of the file is two large photos. She then tries Strong, which redraws each page at 150 DPI, and the file drops to a fraction of the original size. She opens the result, zooms in on the university seal and the registration number, and both are clear. She uploads it with room to spare.

Had Strong still been too large, she could have tried Extreme and checked readability again, or re-scanned the certificate with better lighting and a plain background, which compresses far better than a shadowy photo.

## Basic or Strong: a quick way to decide

Open your PDF in any reader and try to select a word of text. If you can select it, the PDF is digital, and Basic is the safe first choice. If the whole page selects as a single picture, or nothing selects at all, it is a scan, and Strong will usually save much more. Many real-world files are a mix, such as a typed form with a scanned photo attached, so do not hesitate to try both and compare the numbers on the result screen.

## What happens on your device

The compressor uses a PDF library and your browser's own image engine. Basic re-saves the file structure; Strong and Extreme render each page and save it as a JPEG inside a new PDF. All of this happens in the browser tab, which is why big scanned files take longer on a phone than on a laptop, and why nothing is ever uploaded.
