## Everyday conversions with worked examples

### Height in feet and inches

A height of 5 feet 8 inches is 68 inches. Multiply by 2.54 to get **172.72 cm**. Doctors, passport forms and fitness apps often want centimetres, while many people in India still describe height in feet.

### Flat size in square feet and square metres

A 1,200 sq ft flat is 1,200 × 0.0929 = about **111.48 sq m**. Builders usually advertise in square feet, while some official documents use square metres, so it helps to check both.

### Indian land units

These are fixed in the converter:

- 1 acre = 43,560 sq ft = 100 cents = 40 guntha
- 1 cent = 435.6 sq ft
- 1 guntha = 1,089 sq ft
- 1 sq yard (gaj) = 9 sq ft
- 1 hectare ≈ 2.471 acres

Units like **bigha, biswa and kanal** are deliberately left out because their size varies by state. For these, rely on the value used by your state's land records or a local surveyor.

### Gold and weight

The converter uses the standard Indian **tola of 11.6638 g**. Gold is usually priced per 10 g, which is about 0.857 tola. One **quintal** is 100 kg, the unit in which mandi prices and MSP for crops are commonly quoted.

### Temperature

To convert Celsius to Fahrenheit, multiply by 9/5 and add 32. A body temperature of 37 °C is 98.6 °F. The tool warns you if a value is below absolute zero, −273.15 °C.

### Speed and fuel

100 km/h is about 62.14 mph. For fuel economy from UK or US sources, note that a UK gallon (4.546 litres) is larger than a US gallon (3.785 litres), so the same car gets a higher mpg figure in UK terms.

### Phone and laptop storage

A 1 TB drive holds 1,000,000,000,000 bytes, which your computer may report as about **931 GiB**. The converter lists both decimal units (KB, MB, GB) and binary units (KiB, MiB, GiB) so you can see the difference.

## Common conversion mistakes

- **Converting area with a length factor.** 1 m is 3.28 ft, but 1 sq m is 10.76 sq ft, because you square the factor.
- **Mixing US and UK units,** especially gallons, pints and fluid ounces.
- **Assuming a bigha is the same everywhere.** It is not.
- **Rounding too early.** Keep extra decimals until the final answer, especially for land and gold, where small differences cost money.
- **Adding 32 before multiplying** when converting Celsius to Fahrenheit. Multiply first, then add.
- **Confusing GB and GiB** when checking storage or download sizes.

## Metric and imperial: why India uses both

India adopted the metric system officially decades ago, and science, trade and government records use it. Yet older units survive in daily speech. People quote height in feet and inches, TV and phone screens in inches, flats in square feet, and land in acres, cents, guntha or bigha depending on the region.

At the same time, many online recipes, product listings and fitness apps come from the US or UK, where pounds, ounces, cups and miles are common. A converter that holds both systems side by side, with the Indian units added, saves you from searching for a different factor every time.
