Before you sign anything important, though, it is worth knowing what kind of signature the other side expects. A picture of your signature is fine for many purposes, but not all.

## Signature image, e-sign or DSC: which one do you need?

People use "e-sign" loosely, so here is a plain-language comparison. This is general information, not legal advice.

| Type | What it is | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|
| Signature image (drawn, typed or scanned) | A picture of your signature placed on the PDF | Offer letter acceptance, rent agreements between individuals, internal approvals, school and society forms, vendor quotations |
| Aadhaar eSign | An electronic signature issued through a licensed eSign service after Aadhaar-based authentication | Many online agreements and applications that ask specifically for eSign |
| Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) | A cryptographic certificate from a licensed Certifying Authority, usually on a USB token | Company law (MCA) filings, many tax and tender portals, documents that must be tamper-evident |

India's Information Technology Act, 2000 recognises electronic signatures, and the cryptographic kinds (DSC and eSign) can show who signed and whether the file changed after signing. A signature image cannot do that; it is evidence of intent, much like a scanned signed page.

So the practical rule is simple: **if the portal, bank, employer or authority asks for a DSC or eSign, a drawn signature will not do.** If they just ask you to "sign and send back", a signature image is usually what they mean. Some documents, such as wills and property sale deeds, have their own formalities, so take proper advice for those. When in doubt, ask the receiving party what they accept.

## How to sign a PDF step by step

1. Open [Sign PDF](/tools/sign-pdf/) and choose the PDF you need to sign.
2. Click **Sign** in the toolbar. A signature window opens with three tabs.
3. Choose how to create your signature:
   - **Draw**: sign with your mouse, trackpad, finger or stylus, and pick an ink colour.
   - **Type**: type your name and choose one of four handwriting-style fonts.
   - **Upload**: add a photo or scan of your real signature; tick **Remove white background** for a clean result.
4. Confirm, then drag the signature onto the signature line and resize it from the corners.
5. Use **Add text** to type the date and place, and **Tick** for any checkboxes.
6. Scroll through all pages. Many agreements need a signature or initials on every page, so duplicate the signature (Ctrl+D) as needed.
7. Click **Apply & download** to save the signed copy.

## Tips for a signature that looks genuine

**Drawing on a phone is easier than with a mouse.** If you are on a laptop, consider opening the tool on your phone and signing with your finger, or use a stylus on a tablet.

**An uploaded signature looks most natural.** Sign on clean white paper with a blue or black gel pen, take a photo in daylight with no shadow, and upload it with background removal on. If you sign PDFs often, create a reusable transparent PNG once with the [Signature Maker](/tools/signature-maker/).

**Keep the size realistic.** A signature that spills over the printed line or is tiny in a corner draws attention. Resize it to roughly match the space provided.

**Initials on every page.** For rent agreements and vendor contracts, it is common to initial each page. Create a second, smaller signature with just your initials.

## Common mistakes when signing PDFs

- **Signing the wrong version.** Check the date and the final clauses before signing. Once you send a signed PDF, it is hard to take back.
- **Using a signature image where a DSC is mandatory.** Government and company filings that require a DSC will reject a signed image.
- **Sharing your signature file casually.** A clean PNG of your signature is valuable. Do not post it in group chats or leave it in shared folders.
- **Forgetting the date.** Many documents are incomplete without the date and place next to the signature.
- **Signing a scanned page that is crooked.** If the document is a phone photo, clean it up first with [Scan to PDF](/tools/scan-to-pdf/) so the final copy looks professional.

## A quick checklist before you hit download

- The signature sits on the correct line, on the correct page.
- Initials appear on every page if the document asks for them.
- The date, place and your printed name are filled in.
- No stray tick marks or test signatures are left on the page.
- You have kept the unsigned original, in case a correction is needed later.
- You know whether the receiver accepts a signature image, or needs a DSC or eSign instead.

## Office workflows: approvals and onboarding

Small offices often route purchase orders, leave forms and vendor quotations for a manager's signature. Instead of printing, signing and scanning, the approver can open the PDF, add their signature and the date, and send it back in a couple of minutes. HR teams can send offer letters as PDFs and ask candidates to sign and return them the same way.

For anything with a legal or regulatory requirement for a particular signature type, follow that requirement. For everything else, signing in the browser saves paper, printer trips and time.
