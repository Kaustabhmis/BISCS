Compressing an image means making its file smaller, measured in KB or MB, while keeping it looking as close to the original as possible. You need it when a form rejects a photo for being too large, when an email bounces because of attachment size, or when a website loads slowly because of heavy pictures.

The [Compress Image](/tools/image-compressor/) tool from BISCS India handles JPG, PNG and WebP. You can either set a quality level yourself or type a target such as 50 KB and let the tool work out the best settings that fit.

## How does image compression actually work?

There are three ways to make an image file smaller, and good compressors combine them:

1. **Lower the encoding quality.** JPG and WebP throw away fine detail the eye barely notices. A quality of 75% often halves the file with no visible change.
2. **Reduce the dimensions.** A 4000 × 3000 photo has 12 million pixels. At 1600 × 1200 it has under 2 million, so even at the same quality the file is far smaller.
3. **Use a more efficient format.** A camera photo saved as PNG can be several times larger than the same photo as JPG. WebP is usually smaller still.

The target size mode in the BISCS India tool searches through quality levels to find the best one under your limit. Only if quality alone cannot get there does it start reducing the dimensions.

## Step by step: compress a photo to a target size

1. Open [Compress Image](/tools/image-compressor/) and add your photos. You can drop in several at once or paste an image with Ctrl + V.
2. Choose the mode at the top of the panel:
   - **Quality** if you just want smaller files and are happy to set the slider yourself.
   - **Target size** if a portal or app gives you a KB limit.
3. In Target size mode, type the limit in KB or tap a chip: 20 KB, 50 KB, 100 KB, 200 KB, 500 KB or 1 MB.
4. Optionally set a **Max width / height**, such as 1920 px for websites or 1280 px for email.
5. Pick the **Output format**. Auto keeps JPG for photos, uses WebP for images with transparency, and converts opaque PNGs to JPG.
6. Drag the comparison slider across the preview to check quality, read the before and after sizes, and download. Batches download as a ZIP.

## Compressing photos and signatures for Indian application forms

Recruitment and admission portals, such as those for SSC, UPSC, IBPS and other bank exams, state public service commissions and university admissions, almost always set a size range in KB for the photograph and the signature. Often there is both a minimum and a maximum, and sometimes fixed pixel dimensions as well.

These limits are not the same everywhere, and they change between notifications. Always read the current official notice or the portal’s upload instructions before you start, rather than relying on a number you saw on a forum.

A reliable workflow:

1. Crop the photo so the face is well framed, using [Crop Image](/tools/image-cropper/).
2. If dimensions are specified, set them exactly with [Resize Image](/tools/image-resizer/).
3. Compress with Target size set a little below the maximum, for example a few KB under it, to leave a safety margin.
4. Check that the result is above the minimum. If it is not, raise the target or the quality.
5. Make sure the format matches what the portal accepts, which is usually JPG.

The same approach works for scanned signatures, caste or income certificates saved as images, and thumb impressions.

## Practical tips

- **Start from the best original.** Compressing a photo that WhatsApp has already compressed piles new losses on old ones. Use the original camera file where possible.
- **Crop before you compress.** Removing empty wall or sky reduces the pixels the compressor has to fit into your limit, so the important part keeps more quality.
- **Screenshots are special.** Text-heavy screenshots look best as PNG or at high JPG quality. If they must be tiny, reduce the width rather than crushing the quality.
- **Watch the Saved figure.** If a file shows “Already optimised”, it was already efficient and further squeezing would only make it worse.
- **For PDFs, use a different tool.** If you need a smaller PDF rather than a smaller image, [Compress PDF](/tools/compress-pdf/) is designed for that.

## Common mistakes

- **Aiming exactly at the limit.** Some portals count KB slightly differently. A file of exactly the maximum can still be rejected, so leave a small margin.
- **Compressing again and again.** Each pass at low quality adds more damage. If the result is too big, go back to the original and set a smaller target.
- **Ignoring the minimum size.** A 6 KB photo for a form that wants at least 20 KB will be refused just as firmly as an oversized one.
- **Choosing PNG for a photo and wondering why it will not shrink.** Switch to JPG or Auto.
- **Over-compressing a signature.** Thin pen strokes break up at low quality. Crop tightly and keep quality reasonable instead.

## Does it work offline and is it private?

The compression runs in your browser using its built-in image encoder. Your photos are not uploaded to any server, so personal pictures, ID scans and documents stay on your device. Large batches of high-resolution photos may take a little longer on an older phone, simply because the work is being done locally rather than on a remote machine.
