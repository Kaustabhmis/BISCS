Most viewers read only the first line or two of a YouTube description, so that opening must explain the video on its own. Everything below it, such as chapters, links, credits and hashtags, is reference material for the people who expand the box and for YouTube's understanding of the video.

The free [YouTube Description Generator](/tools/youtube-description-generator/) puts those pieces in a sensible order for you. You fill in what you have, leave the rest blank, and copy a finished description that fits within YouTube's 5,000-character limit.

## The anatomy of a good description

Think of a description in three layers.

**The hook (first ~150 characters).** This shows before the "…more" link and often in search results. Say what the viewer will learn or see, using the words they would search for.

**The body.** Key points, chapters with timestamps, and the links you mentioned in the video. This is where a viewer comes to find the product you reviewed or the template you promised.

**The footer.** Subscribe link, social profiles, credits for music or footage, business contact details and a handful of hashtags.

A description does not need to be long. A two-line summary with chapters and three links is better than 4,000 characters of repeated keywords.

## Getting chapters right the first time

Chapters split the video timeline into labelled sections, which helps viewers jump to the part they need. YouTube only turns timestamps into chapters when they follow its rules, and the generator checks each one live:

- The first timestamp is **0:00**.
- There are **at least three** timestamps.
- They are in **increasing order**.
- Each chapter is **at least 10 seconds** long.

A green tick appears next to each rule you meet and a cross next to any you miss, with the name of a chapter that is too short. If a line cannot be read, the tool tells you its line number. Formats like `1:30 Setup`, `Setup - 1:30` and pasted spreadsheet rows all work. Keep chapter titles short and descriptive, such as "Installing the app" rather than "Part 2".

## What the warnings mean

Under the preview, the tool flags common problems before you paste:

- **Over 5,000 characters**, with the number to remove.
- **The characters < and >**, which YouTube does not accept in descriptions.
- **More than 15 hashtags**, as a gentle nudge, and **more than 60**, because YouTube then ignores every hashtag on the video.
- **Links without https://**, which may not become clickable.
- **An invalid-looking contact email** or **affiliate links with no disclosure text**.

The meter turns amber as you approach the limit and red when you pass it.

## Habits that make descriptions more useful

- Write the summary for a human first. If it reads naturally and includes the topic, it is doing its job.
- Repeat the most important link near the top for tutorials that depend on a download or a form.
- Use the same footer on every video so regular viewers know where to look.
- Credit music and footage exactly as the licence asks. A CC BY track, for instance, usually needs the title, artist, licence and a link.
- For business channels, the Business template puts contact details and highlights first, which suits service videos aimed at local customers.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Starting with "Hi guys, welcome back".** It wastes the only lines most people see.
- **Keyword stuffing.** Long lists of unrelated search terms can break YouTube's spam policies.
- **Dead or untracked links.** Check each one before publishing; for campaign links, add UTM parameters with the [UTM Link Builder](/tools/utm-builder/) so you can see what YouTube traffic does on your site.
- **Forgetting the disclosure.** Affiliate and sponsored content should be clearly marked, both in the text and in YouTube Studio where it applies.
- **Hashtags that do not match the video.** They confuse viewers and can count as misleading metadata.
