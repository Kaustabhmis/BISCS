A rent receipt is a simple document in which your landlord confirms that they received a stated amount of rent from you for a particular month and property. Salaried employees in India use them mainly to claim House Rent Allowance (HRA) exemption, by submitting them to their employer or keeping them as proof for their tax return.

The fastest way to create them is with a generator: enter the details once, choose the months, print, and get your landlord to sign. Here is how, and what the rules expect.

## What should a rent receipt include?

A receipt that most employers accept has:

- the **tenant's name**;
- the **landlord's name**, and **PAN** where required;
- the **address of the rented property**;
- the **rent amount**, in figures and words;
- the **month or period** covered;
- the **payment mode** (cash, UPI, bank transfer or cheque) and a reference number if available;
- a **receipt number** and **date**;
- the **landlord's signature**, and a revenue stamp where applicable.

## How HRA exemption works

HRA exemption is available **only under the old tax regime**. Under the new regime, which is now the default, HRA received is fully taxable and rent receipts do not reduce your tax.

Under the old regime, the exempt part of HRA is the lowest of:

1. the actual HRA you received;
2. rent paid minus 10% of salary (Basic plus DA);
3. 50% of salary if you live in a city classed as a metro for this purpose, or 40% elsewhere.

The list of metro cities is set by the tax rules, and has traditionally been Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai; check the current list for the year you are claiming. ## When is the landlord's PAN required?

Employers generally ask for the landlord's PAN when the **annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000**, roughly ₹8,333 a month. The generator warns you if your rent crosses this level without a PAN, and checks that the PAN you enter has the right shape (five letters, four digits, one letter).

If your landlord genuinely does not have a PAN, a signed declaration from them saying so is usually accepted. You can check the format of a PAN with the [PAN & IFSC Checker](/tools/pan-ifsc-validator/), though that only confirms the pattern, not that the PAN belongs to your landlord.

## Do you need a revenue stamp?

It is common practice, and many employers expect it, to affix a **₹1 revenue stamp** on a rent receipt when the rent is **paid in cash and exceeds ₹5,000**, with the landlord signing across the stamp. The tool shows a reminder in that situation.

Stamp duty on receipts is governed by stamp law, and practice varies, so if your HR team or tax adviser asks for something different, follow them. Payments by bank transfer or UPI leave their own trail, which is the stronger evidence in any case. Revenue stamps are usually sold at post offices.

## Common mistakes that get HRA claims questioned

- **Receipts that do not match your bank statement.** If you pay by UPI, the receipts should show the same amounts.
- **Missing landlord signature.** An unsigned receipt is just a printout.
- **Wrong property address.** It should be the house you live in, not your permanent address.
- **Claiming HRA for a house you own and live in.** HRA is for rented accommodation.
- **Rent paid to your spouse.** Tax authorities generally do not accept this.
- **Round figures with no rental agreement** for high rents. Keep the agreement, especially if rent is substantial.
- **Choosing the new regime and still submitting receipts.** They will not reduce tax under that regime.

## Tips for tenants

- **Pay by bank transfer or UPI** where possible. It is the easiest proof that rent was actually paid.
- **Generate the whole year at once** and get them signed together, rather than chasing the landlord every month.
- **Keep scanned copies.** Many employers want digital proofs uploaded to a portal; scan signed receipts and combine them into one file with [Merge PDF](/tools/merge-pdf/) if needed.
- **Missed the employer deadline?** Under the old regime, you can usually still claim the exemption when filing your income tax return, so keep the receipts and agreement safe.
- **Rent to parents** can be valid if a parent owns the home, the rent is genuinely paid, and the parent declares it as income.
