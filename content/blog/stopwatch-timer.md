An **online stopwatch** counts up from zero to measure how long something takes. A **countdown timer** counts down from a time you set and sounds an alarm at zero. A **Pomodoro timer** cycles through focus sessions and breaks to help you study or work in steady bursts.

The free [Stopwatch & Timer](/tools/stopwatch-timer/) puts all three on one page. There is nothing to install, it works on phones and computers, and it shows the time left in your browser tab so you can glance at it while doing something else.

## Stopwatch, timer or Pomodoro: which do you need?

| Mode | Best for | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Stopwatch | Measuring how long something takes | Millisecond display, laps with best and worst highlighted, keyboard shortcuts |
| Timer | Stopping at a fixed time | Hours, minutes and seconds, presets from 1 minute to 1 hour, progress ring, alarm |
| Pomodoro | Focused study or work | Focus, short break and long break phases, auto-start, session counter |

## How to use the stopwatch

1. Open the [Stopwatch & Timer](/tools/stopwatch-timer/). The **Stopwatch** tab is selected.
2. Press **Start** or the **Space** key.
3. Press **Lap** or **L** to record a split. Each lap shows its own time and the running total.
4. Press **Pause**, then **Resume** to continue.
5. Press **Reset** or **R** to clear everything.

With two or more laps, the fastest lap is highlighted as best and the slowest as worst, which is useful for running, swimming or practising a speech section by section.

## How to use the countdown timer

1. Switch to the **Timer** tab.
2. Enter hours, minutes and seconds, or tap a preset: 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30 or 45 minutes, or 1 hour.
3. Press **Start**. The ring empties as time passes, and the browser tab title shows the remaining time.
4. Press **Pause** if you are interrupted, and **Resume** when ready.
5. At zero you hear four beeps, see a "Time's up!" message, and the tab title changes so you notice even from another tab.

## How to use the Pomodoro timer

1. Switch to the **Pomodoro** tab.
2. Keep the classic settings of **25 minutes focus, 5 minutes short break and 15 minutes long break**, or change them.
3. Leave **Start the next session automatically** ticked if you want a continuous cycle.
4. Press **Start** and work on a single task until the bell.
5. Take the break when it begins. After every fourth focus session, a long break follows.
6. Use **Skip** to jump to the next phase, or **Reset** to start the cycle again.

Dots and a counter show how many focus sessions you have completed, which is a satisfying record of a productive day.

## Practical ways to use it

### Study and exam practice

Board exam and competitive exam preparation depends on speed as much as knowledge. Set the timer to the section's time limit and solve a past paper under real pressure. Use the stopwatch to see how long each question type takes, then focus practice where you are slowest.

### Workouts

Time planks, skipping rounds or rest periods. For interval training, the Pomodoro tab with custom durations such as 1 minute of work and 1 minute of rest can act as a simple interval timer.

### Cooking

Pressure cooker whistles are one thing; resting dough, soaking rice or boiling eggs need a timer. Set it and get on with something else.

### Presentations and meetings

Rehearse a talk with laps for each slide, or run a visible countdown so a meeting keeps to time.

### Screen breaks

Long hours at a screen can strain your eyes. A common suggestion is the 20-20-20 habit: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for 20 seconds. A 20-minute timer makes it easy to remember.

## Does the timer stay accurate in the background?

Browsers slow down pages in background tabs to save battery. Many simple timers count ticks and drift as a result. This tool records the exact end time when you press Start and compares it with the clock, so the countdown stays accurate whether the tab is visible or not. A background check also keeps the tab title and alarm on schedule.

There are limits worth knowing:

- **Closing or refreshing the tab** stops the timer and clears it.
- **A sleeping laptop or locked phone** may pause the browser, so the alarm can be late or silent.
- **Muted devices** will not play the beeps. Check your volume first.

For important alarms, keep the screen on and the tab open, or use your phone's built-in clock app as a backup.

## Reading your lap times

Laps are more useful than a single total when you want to improve. Suppose you run four laps of a park track and record 2:05, 1:58, 2:11 and 2:24. The tool marks 1:58 as your best lap and 2:24 as your worst. The pattern shows you started a little fast and faded at the end, so next time you might hold back slightly on the first two laps.

The same idea works for a mock exam: tap Lap after each section to see where your time actually goes.

## Common mistakes

- Pressing Start before setting the time, then wondering why it shows the previous value. Set first, then start.
- Leaving the device on silent.
- Treating the Pomodoro as rigid. If 25 minutes is too long or short for you, adjust it.
- Skipping breaks. The method works because breaks are real breaks, away from the screen.
- Expecting official timing. Use certified equipment for competitions.
