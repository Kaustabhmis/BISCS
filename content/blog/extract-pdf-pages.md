To extract pages from a PDF, open [Extract PDF Pages](/tools/extract-pdf-pages/), click the pages you need (or type them as `1-3, 8`), and click **Extract pages**. You get a new, smaller PDF with only those pages. Prefer each page on its own? Tick **Save each page as a separate PDF** and download a ZIP instead.

Your original stays unchanged, the pages keep their full quality, and the document never leaves your device.

## Why extract instead of sending the whole file?

Sharing only what is needed is good practice, both for privacy and for the person receiving the file. A few situations where it helps:

- **Sending a single payslip.** HR sent a 12-month PDF, and the landlord's agent needs only the latest one.
- **Sharing one chapter.** A teacher wants pages 45 to 60 of a textbook PDF for a class worksheet.
- **Proof for a claim.** An insurance claim needs only the discharge summary and the final bill, not the full 30-page hospital file.
- **A single property document page.** A society office asks for the page showing the flat number and owner names, not the entire sale deed.
- **Interview preparation.** Pull the two relevant pages from a long company report to read on your phone.

## How to extract PDF pages step by step

1. Open [Extract PDF Pages](/tools/extract-pdf-pages/) and choose the PDF.
2. Click the thumbnails of the pages you want. Shift-click to select a whole run between two pages.
3. Or type page numbers and ranges in **Pages to extract**, for example `2, 5-9, 14`.
4. Use **Quick select** if helpful: All, None, Odd or Even.
5. Decide on the output: one combined PDF (default) or each page as a separate PDF in a ZIP.
6. Click **Extract pages** and download the result.

## Extract vs split vs remove

All three tools rebuild a PDF from chosen pages, but they suit different ways of thinking.

| Tool | You tell it… | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Extract PDF Pages | Which pages to **keep** | One new PDF, or one PDF per page |
| [Remove PDF Pages](/tools/remove-pdf-pages/) | Which pages to **drop** | One new PDF |
| [Split PDF](/tools/split-pdf/) | Where to **cut** the document | Several PDFs covering the whole document |

If you need 3 pages out of 80, extract. If you need 77 out of 80, remove. If you need all 80 broken into sections, split.

## Combining extract with other tools

Extraction is often the first step in a bigger task.

**Building an application bundle.** Extract the relevant pages from several long documents (one page from your passport scan, two pages from a bank statement), then join them with [Merge PDF](/tools/merge-pdf/).

**Making a printable handout.** Extract the pages, then add numbering with [Add Page Numbers](/tools/pdf-page-numbers/) so the new handout starts at page 1.

**Reordering.** Extracted pages are saved in document order. If you need a different order, open the new file in Organize PDF and drag the pages around.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Going by printed footer numbers.** Many reports start numbering after the cover and contents pages. Always use the position shown under each thumbnail.
- **Forgetting the separate-files option.** If you need ten pages as ten separate attachments, tick the option instead of extracting ten times.
- **Assuming click order sets page order.** The output follows the original document order.
- **Extracting part of a signed agreement as "proof".** A single page cut from a contract may not be accepted as the full document. Ask what the receiver actually needs.

## Privacy: share less, and keep it local

Extracting is itself a privacy tool. A loan document, a medical file or a property paper usually contains far more personal information than any single recipient needs. Sending only the relevant pages reduces what is exposed if an email is forwarded or a phone is lost.

The tool adds a second layer of privacy: processing happens in your browser. Your PDF is not uploaded, so there is no copy sitting on a server somewhere. This is particularly reassuring for documents downloaded from DigiLocker or other sources that carry Aadhaar or PAN details.

## Example: sending just the right pages to an employer

Karthik is switching jobs, and his new employer's HR team asks for "the last three months' payslips and your relieving letter". His old company's portal let him download the whole year's payslips as one 12-page PDF, and the relieving letter came as page 3 of a 5-page exit document.

He opens the payslip PDF, types `10-12` to select the last three months, and extracts them into a new file. Then he opens the exit document and extracts page 3 alone. Finally he merges the two results so HR receives a single, four-page PDF with exactly what they asked for and nothing else, no annual salary breakup, no tax projections, no personal exit feedback.

This is a small thing, but it is the kind of care that makes paperwork go smoothly, and it keeps the rest of your financial details to yourself.

## What happens to links, bookmarks and forms?

The extracted pages look exactly like the originals. However, document-level extras such as the bookmark panel, which point to the whole original file, are not rebuilt for the new document. For most everyday uses (payslips, bills, certificates), this makes no difference, because those files rarely have bookmarks to begin with.

## Tips for neat results

- Rename the extracted file before sending, for example `Payslip-October.pdf` rather than `salary-extracted.pdf`.
- For very large PDFs, typing ranges is faster than scrolling through hundreds of thumbnails.
- If the extracted pages are scans and the file is still large, run it through Compress PDF before emailing.
- Check the page count on the result screen; it confirms exactly how many pages were saved.
