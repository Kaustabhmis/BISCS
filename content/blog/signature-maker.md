To create a signature image, draw your signature with a mouse, finger or stylus, or type your name in a handwriting-style font, then download it as a **transparent PNG**. You can place that image on PDFs, letters, invoices and forms, and it will sit cleanly over the signature line without a white box.

The free [Signature Maker](/tools/signature-maker/) does this in your browser. Your signature is never uploaded, and the exported image is automatically trimmed and saved at high resolution.

**Important:** a signature image is not a **Digital Signature Certificate (DSC)** or **Aadhaar eSign**. Where a law, portal or contract requires those, an image will not do. More on this below.

## When is a signature image useful?

- Signing **PDF letters, quotations and offer letters** before emailing them.
- Adding a signature to **invoices, rent receipts and salary slips**.
- Signing **internal forms**, leave applications and declarations that accept scanned signatures.
- Creating an **email signature** or a sign-off for a letterhead template.
- Avoiding the print, sign and scan routine for everyday paperwork.

## How to draw your signature

1. Open the [Signature Maker](/tools/signature-maker/). The **Draw** tab is selected.
2. Pick an **ink colour**: black, blue, green, red, or a custom colour. Blue is common for signatures in India because it shows a signature is original rather than photocopied.
3. Set the **pen thickness**.
4. Sign above the line in the pad, using a mouse, a finger on a touchscreen, or a stylus.
5. Use **Undo** to remove the last stroke, or **Clear** to start again.
6. Press **Download PNG**. The image is trimmed to your signature with a small margin.

## How to type a signature

1. Switch to the **Type** tab.
2. Enter your name, up to 60 characters.
3. Choose one of six handwriting styles: **Great Vibes, Dancing Script, Allura, Sacramento, Satisfy or Homemade Apple**. Each button previews your own name.

A typed signature looks neat, but it will not match your real handwritten signature. Use it where a stylised name is acceptable, such as email sign-offs or informal documents, and draw your real signature where it needs to match your bank or ID records.

## Getting a clean, professional result

- **Sign big.** Use the full width of the pad; the export is scaled at high resolution and trimmed, so it stays crisp when shrunk on a page.
- **Use a stylus or finger** on a phone or tablet if you can. A mouse tends to make shaky lines.
- **Keep a steady pace.** Very fast strokes can look jagged; very slow ones can wobble.
- **Choose a slightly thicker pen** if your signature looks thin when placed on a document.
- **Leave transparency on** unless a system specifically needs a white background, in which case tick **White background instead of transparent**.
- **Save the PNG somewhere private,** such as an encrypted folder, not your phone's public gallery.

## Signature image, DSC or eSign: what is the difference?

| Method | What it is | Typical uses | Legal weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signature image | A picture of your signature placed on a document | Letters, quotations, internal forms, informal agreements | Depends on what the other party accepts; easy to copy |
| Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) | A cryptographic certificate issued by a licensed Certifying Authority, usually on a USB token | Company and tax filings, tenders, many government portals | Recognised under the Information Technology Act, 2000 |
| Aadhaar eSign | An online electronic signature authenticated with Aadhaar OTP through a licensed provider | Agreements, account opening, consent forms | Recognised under the Information Technology Act, 2000 |

A DSC or eSign is mathematically tied to the document, so any later change is detectable. An image can be copied onto any file by anyone who has it. So for contracts of real value or government filings that demand a DSC or eSign, use those methods. For everyday paperwork, a clean image is often all that is needed. When unsure, ask the person or organisation receiving the document what they accept.

## Placing your signature on different documents

- **PDFs:** use a PDF signing tool to drop the PNG on the signature line and resize it. Keep it roughly the size of a real handwritten signature, about 4 to 5 cm wide on an A4 page.
- **Word or Google Docs:** insert the PNG as an image, set text wrapping to "in front of text" so it can sit over the line, then resize.
- **Invoices and receipts:** many invoice and receipt templates have a signatory line. Place the signature just above it, next to the "For company name" text.
- **Scanned forms:** if you are signing a scanned page, make sure the signature colour contrasts with the scan, and export the final document as PDF.

Whatever the document, zoom in before sending to check that the signature is not covering important text and that the edges look clean.

## Common mistakes

- **Sending your signature PNG to people who do not need it.** Treat it like a key.
- **Using a typed font where your real signature is required,** such as bank documents.
- **Placing the image too large** so it overlaps text or the signature box.
- **Using a white background** on coloured letterheads, which leaves a visible box.
- **Assuming an image counts as a digital signature** for statutory filings.
- **Signing on a dirty or cracked touchscreen,** which causes broken strokes.

## Protecting your signature

- When you share a signed copy, send a PDF rather than the signature file itself.
- For documents you share widely, consider adding a visible watermark such as "Copy for KYC only – not valid for other use" with the [Image Watermark](/tools/image-watermark/) tool or [Watermark PDF](/tools/watermark-pdf/).
- Keep the original PNG offline and delete copies from downloads folders on shared computers.
