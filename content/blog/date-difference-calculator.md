To find the number of days between two dates, count the calendar days from the start date to the end date. If both the first and the last day should be counted, add one. From 1 January 2026 to 31 March 2026 is 89 days, or 90 days if you include 31 March.

That "plus one" question is where most counting mistakes happen. The free [Date Difference calculator](/tools/date-difference-calculator/) gives you a tick box for it, and shows the answer in days, weeks, months and working days at the same time.

## When do you need to count days between dates?

Day counts turn up in everyday Indian life more often than you might expect:

- **Notice periods** at work, often 30, 60 or 90 days.
- **Leave applications**, where both the first and last day are counted.
- **Interest calculations** on fixed deposits or short loans, which are based on the number of days.
- **Deadlines** for returns, replies to notices, refunds or warranty claims.
- **Project timelines** and how many working days a task really has.
- **Countdowns** to a wedding, exam or festival trip.

## Worked examples

### A quarter of the year

From 1 January 2026 to 31 March 2026:

- Without the end date: 89 days, which reads as 2 months and 30 days. There are 63 working days.
- With the end date: 90 days, exactly 3 months, with 64 working days (31 March 2026 is a Tuesday).

### A financial year

India's financial year runs from 1 April to 31 March. From 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 is 364 days exclusive, or 365 days when you include the last day. Since February 2027 has 28 days, the inclusive count is a normal 365-day year.

### A notice period

You resign on 2 November 2026 and your last working day is 31 January 2027. That is 90 days between the dates, with 65 Monday-to-Friday days. Your HR policy decides whether the notice is measured in calendar days or working days, so check which one your contract uses.

### A countdown

From 2 November 2026 to Christmas Day, 25 December 2026, is 53 days.

## Inclusive or exclusive: which should you use?

| Situation | Count the end date? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "How many days until…" | No | You want the gap between today and the event |
| Leave from 10 to 14 March | Yes | Both the 10th and 14th are days off |
| Hotel nights | No | Checking out on the last day is not a night stayed |
| Interest on a deposit | Usually no | Banks typically count the days money was held; check your bank's method |
| Event that runs "from … to …" | Yes | Both dates are part of the event |
| Age or tenure | No | Measured as elapsed time |

When in doubt, read the wording: "from X to Y inclusive" means both days count, while "within N days of X" normally starts counting the day after X. For legal or tax deadlines, follow the rule stated in the relevant law or notice.

## How working days are counted

The calculator counts **Monday to Friday** as working days and Saturday and Sunday as weekend days. It does not know about holidays, because national, state and company holidays vary across India. To get an accurate figure:

1. Note the working days shown by the tool.
2. Subtract public or company holidays that fall on weekdays in your range.
3. If your office works on Saturdays, or on alternate Saturdays, add those days back.

This is still much faster and safer than counting on a wall calendar.

## Common date-counting mistakes

- **Forgetting the plus one** for inclusive periods like leave or event days.
- **Assuming 30 days per month.** A "one month" notice from 31 January ends on a different day from one starting on 1 March.
- **Missing leap years.** 2028 has a 29 February, so spans crossing it gain a day.
- **Counting weekends as working days** in project plans.
- **Ignoring holidays** when estimating delivery or processing times.
- **Mixing date formats.** 03/04 can be 3 April or 4 March. A date picker avoids this.

## Tips for planning with date differences

- Use the **weeks** figure for things measured in weeks, such as pregnancy check-ups or course schedules.
- Use **total months** when billing or rent is monthly, but check how part-months are charged.
- For SLAs and turnaround times, rely on **working days**, then subtract holidays.
- Keep a note of whether a figure is inclusive or exclusive when you share it, so others read it the same way.
