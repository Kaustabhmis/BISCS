Adding music to a video comes down to three decisions: which track, how loud, and whether the original sound stays. Get the volume balance right and the music lifts the video; get it wrong and it drowns the person speaking.

The [Add Music to Video](/tools/add-music-to-video/) tool handles the mixing in your browser. You choose a music file, decide whether to keep the original audio, set both volumes and fades, and download a new MP4.

## First, the copyright question

This matters more than any setting. Platforms scan uploaded videos for copyrighted music. If they find a match, the outcome depends on the rights holder: the video may be muted, blocked in some countries, have its earnings sent to the rights holder, or attract a copyright claim. Repeated problems can lead to strikes.

Safer sources of music:

- **YouTube Audio Library**, found in YouTube Studio, with free tracks and sound effects. Check each track's licence, as some require you to credit the artist in the description.
- **Music you created**, or recorded with friends who agree to its use.
- **Licensed stock music** that you paid for or obtained under a clear licence.
- **Instagram's and Facebook's in-app music**, added inside those apps, where the platform has arranged licensing for use there.

Hit songs from films or albums are almost always protected, even if you only use a few seconds. "No copyright intended" notes do not change that.

## How to add music to a video

1. Open [Add Music to Video](/tools/add-music-to-video/) and choose your video (MP4, MOV, WebM or MKV).
2. Press **Choose music file** and pick a track. MP3, M4A, WAV, AAC and OGG are accepted. A small player lets you listen to it.
3. Under **Audio**, choose **Mix with original** to keep the existing sound, or **Replace original** to use the music alone.
4. With Mix selected, set **Original sound volume** (100% by default). Then set **Music volume** (60% by default). Both sliders go up to 200%.
5. Set **Fade in (s)** and **Fade out (s)**. The defaults are 1 and 2 seconds.
6. If the best part of the song starts later, use **Skip start of music (s)**. To bring the music in after an introduction, set **Music starts at (s)**.
7. If the track is shorter than the video, decide whether to tick **Loop music to the end of the video**.
8. Read the note under the settings, which explains whether music will repeat or be cut, and press **Add music**.
9. Preview the result and download the MP4.

The audio is encoded as AAC, which every major platform plays. The video engine that does the mixing downloads once (about 32 MB) and is cached by your browser; your files stay on your device throughout.

## Mix or replace?

| Situation | Choose | Typical settings |
|---|---|---|
| Vlog, tutorial or interview | Mix with original | Original 100%, music 15–30% |
| Travel montage with wind noise | Replace original | Music 80–100% |
| Product shots with no speech | Replace original | Music 70–100% |
| Event video with crowd sounds | Mix with original | Original 60–80%, music 40–60% |
| Old recording with a hum | Replace original | Music to taste |

These are starting points. Every track and recording is different, so listen and adjust.

## Getting the balance right

Speech sits in roughly the same frequency range as many instruments, so music competes with voices more than you might expect. A few practical habits:

- **Pick calm tracks for talking videos.** Instrumental music without vocals is far easier to talk over.
- **Check on a phone speaker.** Most short videos are watched on phones, where bass vanishes and mid-range music seems louder.
- **Use a gentle fade-in.** One or two seconds feels natural; zero can sound like a mistake.
- **End with the video, not the song.** A two- or three-second fade-out avoids an abrupt cut on the last frame.
- **Do not push past 100% unless needed.** Boosting quiet audio can cause distortion if the original was already loud.

## Timing the music to your video

Two settings help the music fit the story rather than simply running underneath it. **Skip start of music (s)** jumps past a slow introduction in the track, so a lively section begins with your first shot. **Music starts at (s)** delays the music, leaving the opening seconds with the original sound only, which suits a greeting or a title card before the montage begins. Combine both with fades and the music sounds as if it was edited by hand.

## Quality and speed

If your video is H.264 or HEVC, which covers most phone and camera recordings, the picture is copied straight across and only the sound is rebuilt. The result card then says the picture was copied without quality loss, and the whole job is quick even on a phone. Other formats are converted to H.264 MP4, which takes longer.

The video always keeps its original length. To make a clip shorter before adding music, use the [Video Trimmer](/tools/video-trimmer/) first.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Using a song just because it is trending.** Popularity does not make it free to use.
- **Burying the voice.** If viewers have to strain to hear, they leave.
- **Forgetting to mute the original when it is noisy.** Traffic and wind under music sounds messy; choose Replace instead.
- **Leaving the credit out.** If your Audio Library track requires attribution, paste it into the description.
- **Expecting music to rescue a weak video.** It sets a mood, but the content still has to hold attention.
