The number of calories you need each day depends on your body size, age, sex and how active you are. A common way to estimate it is to work out your **BMR** (the energy your body uses at rest) and multiply it by an **activity factor** to get your **TDEE** (total daily energy expenditure). Eating close to your TDEE keeps your weight roughly stable.

For a 30-year-old man who is 170 cm tall, weighs 70 kg and exercises lightly, that comes to about 2,224 kcal a day. The free [Calorie Calculator](/tools/calorie-calculator/) works this out for you in seconds.

**Please note:** these are estimates for healthy adults. They are not medical advice. If you have a health condition, are pregnant, or plan a significant change in diet, consult a doctor or registered dietitian.

## What are BMR and TDEE?

- **BMR (basal metabolic rate)** is the energy needed to keep you alive at complete rest: breathing, heartbeat, temperature and organ function. For most people it makes up the largest share of daily energy use.
- **TDEE (total daily energy expenditure)** is BMR plus everything else: walking, working, household chores, exercise and digesting food.

Your TDEE is your **maintenance calories**. Eat less than this on average and you tend to lose weight; eat more and you tend to gain.

## The formula behind the calculator

The BISCS tool uses the **Mifflin–St Jeor equation**, which is widely used by dietitians as a starting estimate:

- **Men:** BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5
- **Women:** BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

BMR is then multiplied by an activity factor:

| Activity level | Description | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| Sedentary | Little or no exercise | 1.2 |
| Light | Exercise 1–3 days a week | 1.375 |
| Moderate | Exercise 3–5 days a week | 1.55 |
| Active | Hard exercise 6–7 days a week | 1.725 |
| Very active | Physical job or training twice a day | 1.9 |

## Worked examples

### Arjun: light activity, wants to lose weight slowly

Arjun is 30, 170 cm and 70 kg, and goes to the gym twice a week.

- BMR = 700 + 1,062.5 − 150 + 5 = 1,617.5, shown as about 1,618 kcal.
- TDEE = 1,617.5 × 1.375 ≈ 2,224 kcal.
- A 500 kcal daily deficit gives a target of about 1,724 kcal.

At maintenance, the suggested split of 25% protein, 50% carbohydrate and 25% fat works out to about 139 g protein, 278 g carbohydrate and 62 g fat. These are starting points; a dietitian may adjust them for his goals.

### Meena: sedentary desk job

Meena is 28, 158 cm and 60 kg, and rarely exercises.

- BMR = 600 + 987.5 − 140 − 161 = 1,286.5, about 1,287 kcal.
- TDEE = 1,286.5 × 1.2 ≈ 1,544 kcal.
- A 500 kcal deficit would mean about 1,044 kcal a day.

The calculator marks that last figure as **low**, because it is below roughly 1,200 kcal. For Meena, a milder 250 kcal deficit (about 1,294 kcal) combined with more daily movement is a more sustainable option, and worth discussing with a professional.

## How fast can you safely lose or gain weight?

The goal table uses rough rules of thumb: a deficit of about 500 kcal a day is often linked to around 0.5 kg of loss per week, and 250 kcal to around 0.25 kg. Real results vary because water, muscle, hormones and how accurately you track food all play a part.

Gradual change is usually easier to sustain. Very low intakes can make it hard to get enough protein and micronutrients, and the tool deliberately flags targets below about 1,200 kcal for women and 1,500 kcal for men. Going under those levels should only happen with medical supervision.

## Common mistakes with calorie targets

- **Overstating activity.** Choosing "Active" because of one intense week inflates the number.
- **Treating the result as exact.** Formulas estimate averages; your real need may be a few hundred calories different.
- **Forgetting cooking oil, ghee and drinks.** Tea with sugar, juices and the oil in a tadka add up quickly.
- **Changing everything at once.** Adjust intake by small steps and watch the trend for two to four weeks.
- **Ignoring protein.** A low-calorie diet that is also low in protein can cost muscle.
- **Using it for children, teenagers or pregnancy.** These need professional guidance.

## Practical tips

- Weigh yourself at the same time of day, a few times a week, and look at the weekly average.
- Read nutrition labels on packaged foods; they list energy per serving and per 100 g.
- Recalculate after a change of 3 to 5 kg, since your needs change with your weight.
- Combine diet changes with activity. Even a daily brisk walk raises your TDEE.
