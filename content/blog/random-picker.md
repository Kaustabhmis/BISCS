A random name picker chooses one or more entries from a list so that **every name has an equal chance**. Paste the names, one per line, choose how many winners you need, and press the button. The same idea works for splitting a group into **random teams**.

The free [Random Name Picker](/tools/random-picker/) does both. It uses your browser's secure random generator, and your list stays on your device.

## Where a random picker helps

- **Lucky draws** at Diwali parties, office annual days, society events and school fairs.
- **Classrooms,** to choose who answers next, who presents first, or who reads aloud.
- **Team games,** from box cricket and football to quiz nights and hackathons.
- **Work rotas,** such as who hosts the next stand-up or brings the snacks.
- **Giveaways** for small businesses and social media pages.
- **Secret Santa** style ordering, when you just need a random sequence.

## How to pick a random winner

1. Open the [Random Name Picker](/tools/random-picker/).
2. Paste or type your **entries**, one per line. A counter shows how many there are.
3. Keep **Ignore duplicates** ticked to count each name once.
4. Choose **Pick winners** mode and set the **number of winners**.
5. Tick **Remove winners from the list** if you will run several rounds and nobody should win twice.
6. Press **Pick winner**. The names spin briefly, then the winners appear in order.
7. Use **Copy results** to paste the list into a chat or announcement.

The **Shuffle list** button randomly reorders your entries, which is handy for deciding a speaking order. **Clear** empties the box.

## How to make random teams

1. Paste the names as above.
2. Switch to **Make teams**.
3. Choose to split by **Number of teams** or by **People per team**.
4. Enter the number and press **Make teams**.
5. Each team appears in its own coloured card. Copy the results to share them.

The picker shuffles everyone first, then deals names into teams one at a time, like dealing cards. So team sizes never differ by more than one.

### Examples

- **12 students into 3 teams:** three teams of 4.
- **14 players, 4 per team:** the tool makes 4 teams, with two teams of 4 and two teams of 3.
- **22 people for two cricket sides:** two teams of 11.

## How fairness works

A fair picker needs two things.

**A good random source.** The tool uses `crypto.getRandomValues`, the browser's cryptographically secure generator, rather than a simple formula that can show patterns.

**An unbiased shuffle.** It uses the Fisher–Yates shuffle, which gives every possible ordering exactly the same chance. Winners are simply the first names in the shuffled list.

The rolling animation is decoration. The result is fixed the moment you press the button, so nobody can time a tap to land on a favourite.

## Running a lucky draw people trust

Fairness is also about being seen to be fair:

1. **Collect entries in writing** and share the final list before the draw.
2. **Decide the rules in advance:** number of prizes, one prize per person or not, and what happens if a winner is absent.
3. **Project the screen** so everyone watches the pick live.
4. **Draw the smallest prize first** if you want to keep suspense for the main prize, using "Remove winners" between rounds.
5. **Save the result** by copying it into the event group or taking a screenshot.

If entries are tickets with numbers rather than names, the [Random Number Generator](/tools/random-number-generator/) may be simpler: draw numbers in the ticket range without repeats.

## Preparing your list

A clean list makes the draw quicker and avoids disputes:

- **One entry per line.** Blank lines are ignored, and spaces at the start or end of a line are trimmed.
- **Use full names** where two people share a first name, such as "Riya S" and "Riya M".
- **Copy straight from a spreadsheet.** Paste a single column from Excel or Google Sheets and each cell becomes one line.
- **Add weight deliberately.** If someone bought three tickets, enter their name three times with a ticket number, such as "Anil – 1", "Anil – 2", "Anil – 3". Each line is then a separate, valid entry.
- **Items work too.** The picker does not care whether entries are people. Use it for topics, chores, restaurant options or quiz questions.

## Picking a random order

Sometimes you need a sequence rather than a winner: presentation order in a class, batting order in gully cricket, or the order of interviews for the day. Press **Shuffle list** and the whole list is rearranged at random in the entry box. Copy it, or pick winners equal to the total number of entries to get a numbered order you can share.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving duplicates in by accident,** which gives some people extra chances. Keep Ignore duplicates on unless entries are deliberate.
- **Spelling a name two ways,** such as "Mohd. Arif" and "Mohammed Arif". Ignore duplicates only catches identical text, so tidy the list first.
- **Forgetting to remove winners** between rounds when each person should win once.
- **Changing the rules after the draw.** Agree them before you press the button.
- **Making teams by headcount only** when skill levels vary widely. Random teams are fair in chance, not necessarily in strength; you can re-run or balance a few players by agreement.
