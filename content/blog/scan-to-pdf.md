To scan a document to PDF with your phone, open [Scan to PDF](/tools/scan-to-pdf/), tap **Scan with camera**, capture each page, check the automatically detected corners, choose a filter such as **Enhanced** or **B&W**, and download one PDF. No app to install, and the pages never leave your phone.

You can also upload photos you have already taken. The scanner finds the edges of the paper, straightens the perspective so the page looks flat, and cleans up the background.

## Why a scanner beats a plain photo

A normal phone photo of a document has problems: the page is skewed, the table shows around the edges, shadows darken one side, and the paper looks grey or yellow. A scanner fixes all of these:

- **Edge detection** finds the four corners of the paper.
- **Perspective correction** turns a tilted shot into a flat, rectangular page.
- **Filters** whiten the background and sharpen text.
- **Page sizing** snaps pages that are nearly A4 to an exact A4 shape.

The result looks like it came from an office scanner and is far easier to read, print and upload.

## How to scan a document step by step

1. Open [Scan to PDF](/tools/scan-to-pdf/) on your phone (or a laptop with a webcam).
2. Tap **Scan with camera** and allow camera access. Or choose **Upload photos** to use existing images.
3. Hold the phone above the page with all four corners visible, in good even light. If your phone supports it, a flash button appears for dark rooms, and a flip button lets you switch cameras.
4. Capture each page. Captured pages appear in a grid.
5. Select a page to check its detected corners. Drag the handles (with the magnifier to help) if any corner is off.
6. Choose a **Filter**: Original, Enhanced, Gray or B&W. Apply it to one page or all pages.
7. Drag pages to reorder them, and rotate any that are sideways.
8. Choose the **PDF page size** (A4, Letter or Fit image), **JPEG quality** and a **File name**.
9. Download as a PDF, or as JPG images in a ZIP.

## Choosing a filter

| Filter | Effect | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Original | No colour change, just straightened | Photos, colourful certificates, ID cards where colour matters |
| Enhanced | Brighter background, sharper text, colour kept | Most everyday documents, receipts, letters with coloured stamps |
| Gray | Removes colour, keeps tones | Forms and printed pages where colour is irrelevant |
| B&W | High-contrast black and white | Typed text, notes, pages to be printed or OCR'd |

Enhanced is the default because it suits most documents. If a portal or office specifically asks for black-and-white copies, use B&W or Gray.

## Scanning ID documents and certificates

Indians scan Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID, driving licences, mark sheets and caste or income certificates for countless applications. A few pointers:

- **Scan both sides** of cards as separate pages, front first.
- **Use Original or Enhanced** so photos and holograms remain recognisable.
- **Check portal requirements.** Each portal sets its own format and size limit, and some want a JPG rather than a PDF. Read the instructions next to the upload field.
- **Reduce size if needed.** Lower the JPEG quality slider before downloading, or compress afterwards with [Compress PDF](/tools/compress-pdf/).
- **Consider DigiLocker first.** For documents issued through DigiLocker, the official digital copy may be accepted and is cleaner than any scan.

Because processing happens on your device, the images of your ID never pass through a third-party server.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Poor light.** Shadows and dim rooms cause blur and grey pages. Daylight near a window, without direct sun on the page, works best.
- **Low contrast background.** White paper on a white bedsheet is hard to detect. Use a dark table or floor.
- **Cutting off corners.** If a corner is outside the frame, detection fails and part of the page is lost.
- **Using B&W for photos.** Faces and coloured seals turn harsh. Use Original or Enhanced for these pages.
- **Very high quality for portal uploads.** A 95% JPEG quality multi-page PDF can exceed small upload limits. Try 70–85%.

## Page shape and size options

The **Shape** setting controls how the straightened page is proportioned. **Auto** snaps pages that are close to A4 to an exact A4 shape, which tidies up small measurement errors; **A4 ratio** always uses A4 proportions; **As measured** keeps the exact shape detected, which is better for receipts, ID cards and other non-A4 items.

The **PDF page size** then decides the paper size inside the PDF: A4 for Indian offices and portals, Letter for US recipients, or Fit image to make each PDF page exactly the size of the scan. For a mix of an A4 form and a small ID card, A4 with As measured keeps the card's natural proportions, scaled to fit and centred on an A4 page. If you need the card printed at its real size, print the JPG at actual size instead.

## Scan to PDF or Images to PDF?

If your photos are already clean and straight (for example screenshots or images from a flatbed scanner), [Images to PDF](/tools/jpg-to-pdf/) is quicker and keeps the original image quality. If you are photographing paper on a table, the scanner's edge detection and filters make a much bigger difference.

## After scanning: what next?

Scanning is often the first step in a workflow:

- Need the text? Run the scan through [Image to Text](/tools/image-to-text/), or convert it with [PDF to Word](/tools/pdf-to-word/), which can OCR scanned pages in English, Hindi and Bengali.
- Need to sign it? Open the PDF in Sign PDF.
- Need to combine it with other documents? Use Merge PDF.
