Choose a handful of hashtags that describe exactly what your post is about, and match the number to the platform: about 3 for YouTube, 3–5 for Instagram and 1–3 for Facebook. More is rarely better; relevant is.

The [Hashtag Generator](/tools/hashtag-generator/) on BISCS India builds that short list for you. It turns your topic into groups of specific, niche, broad and local hashtags, selects a starter set for the platform you choose, and lets you swap tags in and out with a tap.

## Hashtag rules on each platform

Every platform treats hashtags a little differently. The generator shows the relevant rule above your selection when you switch tabs.

**YouTube.** Hashtags cannot contain spaces. If your title has none, YouTube can show the first three hashtags from the description above the title. If a video carries more than 60 hashtags, YouTube ignores all of them. Three to five good ones are plenty.

**Instagram.** You can add up to 30 hashtags to a post, but Instagram's current advice leans towards a few highly relevant ones, around 3–5, rather than the full allowance.

**Facebook.** Hashtags work, but posts with many of them tend to look spammy. One to three is typical.

Across all three, a hashtag is a link to a feed of other posts using it. If it does not describe your content, a viewer who taps it has no reason to come back.

## Understanding the five groups

Suggestions are grouped so you can build a balanced mix:

| Group | What it contains | Example for "Kolkata street food" |
|---|---|---|
| From your topic | Your own words combined, plus "tips" and "india" versions | #KolkataStreetFood, #StreetFood |
| Niche | Specific tags for the detected category | #IndianStreetFood, #FoodVlog |
| Broad | Large, very competitive tags | #Food, #Foodie |
| Local | India and, if ticked, Kolkata / West Bengal tags | #India, #IncredibleIndia |
| Platform | Platform-specific tags like #Shorts or #Reels | #YouTubeShorts |

The automatic pick leans on the first two groups and adds at most one broad tag. That reflects a simple idea: a specific hashtag puts you among fewer, more relevant posts, while a giant tag like #Food is crowded within seconds. Platform tags such as #Shorts or #Reels are listed only so you can add them when they genuinely fit, for example on a vertical short video.

## A practical mix for a YouTube Short

Suppose you post a 40-second clip of making phuchka at a Kolkata stall, and you type "phuchka Kolkata street food" as the topic so the dish name appears in your own group. A balanced YouTube set could be `#KolkataStreetFood #Phuchka #Shorts`. The first describes the subject and place, the second is the dish itself, and the third tells viewers the format. Add those to the description, keep the title clean, and you are done.

For the same clip on Instagram, you might use five: the two above, #IndianStreetFood, #KolkataFood and #Reels.

## Building a reusable hashtag set

If you post regularly on one theme, you will notice the same handful of tags working for most posts. It helps to keep a short personal bank:

- **Two or three signature tags** that describe your channel or series, used consistently so viewers can find all related posts.
- **A rotating set of topic tags** chosen fresh for each post from the generator's "From your topic" and "Niche" groups.
- **One location tag** when place matters, such as a city for food, travel or local business posts.

Generate once per post, copy the selection into a notes file alongside the caption, and over a few weeks you will see which combinations suit your audience. Your platform's own analytics, rather than any hashtag tool, are the place to judge what actually brought views.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Using the maximum because you can.** Thirty loosely related tags dilute the message and look automated.
- **Tagging trends that have nothing to do with the post.** Platforms treat misleading hashtags as spam, and viewers notice.
- **Putting many hashtags in a YouTube title.** It crowds the most valuable text; the description is usually the better place.
- **Forgetting spaces break hashtags.** "#street food" becomes #street. The generator joins words for you.
- **Using banned or unrelated sensitive tags.** Some hashtags are restricted on certain platforms. If a tag returns few or no recent posts when you tap it, choose another.
- **Copying the same block onto every post.** Repeating identical tags regardless of content tells viewers nothing.
