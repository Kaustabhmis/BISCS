For FY 2025-26 (assessment year 2026-27), most salaried people in India pay less tax under the new regime unless they claim large deductions. The new regime has wider slabs, a ₹75,000 standard deduction and a Section 87A rebate that makes tax nil when taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh. In practice, a salary of up to ₹12.75 lakh with no other income pays no tax.

The old regime has higher rates but allows deductions such as HRA, 80C, 80D and home loan interest. Which one is better depends on your numbers. The free [Income Tax Calculator](/tools/income-tax-calculator/) works out both and tells you which saves more.

## What are the tax slabs for FY 2025-26?

| Taxable income | New regime rate | Old regime rate (below 60) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to ₹2.5 lakh | Nil | Nil |
| ₹2.5 lakh – ₹4 lakh | Nil | 5% |
| ₹4 lakh – ₹5 lakh | 5% | 5% |
| ₹5 lakh – ₹8 lakh | 5% | 20% |
| ₹8 lakh – ₹10 lakh | 10% | 20% |
| ₹10 lakh – ₹12 lakh | 10% | 30% |
| ₹12 lakh – ₹16 lakh | 15% | 30% |
| ₹16 lakh – ₹20 lakh | 20% | 30% |
| ₹20 lakh – ₹24 lakh | 25% | 30% |
| Above ₹24 lakh | 30% | 30% |

Under the old regime, the nil band is ₹3 lakh for those aged 60 to 79 and ₹5 lakh for those 80 and above. The new regime slabs are the same for all ages.

Other key figures used by the calculator:

- **Standard deduction:** ₹75,000 (new) or ₹50,000 (old) for salaried people and pensioners.
- **Section 87A rebate:** up to ₹60,000 if new-regime taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less; up to ₹12,500 if old-regime taxable income is ₹5 lakh or less.
- **Cess:** 4% on tax plus surcharge.
- **Surcharge:** 10% above ₹50 lakh, 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore, and 37% above ₹5 crore in the old regime (capped at 25% in the new regime).

## Worked examples

**1. Salary ₹12,75,000, no other income.** New regime: 12,75,000 − 75,000 = ₹12,00,000 taxable. Slab tax is ₹20,000 (4–8 lakh at 5%) + ₹40,000 (8–12 lakh at 10%) = ₹60,000. The 87A rebate cancels it: **tax nil**. Old regime with ₹1.5 lakh 80C and ₹25,000 80D: taxable ₹10,50,000, tax ₹1,27,500 + ₹5,100 cess = **₹1,32,600**.

**2. Salary ₹13,00,000.** New regime taxable income is ₹12,25,000. Slab tax is ₹63,750, but marginal relief caps it at the ₹25,000 earned above ₹12 lakh. With cess, **tax is ₹26,000**, not ₹66,300.

**3. Salary ₹18,00,000 with heavy deductions.** Suppose HRA exemption ₹2.4 lakh, home loan interest ₹2 lakh, 80C ₹1.5 lakh, 80D ₹50,000 and NPS ₹50,000.

- New regime: taxable ₹17,25,000, tax ₹1,45,000 + cess = **₹1,50,800**
- Old regime: taxable ₹10,60,000, tax ₹1,30,500 + cess = **₹1,35,720**

Here the old regime saves about ₹15,080. Drop the HRA, home loan and NPS, and the old regime tax rises to ₹2,96,400, so the new regime wins by a wide margin.

**4. A retired person aged 65.** Suppose a pension of ₹9,00,000 plus FD interest of ₹1,50,000, with health insurance of ₹50,000 under 80D and ₹50,000 of interest deduction under 80TTB entered as "other deductions".

- New regime: 10,50,000 − 75,000 = ₹9,75,000 taxable; slab tax ₹37,500 is fully covered by the 87A rebate, so **tax is nil**.
- Old regime (60 to 79 band, ₹3 lakh nil slab): taxable ₹9,00,000, tax ₹90,000 + ₹3,600 cess = **₹93,600**.

Even with the higher senior exemption, the new regime is far cheaper here, because the rebate covers taxable income up to ₹12 lakh.

## How do you decide between the regimes?

- Add up deductions you will **actually** claim with proof, not ones you might make.
- Your HRA exemption depends on rent, salary and city; work it out first in the [HRA Exemption Calculator](/tools/hra-calculator/).
- Compulsory contributions such as employee PF already count towards 80C, while voluntary ones like [PPF](/tools/ppf-calculator/) top it up to the ₹1.5 lakh limit.
- Re-run the comparison each year, because your salary and deductions change.

## Common mistakes

1. **Assuming ₹12 lakh salary is the limit.** The ₹12 lakh figure is taxable income; salaried people get the ₹75,000 deduction first.
2. **Forgetting other income.** FD and savings interest are taxable and can push you over ₹12 lakh, triggering slab tax.
3. **Claiming old regime deductions under the new regime.** Most, including 80C, 80D and HRA, are not available.
4. **Ignoring cess.** Every rupee of tax carries 4% cess.
5. **Treating capital gains like salary.** Special-rate income is outside this calculator and has its own rules.

## What the calculator does not cover

The tool is built for resident individuals for FY 2025-26. It does not handle capital gains or other special-rate income, business income computations, employer NPS contributions, losses carried forward or marginal relief on surcharge. It also uses familiar section numbers from the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect from 1 April 2026 for later years, with new section numbering, so rules for FY 2026-27 onwards should be checked separately.
