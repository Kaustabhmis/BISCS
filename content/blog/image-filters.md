Photo filters are adjustments that change the look of a picture: brighter or darker, more or less colourful, warmer or cooler, or converted to black and white. They are the quickest way to rescue a dull shot, give a set of photos a consistent mood, or create a vintage feel without installing an editing app.

The [Photo Filters](/tools/image-filters/) tool from BISCS India gives you nine one-tap presets and nine manual sliders. Everything is applied in your browser, and the final file is saved at full resolution.

## What can you adjust?

The sliders cover the edits most people actually use:

- **Brightness** lifts or darkens the whole picture.
- **Contrast** stretches the difference between light and dark areas. More contrast adds punch; less gives a soft, faded look.
- **Saturation** controls how intense colours are. Zero gives black and white.
- **Hue** shifts every colour around the colour wheel. Useful in small amounts to correct a tint, or in large amounts for creative effects.
- **Blur** softens the whole image, handy for backgrounds behind text.
- **Grayscale**, **Sepia** and **Invert** convert the picture partially or fully.
- **Vignette** darkens the edges to frame the subject.

## Tips for natural-looking edits

### Fix light before colour

Get brightness and contrast right first. Colour adjustments look different on a dark photo than on a well-exposed one, so tuning saturation before brightness often means doing it twice.

### Go easy on saturation

Skin tones are the first thing to suffer. If faces start to look orange or red, pull saturation back. For portraits, warmth from the Warm preset often flatters more than raw saturation.

### Use hue for colour casts, gently

Photos under tube lights can turn greenish, and some indoor bulbs make everything yellow. A hue shift of just a few degrees can neutralise that. Large hue shifts make skin look unnatural.

### Keep a consistent look across a set

If you are editing photos for a shop catalogue, a wedding album or an Instagram grid, note the slider values that worked on the first picture and apply the same ones to the rest. Consistency looks more professional than a different filter on every image.

## Editing for social media and online shops

On Instagram or Facebook, a slightly brighter, slightly more vivid image tends to look better in a busy feed, because phone screens show colour well and posts are viewed small. The Vivid preset with brightness pulled back a little is a reliable starting point for food, clothing and travel photos.

For an online shop on a marketplace or your own website, accuracy matters more than drama. A customer who orders a maroon kurta and receives something that looks brown will be unhappy. Keep saturation close to normal, correct only obvious colour casts, and compare the edited photo with the real product in daylight. Use the same settings for every product photo in a collection so the colours look consistent next to each other.

## When filters are not the right tool

Filters change the overall look of a picture. They are not designed for:

- **Old, faded or noisy photos** that need dust removal, colour-cast correction and sharpening. The [Photo Enhancer](/tools/ai-photo-enhancer/) is built for that.
- **Blurring only the background** behind a person. Use the [AI Background Blur & Changer](/tools/ai-background-changer/), which separates the subject first.
- **Artistic effects** such as sketches, cartoons or pop-art. Try [Photo Art Effects](/tools/photo-art-effects/).

## Common mistakes

- **Maximum contrast on a photo with a bright sky.** The clouds turn pure white and lose all detail.
- **Heavy vignette on group photos.** People at the edges end up in the dark.
- **Editing on a very dim screen.** Your photo will look too bright on everyone else’s phone. Check it at normal brightness.
- **Using filters on documents for official upload.** Sepia, vintage or blur effects on an ID scan or certificate can lead to rejection. For documents, only use brightness and contrast to improve readability, or try B&W.
- **Applying a filter, saving, and filtering again.** Each JPG save loses a little quality. Make all your adjustments in one session.
