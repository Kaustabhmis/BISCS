Compound interest is interest earned on both your original money and the interest already added to it. The standard formula is **A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)^(n × t)**, where P is the principal, r the annual rate as a decimal, n the number of times interest is added each year and t the number of years.

₹1,00,000 at 8% for 10 years becomes about ₹2,15,892 if interest is added once a year, and about ₹2,20,804 if it is added every quarter. The free [Compound Interest](/tools/compound-interest-calculator/) calculator on BISCS India works this out for any frequency and can include a fixed monthly top-up.

## Why does compounding grow money faster?

With simple interest, ₹1 lakh at 8% earns ₹8,000 every year, no more. With yearly compounding, year one earns ₹8,000, year two earns 8% of ₹1,08,000, which is ₹8,640, and year three earns 8% of ₹1,16,640. Each year's interest is a little larger than the last.

Over short periods the difference is small. Over decades it becomes the main source of growth. That is why the same 8% looks modest over three years and powerful over thirty.

## How compounding frequency changes the result

Here is ₹1,00,000 at 8% for 10 years, calculated with the tool:

| Compounding | Future value | Interest earned | Effective annual rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yearly | ₹2,15,892 | ₹1,15,892 | 8.00% |
| Half-yearly | ₹2,19,112 | ₹1,19,112 | 8.16% |
| Quarterly | ₹2,20,804 | ₹1,20,804 | 8.24% |
| Monthly | ₹2,21,964 | ₹1,21,964 | 8.30% |
| Daily | ₹2,22,535 | ₹1,22,535 | 8.33% |

More frequent compounding always helps, but with diminishing returns. Quarterly is the common convention for bank fixed deposits in India, which is why it is the default in the calculator.

## Time matters more than frequency

Compare the frequency table above with what happens when you simply leave the money longer. ₹1,00,000 at 8% compounded yearly grows to:

| Years | Value | Growth multiple |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | ₹2,15,892 | 2.16× |
| 20 | ₹4,66,096 | 4.66× |
| 30 | ₹10,06,266 | 10.06× |

Switching from yearly to daily compounding over 10 years added under ₹7,000. Staying invested for 30 years instead of 10 adds nearly ₹8 lakh. The last ten years alone add more than the first twenty combined, because each year's growth is calculated on a much bigger base. If you are deciding between chasing a slightly better compounding frequency and starting a year earlier, start earlier.

## When compounding works against you

The same maths applies to debt. As a purely hypothetical illustration, if an unpaid balance of ₹50,000 grew at 3% a month with nothing repaid, after 12 months it would be about ₹71,288, which is over 42% more in a single year. Real card and loan charges depend on your agreement, fees and repayments, but the lesson is clear: high-interest debt that compounds monthly should usually be cleared before you invest for modest returns.

## Where you meet compound interest in real life

- **Fixed deposits:** banks commonly compound quarterly. The [FD Calculator](/tools/fd-calculator/) adds tenure in years, months and days.
- **Recurring deposits and PPF:** both grow by compounding, with their own rules.
- **Loans and credit cards:** compounding works against you. Unpaid credit card balances can grow very quickly because interest is charged on interest.
- **Inflation:** prices compound too, which is why ₹1 lakh today buys less each year.

## Common mistakes with compound interest

1. **Mixing up nominal and effective rates.** A product quoting 8% compounded monthly actually yields about 8.30% a year.
2. **Using compound formulas where simple interest applies.** Some loans and short deposits use simple interest. Check with the [Simple Interest](/tools/simple-interest-calculator/) calculator when that is the case.
3. **Forgetting tax.** Interest on deposits is taxable each year, which reduces the amount that actually compounds.
4. **Overestimating with high rates.** Small changes in rate make big differences over long periods, so test a lower rate too.
5. **Treating contributions as start-of-month.** This tool adds them at month end; a start-of-month assumption gives a slightly higher number.

## Limits of the calculation

The calculator assumes a constant rate, regular contributions and no withdrawals or taxes. Real products may change rates, apply TDS or round interest at each step. Use the result as a clear illustration of how compounding works and a reasonable estimate for planning.
