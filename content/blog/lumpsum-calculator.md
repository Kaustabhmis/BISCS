A lumpsum calculator tells you what a single, one-time investment could be worth after a number of years at an assumed annual return. The formula is **future value = amount × (1 + r)^n**, where r is the yearly return as a decimal and n is the number of years. ₹1 lakh growing at 12% a year for 10 years becomes about ₹3.11 lakh.

That headline number ignores rising prices, so the free [Lumpsum Calculator](/tools/lumpsum-calculator/) also shows the result in today's rupees. At 6% inflation, the same ₹3.11 lakh would buy roughly what ₹1.73 lakh buys now.

## How the lumpsum formula works

Compounding means each year's growth is added to the base before the next year's growth is calculated. With ₹1,00,000 at 12%:

- After year 1: ₹1,12,000
- After year 2: ₹1,25,440
- After year 3: ₹1,40,493
- After year 10: ₹3,10,585

The money grows about 3.1 times in ten years. Notice that the gain in year one is ₹12,000, but by year ten the yearly gain is over ₹33,000, because the base is so much larger. This acceleration is why time in the market matters more than almost anything else in long-term investing.

Suppose Meera in Kolkata invests a ₹5 lakh bonus and leaves it for 15 years, assuming 10% a year.

| Estimated value after 15 years | ₹20,88,624 |

The nominal number looks impressive, but in purchasing-power terms Meera's money has grown about 1.7 times. That is still meaningful growth, and it is a more honest basis for planning a goal like a home down payment.

## Lumpsum or SIP: which grows more?

At the same steady return, a lumpsum invested at the start always ends higher than a SIP of the same total, because every rupee compounds from day one. For example, ₹1.2 lakh invested at once at 12% for one year becomes ₹1,34,400, while ₹10,000 a month for twelve months becomes about ₹1,28,093 in the [SIP Calculator](/tools/sip-calculator/).

Real markets are not steady, though. A lumpsum invested just before a fall can take years to recover, while a SIP buys at many prices. That is why many investors with a large sum use a systematic transfer from a liquid or debt fund into equity over several months. The calculators show the arithmetic; the choice depends on your risk tolerance and timeline.

## How long does it take to double your money?

A handy shortcut is the Rule of 72: divide 72 by the yearly return to estimate the years needed to double. Here is how the shortcut compares with the exact answer from compound growth:

| Annual return | Rule of 72 estimate | Exact years to double |
|---|---|---|
| 6% | 12.0 | 11.90 |
| 8% | 9.0 | 9.01 |
| 10% | 7.2 | 7.27 |
| 12% | 6.0 | 6.12 |
| 15% | 4.8 | 4.96 |

The rule is close enough for quick mental maths in the range most people care about. It also shows why return assumptions matter so much: at 6%, ₹1 lakh doubles roughly once in 12 years; at 12%, it doubles about twice in the same time and becomes nearly ₹3.9 lakh. To check any specific case, type the amount, rate and years into the calculator and watch the growth multiple in the result.

## When to use a lumpsum calculator

- **Investing a windfall** such as a bonus, gift, maturity proceeds or property sale.
- **Checking an old investment:** enter the original amount and the years held to see what a given return would have produced, or use the [CAGR Calculator](/tools/cagr-calculator/) to find the actual annual return from start and end values.
- **Setting goal amounts:** pair it with the [Inflation Calculator](/tools/inflation-calculator/) to see what your goal will cost in future.
- **Comparing products:** try different returns for equity, debt or deposits to see how much the rate gap matters over 10 or 20 years.

## Checklist before you trust the number

- Is the return assumption reasonable for this type of investment, not just its best recent year?
- Have you looked at the "today's money" figure, not only the nominal value?
- Have you allowed for taxes on gains when you redeem, and any exit load?
- Is your time horizon long enough for the asset's ups and downs?
- If the money is for a near-term need, would a safer option such as a fixed deposit be more suitable? Compare it with the [FD Calculator](/tools/fd-calculator/).

## Limitations to keep in mind

The tool assumes the same return every year, no withdrawals and no extra contributions. Market-linked investments rarely behave like that, and the sequence of returns matters if you need the money on a fixed date. The inflation adjustment uses one steady rate, whereas real inflation varies from year to year and between categories such as education and food.
