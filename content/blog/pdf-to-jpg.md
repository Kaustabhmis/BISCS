To convert a PDF to JPG, open [PDF to Images](/tools/pdf-to-jpg/), select the pages you want (or leave the selection empty for all pages), choose **JPG** or **PNG**, pick a resolution of 72, 150 or 300 DPI, and convert. One page downloads as an image; several pages arrive together in a ZIP.

The pages are rendered in your browser, so the PDF never leaves your device. That is reassuring when the file is a certificate, an e-PAN or a bank document.

## Why turn a PDF into images?

PDFs are great for documents, but images are easier in many everyday situations:

- **Portals that accept only JPG** for a particular field, such as a single certificate or a caste or income certificate on some application forms.
- **WhatsApp and social media**, where an image previews instantly in the chat and a PDF has to be opened.
- **Presentations**, where you want to drop a page or a chart from a report onto a slide.
- **Thumbnails and previews** for a website, a course or a product catalogue.
- **Editing in an image editor**, for example cropping a single table out of a report page.

## How to convert PDF pages to images step by step

1. Open [PDF to Images](/tools/pdf-to-jpg/) and choose your PDF.
2. Select the pages you need by clicking thumbnails, or type them in the **Pages** box (for example `1, 3-5`). Leave it empty to convert every page.
3. Choose the **Format**: JPG or PNG.
4. For JPG, set the **JPG quality** slider. The default of 85% is a good balance.
5. Choose the **Resolution**: 72, 150 or 300 DPI.
6. Convert. A progress bar shows each page being rendered.
7. Download the image, or the ZIP if you converted several pages.

## Choosing format and resolution

| Goal | Format | Resolution | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upload a certificate under a size limit | JPG | 150 DPI | Readable text, modest file size |
| Share on WhatsApp | JPG | 150 DPI | Quick to send, clear on phones |
| Print a page as an image | JPG or PNG | 300 DPI | Enough detail for paper |
| Put a chart or table on a slide | PNG | 150 or 300 DPI | Crisp lines and text, no JPEG artefacts |
| Quick preview or thumbnail | JPG | 72 DPI | Smallest file |

DPI stands for dots per inch. A standard A4 page is about 8.3 inches wide, so at 150 DPI the image is roughly 1,240 pixels wide, and at 300 DPI roughly 2,480 pixels. Doubling the DPI roughly quadruples the number of pixels, which is why 300 DPI files are so much bigger.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Picking 300 DPI for everything.** For screen viewing and most portals, 150 DPI is plenty, and the files are about a quarter of the size.
- **Using PNG for scanned photos.** PNG is lossless, so a scanned page saved as PNG can be very large. Use JPG for scans.
- **Setting JPG quality too low.** Below about 60%, text edges start to look smudgy. If the file is too big, lower the DPI before lowering quality drastically.
- **Forgetting to unzip.** On phones, the ZIP lands in Downloads. Open your Files app and extract it before attaching images elsewhere.
- **Converting when a PDF would do.** If the portal accepts PDFs, upload the PDF itself; it keeps text sharp at any zoom.

## Meeting image size limits on portals

When a portal asks for a JPG under a certain size, the limit and the required dimensions are set by that portal, and they vary. Read the instructions next to the upload button. A workable approach:

1. Convert the page at 150 DPI as JPG with quality around 80%.
2. Check the file size.
3. If it is still too large, use the [Image Compressor](/tools/image-compressor/) to reduce it further, or the [Image Resizer](/tools/image-resizer/) if the portal specifies pixel dimensions.
4. Open the final image and confirm that every line of text is legible.

## What happens to links, forms and selectable text?

An image is a flat picture of the page. Once a page is converted, its text can no longer be selected, copied or searched, clickable links stop working, and form fields become part of the picture. That is fine for sharing and previews, but keep the original PDF as your master copy. If you later need to change something, edit the PDF and convert again rather than trying to edit the image.

If you need the words from an image later, an OCR tool such as [Image to Text](/tools/image-to-text/) can recognise them again, but it is always more accurate to work from the original PDF.

## Single page vs many pages

When you convert one page, the image downloads directly with a name based on your PDF and the page number. When you convert several, they are packed into one ZIP named after your PDF, so your Downloads folder does not fill up with dozens of separate files. On a computer, double-click the ZIP to open it; on Android, open it in the Files app and tap Extract; on an iPhone, tap the ZIP in the Files app and it unpacks into a folder beside it.

## Going the other way

Need to turn images back into a PDF? Use [Images to PDF](/tools/jpg-to-pdf/), which places JPG and PNG files on A4 or Letter pages in the order you choose. And if what you actually want is the text from a PDF rather than a picture of it, PDF to Text extracts selectable text directly.

## Privacy and performance

Rendering pages happens inside your browser using a PDF engine that loads with the page. Your file is read from your device and the images are created on your device; nothing is uploaded. Rendering many pages at 300 DPI is demanding, so a long document takes longer on a phone than on a laptop. If your phone struggles, convert fewer pages at a time or choose a lower resolution.
