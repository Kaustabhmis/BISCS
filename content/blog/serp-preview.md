A SERP preview (search engine results page preview) shows you what your listing will roughly look like in Google before the page goes live. It answers a simple but important question: will my title and description fit, and will they make someone want to click?

The [Google SERP Preview](/tools/serp-preview/) on BISCS India draws a desktop result and a mobile result at the same time, updates them as you type, and flags titles or descriptions that are likely to be cut off with an ellipsis.

## Why preview your search snippet at all?

Your search result is a tiny advert competing with nine others. Ranking on page one is only half the job; the other half is earning the click. A title that ends in "…" just before the important word, or a description that trails off mid-sentence, wastes that chance.

Writing tags in a CMS field gives you no sense of how they will look. The preview puts them in context so you can make small edits, such as moving the keyword forward or trimming a filler phrase, that improve the result without changing the page itself.

## How does Google decide where to cut a title?

Google truncates titles and descriptions by **pixel width**, not by character count. On desktop the title area is roughly 600 px wide; descriptions get a wider area of around 920 px. On mobile the title can wrap to two lines, but the description has less room.

That is why "60 characters" is only a rule of thumb. Compare these two titles of similar length:

- "illustrated literary list of lit titles" is full of narrow letters.
- "WWW MEMBERSHIP MAXIMUM MOMENTUM" is full of wide ones.

The second takes far more space. The BISCS tool measures each title using the Arial font Google uses for results and shows the estimated width beside the character count, with a colour bar that turns amber near the limit and red when the title will be cut.

## How to use the Google SERP Preview tool

1. Open the [Google SERP Preview](/tools/serp-preview/) and press **Sample** if you want to see a worked example first.
2. Type or paste your **Title**. Watch the counter show characters and approximate pixels.
3. Enter the page **URL**. The preview turns it into the breadcrumb-style path Google shows (for example, example.com › blog › seo-tips).
4. Paste your **Meta description**.
5. Add a **Focus keyword**. The preview bolds it in the description, the way Google often bolds matching words.
6. Optionally set a **Site name**, a custom **Breadcrumb** and a **Date** to see how a dated article result looks.
7. Compare the **Desktop preview** and **Mobile preview**, read the messages below the form, and edit until both look clean.

All of this happens in your browser. Your draft titles are not sent anywhere, which is useful when you are preparing pages for a launch that has not been announced.

## A checklist for a snippet that earns clicks

Use this list while you edit in the preview:

- **The topic comes first.** Put the words people search for at the start of the title, where they are least likely to be cut.
- **The title matches the page.** If you promise "Price List 2026" the page must have a current price list. Mismatches lead to quick back-clicks and Google rewriting your title.
- **The description answers "why this page?"** Mention what is inside, such as a calculator, a downloadable format or a comparison table.
- **Specific beats vague.** "Compare FD rates of 12 banks and calculate maturity in seconds" is stronger than "Best information about fixed deposits".
- **One brand mention is enough.** Add your brand name at the end of the title if space allows; do not repeat it in the description.
- **Readable on a phone.** Most searches in India happen on mobile, so check the mobile preview last and make sure the opening words still make sense.

## Common mistakes the preview helps you catch

**Titles that are too short.** A three-word title like "Home | Sharma Traders" wastes valuable space. Titles of 50–60 characters usually make the best use of it, and the tool nudges you when a title is under 30 characters.

**Descriptions that are too short.** If your description is very brief, Google is more likely to replace it with text pulled from the page, which may not be the part you would choose.

**Keyword missing from the title or description.** When you enter a focus keyword, the tool tells you if it is absent from either. That is not a ranking rule, but if the snippet does not mention what the person searched for, fewer people click.

**Long dated snippets.** When Google shows a date in front of a description, it uses up part of the space. Set the date field to see whether your description still fits.

**Broken URLs.** A typo in the URL field produces an error, which is a handy reminder to check the slug itself. Short, readable slugs such as `/gst-calculator/` look more trustworthy than `/index.php?id=4471`. The [Slug Generator](/tools/slug-generator/) can help you make clean ones.

## Preview vs live result: what to expect

It helps to be clear about what any preview can and cannot do:

| | SERP preview | Live Google result |
|---|---|---|
| Title source | The title you typed | Usually your title tag, sometimes rewritten |
| Description | Your meta description | Your description or a passage chosen from the page |
| Truncation | Estimated from pixel widths | Actual rendering on the user's device |
| Extras | Optional date and breadcrumb | May add sitelinks, ratings, images or other features |

Google has publicly said it may rewrite titles that are too long, stuffed with keywords or do not describe the page. The best protection is simply a clear, honest title that fits.
