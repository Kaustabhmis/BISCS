To find the HSN code for a product, search by what the product is (for example, "t-shirt" or "laptop"), identify the right chapter and 4-digit heading, and then take the full 6- or 8-digit code from the official tariff. For services, do the same with SAC codes, which all start with 99. Always confirm the code and its GST rate on the CBIC website before you use it on invoices.

## What do HSN and SAC codes look like?

HSN codes get more specific as digits are added:

| Digits | Level | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Chapter | 61: Articles of apparel and clothing accessories, knitted or crocheted |
| 4 | Heading | 6109: T-shirts, singlets and other vests, knitted |
| 6 | Sub-heading (international HS) | Specific type within 6109 |
| 8 | Tariff item (India) | Used in customs and for exports or imports |

SAC codes for services are usually 6 digits, starting with 99. For example, 9982 is the group for legal and accounting services, and 998211 covers legal advisory and representation in criminal law.

## How many digits do you need on a GST invoice?

The rule under current CBIC notifications depends on your aggregate turnover in the previous financial year:

- **Above ₹5 crore:** 6-digit HSN or SAC on all tax invoices.
- **Up to ₹5 crore:** 4-digit HSN or SAC on B2B tax invoices; optional on B2C invoices.
- **Exporters and importers** generally use the full 8-digit ITC-HS code in shipping documents.

HSN-wise summaries are also reported in GSTR-1, so using consistent codes on every invoice makes return filing easier.

## Goods or services? Tricky cases

Choosing between an HSN and a SAC code is usually obvious, but a few situations catch people out:

- **Restaurants and takeaways** supply a service, so they use SAC 996331, even though the customer receives food.
- **Repairs and maintenance** are services. A shop that repairs laptops uses a SAC code, such as 998713, for the labour, and an HSN code for any spare parts it sells separately on the invoice.
- **Software** can be goods or a service depending on how it is supplied, for example packaged media versus a subscription or development work.
- **Construction** is generally a service under SAC group 9954, while selling building materials alone is a supply of goods.
- **Job work** on someone else's goods is a service, even though the result is a physical product.

Where an invoice mixes goods and services, each line needs its own code. If one supply naturally bundles several items, GST has rules for composite and mixed supplies that decide which rate applies; this is worth confirming with a practitioner.

## Why the finder does not show GST rates

GST rates were rationalised with effect from 22 September 2025, with most goods and services moving into the 5% and 18% slabs and a 40% rate for certain luxury and sin goods. Rates for specific items continue to change by notification. Rather than risk showing a stale rate, the finder focuses on getting the code right. Once you have it, look up the rate on the CBIC website and use the [GST Calculator](/tools/gst-calculator/) to work out the tax.

## Tips for choosing the right code

- **Describe what the item is, not what it is used for.** Classification is mainly by material and nature.
- **Prefer the more specific heading** when two seem to fit.
- **Check your suppliers' invoices.** They often show the HSN for the same goods, though you should still verify it.
- **Keep a product master.** Record each product's HSN once and reuse it in the [Invoice Generator](/tools/invoice-generator/).

## Common mistakes

1. **Using a SAC code for goods or vice versa.**
2. **Showing only 4 digits when your turnover requires 6.**
3. **Copying a rate along with an old code** from a pre-September 2025 invoice.
4. **Guessing from a similar product** instead of checking the heading's description.
5. **Forgetting to validate the customer's GSTIN** on B2B invoices; the [GSTIN Validator](/tools/gstin-validator/) does this quickly.
