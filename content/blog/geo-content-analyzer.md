Generative engine optimisation, usually shortened to GEO, is the practice of shaping your content so that AI systems can understand it, trust it and cite it when they generate answers. When someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews a question, the answer is assembled from sources. GEO is about making your page one of those sources.

The [GEO Content Analyzer](/tools/geo-content-analyzer/) from BISCS India checks an article against fifteen practical signals drawn from published GEO research and long-standing content good practice, and gives you a score with a prioritised list of fixes. It is a checklist, not a promise: no tool can guarantee citations.

## Why GEO matters now

More searches end with an AI-written summary rather than a list of blue links. For a business, that changes the question from "do we rank?" to "are we the source the answer is built from, and are we named or linked?"

The good news is that GEO is not a separate discipline that replaces SEO. A page that AI systems like to cite is usually one that people like to read: it answers the question quickly, explains clearly, shows evidence and is kept up to date. GEO simply puts more emphasis on making each passage easy to lift and quote accurately.

## What does the research say?

The term was popularised by a 2023 academic paper, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", by researchers from institutions including Princeton University and IIT Delhi. They tested different ways of rewriting web content and measured how visible each version became in AI-generated answers.

In broad terms, the methods that helped most were:

- **Citing sources**: adding references to credible sources.
- **Adding quotations** from relevant, named people or bodies.
- **Adding statistics**: specific, concrete numbers instead of vague claims.
- **Improving fluency and readability.**

Keyword stuffing, by contrast, did not perform well. The study used its own test set and AI setups, so treat it as useful direction rather than a formula; real-world AI systems change frequently.

## How to write content that AI systems quote

**Answer first, explain second.** Start with one or two sentences that answer the main question and name the subject. "GST registration is mandatory for most businesses whose annual turnover crosses the threshold set for their state and type of supply" is quotable. "In this article, we will explore..." is not.

**Make every section self-contained.** AI systems often pull a single passage. If a paragraph begins with "It also..." the quote makes no sense on its own. Name the subject again.

**Back claims with evidence.** Cite the official source by name, for example the CBIC or the Income Tax Department, and link to it. Add real figures with dates. Never invent statistics to look authoritative; inaccurate numbers damage trust far more than missing ones.

**Show who is behind the content.** A byline, a short author bio with relevant credentials, and "reviewed by" for financial, legal or health topics all help. The [Entity & Author Schema Builder](/tools/entity-schema-builder/) can add matching structured data.

**Keep it current.** Show a "last updated" date and refresh rates, fees and rules when they change.

## Common GEO mistakes

- **Long, story-style introductions** before the actual answer.
- **Vague claims** such as "many people" or "huge savings" without figures.
- **Walls of text** with no headings, lists or tables.
- **Undefined jargon**, like using "ITC" without ever writing "input tax credit".
- **Anonymous content** on topics where expertise matters.
- **Blocking AI search crawlers** in robots.txt while hoping to be cited. Check your settings with the [AI Crawler Access Checker](/tools/ai-crawler-checker/).
